# Every Answer Leaves Something Open

*To resolve one question is not to resolve everything around it.*

Imagine asking whether a door is open or closed.

You look. It is open.

The question is answered.

But notice how little else had to be answered with it.

You did not need the exact angle of every wood fibre. You did not need the microscopic position of every molecule in the hinge. You did not need to decide whether the door was open by 71.002 degrees rather than 71.003.

For the question you asked, those differences could remain differences that did not matter.

That sounds ordinary, but it hides a useful structure.

An answer does two things at once.

First, it makes one distinction matter. Open and closed can no longer be treated as equivalent for this encounter. Something has been resolved.

Second, by resolving *that* distinction, it also tells us what did **not** have to be resolved. A huge number of finer differences can remain open because the answer does not depend on them yet.

So resolution is not the same as filling in every blank.

It is making the particular difference that has become consequential.

This is the role of what My GUT Deduction calls a **Cut**.

A Cut is not a knife slicing an already-finished world into pieces. It is the relation that makes a previously equivalent distinction consequential at the grain where the relation is happening.

If the question is whether the door is open, the Cut can distinguish open from closed without also demanding the exact microscopic state of the hinge.

In shorthand:

`seated Cut`
→ `resolved distinction`
+ `unresolved remainder`

The unresolved remainder is not a warehouse full of separately rendered answers waiting backstage. It is narrower than that. It means the distinctions the current relation does not need can remain undercommitted relative to the ground just established.

That matters because every answer becomes new ground.

Once we know the door is open, a new question can become meaningful: Is it open far enough for the table to pass through?

That question introduces another distinction.

If the table fits, perhaps the next question is whether it can turn through the hallway.

Each answer closes something. Each answer also changes which unanswered differences can matter next.

This is why the framework does not picture resolution as reality slowly coloring in one gigantic hidden image until every pixel is fixed.

A relation resolves what must become consequential now. What survives becomes ground that later relations can reuse. Finer distinctions can wait until something actually depends on them.

There is an important limit here.

This does **not** mean that opening possibility is free, or that every imaginable alternative exists independently without cost. The framework's safer claim is simply:

> **Unresolved alternatives do not each require separate fine seating.**

One answer can therefore be exact about the distinction it settles without pretending to be a complete answer to everything.

And that gives the Cut its two faces:

`what became different enough to matter`
→ resolved ground

while

`what still makes no consequential difference here`
→ remains open relative to that ground

So an answer is not merely an ending.

It is also a new beginning condition.

> **An answer does not only close a question. It creates the ground from which the next unanswered differences can exist.**

## Status boundary

The Cut/resolution architecture is framework-native derived ground in the current canonical source: a seated Cut resolves the distinction it makes consequential at that grain while distinctions it does not need can remain equivalent relative to the new ground. The door example is an explanatory analogy, not an empirical claim that physical doors literally instantiate a separately tested Cut mechanism. Do not strengthen the article into a claim that all alternatives are independently real or separately rendered.
