## Central question

When an answer settles one distinction, does reality have to settle every nearby distinction too?

## Central payoff

**An answer does not only close a question. It creates the ground from which the next unanswered differences can exist.**

## Framework status

- Framework-native deduction / consequence / candidate correspondence / external-science-led topic / other: Framework-native derived consequence.
- Notes: The current canonical source states that a seated Cut is resolution at its grain and simultaneously leaves an unresolved remainder: distinctions that need not yet become consequential can remain equivalent relative to the new ground. This Short does not claim that widening is free or that alternatives are independently rendered.

## Duplication check

Closest published Short(s): `You Do Not Have to Render the World to Perceive It` touches selective resolution in perception but asks a different question about perception versus rendering.

Closest prepared-but-unpublished Short(s): `The Future Does Not Start from Zero` concerns accumulated history constraining later possibility; `A Prediction Does Not Have to Be a Number` concerns prospective test structure; `The Frog Doesn't Solve Ballistics` concerns reused ground reducing what action must resolve.

Why this Short is distinct: It owns the single-Cut architecture itself: one seated distinction resolves what matters at that grain while simultaneously defining what still need not be distinguished.
