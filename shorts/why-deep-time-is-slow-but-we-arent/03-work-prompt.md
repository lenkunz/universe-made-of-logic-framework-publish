## The exact job of this Short

Own this question:

> Why can human knowledge and practice change within a lifetime while many biological changes spread through inherited differences over generations?

The public payoff is:

> **When a useful relation is preserved in a reusable form, later people can build on it instead of rebuilding the original work.**

Keep the comparison between genetic inheritance and cultural transmission clear and modest:

- Genetic evolution changes populations through heritable variation and reproduction across generations.
- Cultural information can also move between peers and non-parental adults, not only from parent to child, and can sometimes spread within one generation.
- Genetic and cultural evolution interact. Do not describe them as isolated tracks.
- A tool, written instruction, language, institution, or network can preserve and transmit useful structure, but none guarantees permanence.

The article's narrow framework connection is the already-derived **ratchet**:

costly new relation → relation seats → relation becomes reusable ground → later traversal becomes cheaper → more capacity remains for new relations.

Do not make the article about habit or motor learning. The published Bit **When Practice Makes Thinking Disappear** owns individual motor automaticity. This Short owns cross-person and cross-generation transmission and why the carrier changes the route by which a result can accumulate.

## Article shape

Aim for approximately 450–650 words.

1. Accessible opening: one useful technique can move between people without waiting for a new generation.
2. Mechanism: genetic inheritance and cultural transmission use different routes.
3. Tension: why does preserving a useful result change the cost of the next attempt?
4. Framework connection: introduce the ratchet and reusable ground.
5. Boundary: these are not identical physical mechanisms; no biological/cultural rate is derived.
6. Close on the Short's own payoff, not a framework-wide sales pitch.

Keep the article self-contained for a reader who has never heard of My GUT Deduction.
