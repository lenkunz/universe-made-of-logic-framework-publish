### 1.1 Why Does One Diagonal Make a Square Rigid?

Package: [bits/why-does-one-diagonal-make-a-square-rigid/](../../bits/why-does-one-diagonal-make-a-square-rigid/00-manifest.md)  
Task archive: [2026-11-02 topic-pack run](../bits-topic-pack/2026-11-02_1555-why-does-one-diagonal-make-a-square-rigid.md)

**Established external explanation.** Four fixed-length bars with ideal pin joints can still form a shearing quadrilateral. A diagonal fixes an additional opposite-corner distance, dividing the frame into two triangles and removing the simple internal shear mechanism. The dossier correctly distinguishes geometric rigidity from strength, stiffness, load capacity, and real-member failure.

**Framework connection.** The candidate correspondence is that an added consequential relation can remove a previously permitted motion and change what the whole arrangement can support. It is grounded in:

- [Basics III — One and one still make three](../../source/basics-3-one-and-one-make-three.md#one-and-one-still-make-three)
- [Basics III — Relation is what makes properties sayable](../../source/basics-3-one-and-one-make-three.md#relation-is-what-makes-properties-sayable)
- [Main — Relation creates the points it later connects](../../source/a-universe-made-of-logic.md#ii-relation-creates-the-points-it-later-connects)
- [Main — The cheapest compatible continuation](../../source/a-universe-made-of-logic.md#vi-the-cheapest-compatible-continuation)

The package explicitly says the brace is not literally framework R_AB, that adding any relation does not always increase rigidity, and that truss theorems, Laman counting, stiffness, stress, strain, buckling, and design rules have not been derived.

**Audit result:** correctly scoped candidate structural correspondence; no canonical semantic edit warranted. The framework already says a seated relation changes the consequential possibility space. The Bit illustrates that rule with a specific external mechanism rather than adding a new primitive.

### 1.3 Why Can You Hear a Train on Some Nights but Not Others?

Package: [bits/why-can-you-hear-a-train-on-some-nights-but-not-others/](../../bits/why-can-you-hear-a-train-on-some-nights-but-not-others/00-manifest.md)  
Task archive: [2026-11-08 topic-pack run](../bits-topic-pack/2026-11-08_1514-why-can-you-hear-a-train-on-some-nights-but-not-others.md)

**Established external explanation.** Vertical temperature profiles and wind shear alter the sound-speed profile and refract sound paths. A daytime temperature lapse can bend sound upward away from ground-level receivers; a nighttime inversion can bend it downward. Downwind/upwind profiles can also differ. The dossier separates physical sound reaching a receiver from the receiver's detectability against background noise. It correctly says not every night has a strong inversion and not every distant sound becomes louder.

**Framework connection.** This is an external example for the already-maintained candidate that sound is a material mode of recursive relation. The exact owner is [Main — Sound as a material mode of recursive relation](../../source/a-universe-made-of-logic.md#sound-as-a-material-mode-of-recursive-relation). The maintained target includes the conventional small-amplitude fluid relation v_s² = K_s/ρ, with the appropriate elastic/inertial response for other media and modes. The source explicitly says those physical quantities and their ratio have not been derived from native closures.

The dossier preserves that boundary: conventional acoustics explains the atmospheric effect; the framework has not derived temperature-inversion ray bending, wind shear, absorption, turbulence, sound-speed measurements, or decibel changes. Budget is not pressure, energy, temperature, or sound intensity.

**Audit result:** correct candidate correspondence and external-science boundary. The general sound candidate was already in Main and Status; the atmospheric mechanism is a new illustration, not a new framework derivation. No canonical semantic edit warranted.
