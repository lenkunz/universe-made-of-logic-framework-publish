## A. Topic/content source

# Why Does Ice Float When Most Solids Sink?

*Freezing makes water more ordered and more spacious.*

Drop a metal solid into its melt and the solid usually sinks. Put an ice cube in water and it floats. The molecules are still H2O, so why does making them into a more orderly solid make the material take up *more* room?

Density is mass divided by volume. An object floats when its average density is lower than that of the surrounding liquid. Ordinary ice is less dense than liquid water, so buoyancy can support it.

USGS reports that water expands by about 9% on freezing. It also shows a second oddity: under ordinary conditions, fresh liquid water reaches maximum density near 4 °C rather than at the freezing point.

The molecular reason is hydrogen bonding. Water molecules are polar. In ordinary hexagonal ice, hydrogen bonds organize them into an open, approximately tetrahedral network. That ordered lattice keeps neighboring molecules farther apart on average than they can pack in the liquid. The same mass therefore occupies more volume and has lower density.

Liquid water still has extensive hydrogen bonding, but the network is dynamic. Bonds continually break and reform, and molecules can occupy space that the rigid ice lattice leaves open. Melting can therefore let the molecules pack *closer* even though the liquid is less ordered.

> **More order can mean more empty space.**

Water starts behaving strangely even before it freezes. Cooling most liquids makes them steadily denser. Water does that only down to roughly 4 °C at atmospheric pressure. Below that, the influence of open hydrogen-bonded arrangements increasingly offsets ordinary thermal contraction, so density decreases as the liquid approaches freezing.

This matters in lakes. Water near 4 °C tends to sink beneath colder, less-dense surface water. Ice then forms at the top and floats, where the ice layer also reduces heat exchange with colder air. Real lakes remain more complicated: depth, weather, mixing, salinity, and heat flow all matter.

There is another important caveat. Water has many solid phases under different pressures. The familiar floating-ice story is about ordinary ice Ih near everyday pressures, not every possible form of solid water.

The ordinary scientific payoff is:

> **The molecules did not change identity. Their stable organization changed the density of the whole.**
