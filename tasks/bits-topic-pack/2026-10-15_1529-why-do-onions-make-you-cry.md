# Why Do Onions Make You Cry?

*The onion does not simply contain a bottle of tear gas. Cutting it creates the encounter that makes the irritant.*

An intact onion can sit on a counter without making anyone cry. Slice it and, seconds later, nearby eyes may sting and water.

The easy explanation is that cutting “releases the onion chemical.” That is close, but it hides the best part. Chopping does more than open a container. It damages cellular compartments and lets chemistry meet that had previously been kept apart.

Onions store sulfur-containing flavor precursors in their tissues. Tissue disruption brings those precursors into contact with enzymes. Alliinase acts on cysteine-sulfoxide precursors, producing reactive sulfenic-acid intermediates. Lachrymatory factor synthase (LFS) then catalyzes formation of the volatile lachrymatory factor, commonly described as (Z)- or syn-propanethial S-oxide. The volatile product escapes from the damaged tissue, reaches the moist eye surface, irritates it, and stimulates protective tearing.

> **The onion does not merely release a finished tear chemical that was waiting unchanged inside it. Damage changes which molecules and enzymes can meet, and the new contact launches chemistry that produces the volatile irritant.**

That point became much sharper in 2002, when researchers reported that the onion lachrymatory factor is specifically synthesized by a previously unknown enzyme, LFS, rather than merely forming as a spontaneous by-product after alliinase activity.

Intervention makes the pathway even clearer. LFS-suppressed onion lines produce dramatically less lachrymatory factor after tissue disruption. Changing the pathway changes the tears. Suppressing LFS can also redirect reactive sulfur chemistry toward other thiosulfinate products, so cutting opens a branching reaction network rather than uncovering one finished molecule hidden in advance.

The ordinary scientific payoff is:

> **An outcome can appear because a boundary breaks and previously separated participants become able to react.**

Only here does My GUT Deduction enter.

The current framework keeps the Cut and relation tightly connected. Once A and B are referable, a consequential relation can be written:

`A + B → A + B + R_AB`

The onion offers a restrained physical analogy. Before cutting, relevant precursors and enzymes already exist, but cellular organization constrains which relations can occur. Cutting changes those boundaries. New chemical relations become possible, and their products can participate in further relations: volatile transport through air, contact with the eye, irritation, and tearing.

> **A new relation can make a consequence available that was not a standalone property of either side considered alone.**

A second narrow connection is routing. A biological boundary is not merely an outline. Compartmentalization controls which molecules can meet. Damage changes the available routes and therefore the reaction field.

This is a **candidate structural correspondence**, not a framework derivation. Established plant biochemistry explains alliinase, LFS, the volatile lachrymatory factor, and the tear response.
