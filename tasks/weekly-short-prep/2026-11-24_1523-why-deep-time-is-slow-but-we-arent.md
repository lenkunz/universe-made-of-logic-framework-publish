## A. Finished Short source

# Why Deep Time Is Slow, but We Aren't

*Some changes must pass through generations. Others can be carried forward by people, tools, and records.*

A genetic change does not spread because one animal learned a better trick. For a population to change genetically, variations have to be inherited and become more or less common across generations.

A useful technique can travel by another route. One person shows another. A group improves it. A written instruction, a tool, or a diagram lets someone far away begin from what has already been learned.

The difference is not simply speed. It is **what carries the result forward**.

## Two ways to pass something on

Genetic evolution depends on heritable variation and differences in survival and reproduction. A useful variant can become more common as descendants inherit it. The process works across generations, even though its rate can vary widely.

Cultural information has more routes. It can pass from parents to children, but also between peers and from non-parental adults. Some cultural transmission can happen within a single generation. A person does not have to wait for their children to inherit a technique before sharing it with a colleague or neighbour.

These routes interact. Culture can change the environment in which genes are selected, and biological traits can shape how people learn. This is not a contest between two isolated systems.

## The cost of starting again

Imagine the first person who discovers a reliable way to make a tool. The first attempt may involve many failed tries. If the result stays only in that person's hands, the next person may need to repeat much of the work.

But if the useful part is preserved in a demonstration, a template, a written instruction, or the tool itself, later people can start from a richer position. They can reuse what already works and spend their effort on what is still new.

The earlier work has not been refunded. It has become part of the ground.

## The ratchet

My GUT Deduction describes this as a ratchet:

new relation is costly
→ relation seats
→ seated relation becomes reusable ground
→ later traversal becomes cheaper
→ more capacity remains for new relations

The point is not that genes, brains, books, and networks all use one identical physical process. They do not. The shared structure is narrower: once a relation is established and preserved in a form that later relations can reuse, continuation no longer has to pay the full cost of establishing it again.

Growth is not only adding more structure. It is also turning previously expensive structure into reusable ground.

That is one reason later growth can be faster without assuming a faster universal clock or limitless capacity. The next step begins with more structure already available.

## Reuse is powerful, not permanent

A record can be lost. A practice can be forgotten. An institution can fail. A useful idea may be too costly to transmit or adapt. Reuse does not guarantee permanence, and a hosted system does not automatically become independently seated.

But when a useful relation survives in a reusable carrier, change can accumulate faster than any one person could manage alone.

Deep time is one way for change to accumulate. Reusable ground lets the next step begin further ahead.
