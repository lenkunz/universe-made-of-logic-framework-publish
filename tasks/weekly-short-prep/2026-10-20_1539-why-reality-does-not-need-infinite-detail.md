# Why Reality Does Not Need Infinite Detail

*Fine structure can exist without every relation resolving all of it at once.*

Zoom into a map and more streets appear. Zoom further and you get building outlines, entrances, rooms, perhaps the position of a chair.

But when someone asks whether Bangkok is north of Phuket, none of that extra detail helps answer the question.

The chair is not false. The room is not erased. The streets are not blurry.

They are simply not distinctions the present relation needs.

That sounds almost trivial when we talk about maps. It becomes stranger when we ask what reality itself must keep resolving.

Suppose two things interact. Must that relation independently specify every finer distinction inside both participants before anything can happen?

If the answer were yes, complexity would become explosive. A molecule could not participate as one thing unless every relation involving it also rebuilt all of its internal detail. A person could not open a door without the door-person relation somehow resolving the person's cells, molecules, and every finer distinction beneath them.

More detail would create an ever-growing bill that every later relation had to pay again.

My GUT Deduction takes a different route.

A distinction is resolved when it becomes consequential at the grain of the relation being formed. What does not yet make a consequential difference can remain unresolved relative to that relation.

So a Cut has two sides:

`what must differ here`
→ resolved distinction

and

`what this relation does not need to distinguish`
→ unresolved remainder

That remainder is not necessarily fuzzy.

Imagine a road junction. From far enough away, the junction can be one point on a route. At the junction, several exits are perfectly sharp possibilities. Calling the junction one point did not smear those roads into mush. It simply did not choose among them yet.

This is what **coarse** means in the framework.

Coarse is not low-quality reality. It is reality resolved only as finely as the present relation requires.

That gives a useful answer to a nasty scaling problem.

A rich structure can participate as one domain at another grain. Its finer organization can matter when a relation reaches that grain, without every outside relation having to replay the whole structure first.

The framework therefore does not need a universe that is uniformly rendered at maximum resolution.

It needs something more economical:

> **Resolve the difference when the difference becomes consequential.**

This also means that adding possible detail is not the same as paying to seat every detail.

A coarse constraint can govern many finer continuations. Those continuations do not each need to exist as separately resolved miniature worlds merely because they remain possible.

When one becomes consequential, finer resolution can occur from the ground already present.

There is an important limit here.

This does **not** mean missing detail can always be recovered later. If the present topology no longer preserves a distinction, more effort cannot magically reconstruct the exact answer. Unresolved is not the same as secretly storing a perfect microscopic copy.

Nor is this an established physical law. It is a derived consequence of the framework's own resolution architecture. Any physical mapping still has to earn its correspondence against experiment rather than borrowing certainty from the ontology.

But inside the framework, the answer to the infinite-detail problem is clean.

Reality does not have to destroy fine structure to avoid resolving it everywhere.

It only has to let relations stay at the grain where their differences actually matter.

> **Fine detail can be real without being everybody's problem.**
