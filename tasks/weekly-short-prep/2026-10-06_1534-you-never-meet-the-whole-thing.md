## A. Finished Short source

# You Never Meet the Whole Thing

*Interaction does not require complete access.*

You can talk with someone for an hour without resolving their heartbeat, childhood memories, immune system, or every thought they did not say.

The meeting is still real.

You can borrow one book from a library without inspecting every shelf. You can buy bread from a bakery without tracing every movement in the kitchen. You can ask a doctor one question without the appointment becoming a complete description of your body.

Interaction works because it is selective.

Not selective in the sense that the rest must be fake. Selective in the sense that only some differences matter to the relation taking place.

When you order bread, the consequential distinctions may be:

- which loaf;
- how many;
- what it costs;
- whether the exchange is complete.

The temperature of the back wall may be perfectly real. It simply does not need to become part of that exchange.

Change the relation and the surface changes.

A fire inspector needs distinctions the customer did not. A repair technician may need the oven's wiring. The owner may need tomorrow's flour delivery. Each meets the same bakery through a different set of consequential differences.

The bakery does not become a different object every time. But neither does every relation receive the whole bakery.

This is where the framework's idea of a **domain** becomes useful.

A domain can contain enormous internal relational depth while participating as one point in another relation. “One point” does not mean internally simple. It means the current relation can treat the domain as one participant without separately resolving everything inside it.

That is a change of grain.

At one grain, a person is one participant in a conversation. At another, the same person contains organs, cells, habits, memories, and many ongoing relations. Both descriptions can be real because they answer different relational questions.

The framework calls the exposed part a cheaper relational surface: enough distinction for this relation to work, without paying to reproduce the domain's complete internal structure.

That surface is not always small in importance. A signature can commit an organization. A sentence can alter a friendship. A border crossing can change what becomes possible next. Coarse participation can have enormous scope.

But scope is not the same as internal detail.

This also explains why complete access is not the standard for genuine contact. If every interaction had to reproduce every internal distinction of every participant, relation between complex domains would become impossibly expensive. Nothing rich could meet anything else without first becoming a full copy of it.

Instead:

`rich domain`
→ `relation makes some distinctions consequential`
→ `domain participates through those distinctions`
→ `the rest remains real without being separately resolved here`

The claim is narrow but important.

The framework is not saying that we never learn more, that surfaces cannot mislead, or that hidden structure never matters. A new relation can demand finer grain. The doctor may order a scan. The mechanic may open the machine. A conversation may reach a history that was irrelevant five minutes earlier.

But even then, the new relation resolves what has become consequential to it. It does not automatically render the domain without remainder.

You never meet the whole thing at once.

You meet the distinctions through which it can matter here.

And that is not failed contact.

It is how complex things become able to meet at all.

## Claim-status boundary

- **Framework-native Derived consequence:** Points and domains are grain-relative; a rich domain can participate as one in an outer relation; the outer relation need not separately resolve every inner distinction.
- **Framework-native clarification:** “Cheaper relational surface” names the consequential distinctions through which the domain participates at that grain. It is not a literal skin or a new primitive.
- **Illustrative only:** Conversation, bakery, library, medical, organizational, and border examples make the structure familiar. They do not establish those fields as derived physical or psychological correspondences.
- **Not claimed:** Total knowledge is impossible in every sense; hidden structure never matters; surfaces cannot mislead; the framework has derived a quantitative cost formula for every interaction.
