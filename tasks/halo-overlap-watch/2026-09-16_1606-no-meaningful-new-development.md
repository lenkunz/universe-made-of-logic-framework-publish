## New/recent material examined

### Valdarnini — *Hydrodynamical simulations of the merging cluster El Gordo in a two-state self-interacting dark matter scenario*

- arXiv:2608.21844, submitted 22 August 2026; updated 25 August 2026.
- Uses N-body/hydrodynamical simulations and measured weak-lensing profiles of El Gordo's two colliding clusters.
- Finds that a two-state SIDM model with elastic scattering plus an inelastic endothermic up-scattering channel can reproduce several observed properties, with conclusions sensitive to primary mass and projected DM separation uncertainties.
- **Additive halo/baryonic components accounted for in the required discriminator form? No.** The paper fits/compares merger simulations to weak-lensing profiles; it does not report an overlap-region convergence residual after subtracting fitted halo A, halo B, and ordinary baryonic mass.
- **Classification:** Not a qualifying category-1/2 result for this discriminator. It is relevant dark-matter merger modeling, but does not materially advance the specific non-additive-overlap test.

### September 2026 weak-lensing methodology papers

Several genuinely new weak-lensing methodology/systematics papers were checked, including:

- Zhang, *Baryonic feedback suppression of the matter power spectrum* (arXiv:2609.00807, 1 September 2026), which gives a compact fitting description for baryonic suppression in weak-lensing cosmology.
- Lee et al., *BINDing the lightcone* (arXiv:2609.10710, 9 September 2026), which releases ray-traced weak-lensing/SZ maps spanning baryonic-model variations.
- Ramel et al., *Probabilistic characterization of blending with LSST and application to cluster lensing cosmology* (arXiv:2609.12158, 10 September 2026), which introduces a blending-entropy cut that reduces blending-induced bias in cluster lensing profiles and mass estimates.

These are real methodological advances for weak-lensing accuracy, baryonic modeling, and cluster mass inference, but **none is specifically a method for isolating a residual in the overlap of two merging halos after jointly subtracting the two fitted halos plus baryons**. They therefore do not yet materially change the state of this discriminator.

## Previously relevant cases rechecked for context

### Perseus mass bridge

HyeongHan et al., *Direct Evidence of a Major Merger in the Perseus Cluster* (arXiv:2405.00115; later Nature Astronomy) remains one of the closest known observational cases: weak lensing finds a >3σ mass bridge between the main cluster and NGC 1264 subcluster. The bridge is also traced by member galaxies, and idealized merger simulations generate the bridge through repeated core crossings.

**Additive components accounted for?** Only partially in the more detailed published analysis; it is not a clean full `halo A + halo B + baryons` residual decomposition. Conventional merger dynamics also provide an explanation. No new September result was found that upgrades this case.

### Bullet Cluster

Recent high-resolution JWST/DECam lensing work remains valuable for reconstructing multiple halos and merger geometry, but no newly found September paper reports the required overlap residual after additive halo and baryonic subtraction.

## Final assessment

No paper found in the fresh late-August-to-16-September 2026 window reports a statistically meaningful positive overlap-region mass/convergence residual after explicitly modeling/subtracting both merging halos and ordinary baryonic contributions. No new null test was found that strongly constrains such a residual either.

The newest El Gordo work is relevant to dark-matter merger physics but tests a different question. The September weak-lensing methodology papers improve control of baryonic feedback, ray-traced map realism, and blending bias, but are too general to count as a material methodological advance for this specific overlap discriminator.
