## Sources / papers examined

### Valdarnini (2026), *Hydrodynamical simulations of the merging cluster El Gordo in a two-state self-interacting dark matter scenario*
https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.21844

Published/submitted 22 August 2026.

The paper uses N-body/hydrodynamical simulations to reproduce El Gordo under a two-state self-interacting dark-matter scenario and compares the simulations with observed weak-lensing profiles and projected halo separation.

**Additive halo/baryonic components accounted for?** Not in the discriminator's residual sense. This is a model-fitting/simulation study, not an observational measurement of a positive overlap residual after subtracting fitted halo A, fitted halo B, and ordinary baryons.

**Classification:** Contextual / methodological relevance only. It does not materially advance this specific non-additive overlap test.

### Cho et al. (2026), *Joint JWST-DECam Lensing Reveals That the Bullet Cluster Is a Minor Merger*
ApJ 1005, 28 (July 2026); arXiv:2512.03150.
https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.03150

The joint strong+weak-lensing analysis resolves three distinct halo components and derives robust virial masses for the Bullet Cluster, finding a ~10:1 main-to-subcluster mass ratio.

**Additive halo/baryonic components accounted for?** The work explicitly models multiple halo components and improves their individual mass estimates, but it does not report an overlap-region residual of the form required here after subtracting the fitted halos plus baryons.

**Classification:** Methodologically useful background, but not a new direct or partial test of the discriminator.

### Cha et al. (2025), *A High-Caliber View of the Bullet Cluster Through JWST Strong and Weak Lensing Analyses*
https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.21870

The high-resolution mass reconstruction reports a possible mass/ICL trail extending from the Bullet subcluster toward the main cluster.

**Additive halo/baryonic components accounted for?** No dedicated residual decomposition matching `halo A + halo B + baryons` is demonstrated for that trail. Projection/systematic concerns remain part of the interpretation.

**Classification:** Previously known Partial / suggestive correspondence at most; no new development in this run.

### HyeongHan et al. (2025), *Direct evidence of a major merger in the Perseus cluster*
Nature Astronomy 9, 925–931.
https://www.nature.com/articles/s41550-025-02530-w

This remains the closest known observational case to the discriminator. A >3σ mass bridge connects the main Perseus halo and the NGC 1264 subcluster. The published line-profile test subtracts the two halo radial profiles: midpoint significance falls from ~3σ to ~2σ, while integrated bridge significance remains 3.2σ.

**Additive halo/baryonic components accounted for?** The two halo profiles are explicitly subtracted in the line-profile analysis, so this goes materially beyond merely seeing two overlapping halos. However, ordinary baryonic contribution is not cleanly removed in the same residual expression, the bridge is also traced by member galaxies, and the authors' merger simulations can generate the bridge through repeated core passages.

**Classification:** Previously known Partial / suggestive correspondence plus Conventional explanation. Important, but not new in this run.

## Final assessment

No genuinely new result in the current literature window materially changes the state of the test.

The strongest known case remains Perseus because a positive integrated bridge signal survives subtraction of the two halo profiles. It still does not satisfy the full discriminator cleanly because ordinary baryonic contribution is not independently removed in the same residual construction and conventional merger dynamics can reproduce the bridge.

The Bullet Cluster JWST work improves halo modeling and reports an interesting trail, but no publication located in this run demonstrates that the trail remains as a positive residual after fitted halo and baryonic subtraction.

**Final status: No meaningful new development.**
