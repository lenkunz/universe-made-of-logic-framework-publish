## Sources / papers examined in this run

### HyeongHan et al., “Direct Evidence of a Major Merger in the Perseus Cluster”
Nature Astronomy; arXiv:2405.00115.
https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.00115

Weak-lensing analysis identifies a >3σ mass bridge between the Perseus main halo and subcluster. This remains the most discriminator-like known case in the search set.

**Additive components accounted for?** Partially. Two halo profiles are subtracted in the published analysis, but this is not a complete decomposition including all ordinary baryonic contribution. The bridge is also traced by member galaxies, and idealized merger simulations reproduce bridge formation through repeated core passages.

**Classification:** Previously known Partial / suggestive correspondence plus conventional explanation. Not new in this run.

### Cho et al., “Joint JWST-DECam Lensing Reveals That the Bullet Cluster Is a Minor Merger”
arXiv:2512.03150; published 2026 in ApJ.
https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.03150

Provides improved weak+strong-lensing constraints and resolves three halo components, materially refining merger geometry and masses.

**Additive components accounted for?** Multiple halos are modeled, but no dedicated positive overlap residual after subtraction of the principal halos plus baryons is reported.

**Classification:** Methodologically relevant background, but not a new discriminator-level result.

### Famaey, “On the residual missing mass of the Bullet Cluster”
arXiv:2605.10022.
https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.10022

Discusses residual missing mass in the Bullet Cluster in a MOND context using updated JWST-based lensing information.

**Additive components accounted for?** This is not an overlap-region residual test of `halo A + halo B + baryons`; it concerns cluster-scale residual missing mass in an alternative-gravity accounting.

**Classification:** Not a direct or partial test of this discriminator.

### Zhang et al., “Baryonic mass budgets in the central regions of the Bullet Cluster and their consistency with strong lensing in MOND”
arXiv:2606.19454.
https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.19454

Re-estimates central baryonic mass budgets under IGIMF assumptions and argues that less unseen mass may be required in the core regions.

**Additive components accounted for?** Baryonic accounting is central to the paper, but it does not isolate a positive overlap-region residual beyond two fitted merging halos and baryons.

**Classification:** Conventional/baryonic accounting context, not a new overlap discriminator test.
