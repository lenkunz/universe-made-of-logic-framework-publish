## Meaningful result

### Cha et al., *Weak-lensing Analysis of Intracluster Filaments in Abell 2744: Matched-filter Scans and Stepwise 2D Tracing*

arXiv:2604.07445. First submitted 8 April 2026; revised in August 2026, with a recent v3/indexing surfacing in the current search.

The study uses wide-field Magellan/MegaCam weak lensing to detect and characterize filamentary mass structures around the complex merging cluster Abell 2744. It combines conventional matched-filter scans with a new stepwise 2D tracing method that follows locally varying/curved shear structure rather than forcing a filament to pass through one global reference point.

A particularly relevant technical feature is that the filament matched-filter analysis subtracts the host cluster's azimuthally averaged tangential shear profile before searching for the much weaker filament signal, and models line-of-sight large-scale-structure noise. The stepwise method then traces locally coherent directions and materially improves the fit for the eastern structure, whose parameters were unstable under a single global direction. The detected NW and eastern structures align with diffuse X-ray structures and previously inferred merger axes.

**Classification:** Methodological advance.

**Why it matters to the discriminator:** This is not the desired binary-halo overlap residual test, but it demonstrates a practical weak-lensing pipeline for removing a dominant host-halo contribution and then extracting low-amplitude, geometrically complex residual/filamentary shear. The stepwise tracing specifically reduces the risk that a curved or offset overlap-associated structure is washed out by an overly rigid straight-axis template. That makes it directly useful as a methodological ingredient for a future `halo A + halo B + baryons` subtraction test.

**Were additive halo/baryonic components accounted for?** Only partially. The host cluster's azimuthally averaged shear is subtracted for the filament search, but this is not a decomposition of two fitted merger halos plus ordinary baryons followed by an inter-halo residual measurement. X-ray alignment is used as physical context, not as a complete baryonic subtraction.

**Strongest caveat:** The structures are interpreted as ordinary cosmic-web/intracluster filaments associated with Abell 2744's assembly. The paper does not claim a non-additive overlap excess, and its detected filament signal must not be counted as support for the framework prediction itself.

## Assessment

This run changes the **methodological** state of the test, not the observational evidence for the predicted excess. Cha et al.'s host-halo subtraction plus locally adaptive 2D weak-lensing tracing is a useful candidate technique for designing a sharper overlap-residual search in complex mergers.
