## New/recent material examined

### Dutra, Natarajan & Roche — *Where Cluster Galaxies Reside and How Strongly They Lens* (arXiv:2610.14167)

Submitted 2026-10-09; updated 2026-10-14.

The paper studies galaxy-galaxy strong-lensing efficiency in observed HST clusters versus TNG-Cluster analogs. It separates member galaxies by whether they lens independently or are boosted by the surrounding cluster field, and reports differences in cluster-critical occupancy and boosted-lens fractions that persist at fixed subhalo mass and after inclusion of line-of-sight structure.

**Relevance to discriminator:** environmental lensing fields can materially change local lensing strength, reinforcing that residual analyses must propagate cluster-field and line-of-sight effects rather than treating subcomponents in isolation.

**Additive halo/baryonic components accounted for?** No. This is not an overlap-region residual analysis and does not subtract two merger halos plus ordinary baryons.

**Classification:** Not promoted to a notification-worthy Methodological advance for this specific watch. It is useful background/systematics context, but it does not materially improve or constrain the pairwise non-additive overlap test beyond controls already identified in earlier runs.

### Patel et al. — *The BUFFALO Survey: The Subhalo Mass Function for Abell 2744 with Strong+Weak Gravitational Lensing*

Recently deposited/indexed material was rechecked. The reported excess of detected substructures in Abell 2744 relative to BAHAMAS is interpreted by the authors as likely connected to the cluster's exceptionally disturbed merger state. This result was already surfaced in a prior watch run and is therefore not new for notification purposes.

**Additive halo/baryonic components accounted for?** Not in the required `halo A + halo B + baryons` residual form.

**Classification:** Previously surfaced conventional-explanation/methodological control; no new state change today.

### Valdarnini — *Hydrodynamical simulations of the merging cluster El Gordo in a two-state self-interacting dark matter scenario* (arXiv:2608.21844)

Recent searches resurfaced this August 2026 work. It compares hydrodynamical merger simulations to weak-lensing constraints for El Gordo and emphasizes sensitivity to primary mass and projected dark-matter separation.

**Additive halo/baryonic components accounted for?** No dedicated observational overlap residual after two fitted halos and baryons are removed.

**Classification:** Relevant merger-model background, but not new in the present window and not a direct test of the discriminator.
