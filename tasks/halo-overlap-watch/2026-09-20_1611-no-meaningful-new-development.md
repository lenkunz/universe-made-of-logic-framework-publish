## Sources/papers examined

### Scofield et al., *An Active Galaxy Cluster Merger at Cosmic Noon Revealed by JWST Weak Lensing and Multiwavelength Probes*
ApJ Letters 999 L1 (2026); arXiv:2512.11022.

Current indexing continues to surface this JWST weak-lensing reconstruction of XLSSC 122 at z≈1.98. It finds a mass peak coincident with the X-ray peak and BCG, coherent NE–SW elongation across datasets, and an SZ versus mass/X-ray offset consistent with merger activity.

**Additive components accounted for?** No dedicated `halo A + halo B + baryons` subtraction or inter-halo residual test is reported.

**Classification:** contextual/methodological merger-lensing work, not a new test of the discriminator.

### Cha et al., *A High-Caliber View of the Bullet Cluster Through JWST Strong and Weak Lensing Analyses*
ApJ Letters 987 L15 (2025); arXiv:2503.21870.

Current searches continue to surface the possible mass + intracluster-light trail from the Bullet subcluster toward the main cluster. This is already known and was covered in earlier watch runs.

**Additive components accounted for?** No. The trail is not established as a residual after separately fitted main/subcluster halos and ordinary baryons are removed.

**Classification:** previously known partial/suggestive feature at most; no new development.

### HyeongHan et al., Perseus merger / mass bridge
Nature Astronomy (2025), previously tracked.

The Perseus bridge remains the closest known observational case because earlier analysis included subtraction of two halo radial profiles and retained an integrated bridge signal, but it is not new in this run and does not complete the full halo-plus-baryon residual decomposition. Conventional merger-history simulations also reproduce bridge-like structure.

**Additive components accounted for?** Partially, not fully.

**Classification:** previously known partial/suggestive correspondence plus conventional explanation; unchanged.

### Hackett, *Long-Lived Merger Signatures in the Perseus Cluster and a Candidate Remnant Interpretation*
Galaxies 14(3):52 (2026); preprint posted April 2026.

This paper proposes a residual mass–gas-offset observational program motivated by Perseus, but it is not a new lensing measurement of the requested inter-halo residual and predates the current search window.

**Additive components accounted for?** Not applicable as a new measurement.

**Classification:** related proposed methodology/interpretation, not a new development for this watch.
