## Findings

### HyeongHan et al., *Direct evidence of a major merger in the Perseus cluster* (Nature Astronomy, 2025)

Still the closest observational case to the discriminator. The weak-lensing analysis reports a >3σ mass bridge between the main Perseus halo and the NGC 1264 subcluster. In the published line-profile test, subtraction of the two halo radial profiles reduces the bridge midpoint from about 3σ to about 2σ, while the integrated bridge signal between the halos remains 3.2σ.

**Assessment:** Partial / suggestive correspondence, not a direct framework-specific residual. The bridge is also traced by cluster-member galaxies, and the authors' ordinary merger simulations generate the bridge through multiple core crossings. The analysis is not a complete `halo A + halo B + baryons` residual decomposition.

### Hackett, *Long-Lived Merger Signatures in the Perseus Cluster and a Candidate Remnant Interpretation* (Galaxies, 2026)

This 2026 paper discusses the already-published Perseus weak-lensing bridge and proposes a broader observational program based on residual mass–gas offsets and merger-history proxies.

**Assessment:** Conceptually/methodologically adjacent, but it does not provide a new independent overlap-residual measurement satisfying the discriminator. It therefore does not change the evidence status.

### Saha et al., *Lensing in the Blue III: Weak Lensing Shape Catalogs of 30 Merging Galaxy Clusters* (2026)

A new SuperBIT weak-lensing data product covering 30 merging clusters, with calibrated galaxy shapes, PSF-residual diagnostics, and simulation-based shear-bias tests.

**Assessment:** Potentially useful enabling data for a future stacked or per-system overlap-residual test, but the work itself does not report the required positive residual after additive halo/baryon subtraction. Methodological opportunity, not evidence for the discriminator.

### 2026 review: *Merging Galaxy Clusters and the Search for New Physics of Dark Matter*

The review emphasizes how merger-lensing conclusions can be sensitive to smoothing, centroid definitions, low-S/N peaks, star masking, and asymmetric dark-matter wakes.

**Assessment:** Relevant caution for designing the discriminator. It strengthens the need for preregistered halo subtraction, matched smoothing, null rotations/control regions, and robust pipeline variants, but does not supply a new positive overlap-excess result.
