### Cho et al., *Joint JWST–DECam Lensing Reveals that the Bullet Cluster Is a Minor Merger*
The Astrophysical Journal 1005, 28 (July 2026), DOI 10.3847/1538-4357/ae6a90; arXiv:2512.03150.

Source checked: https://scixplorer.org/abs/2026ApJ..1005...28C/abstract

This joint weak+strong-lensing analysis resolves three halos and obtains robust virial masses, yielding a main-to-subcluster mass ratio of about 10:1 and classifying the Bullet Cluster as a minor merger. It materially improves the component mass model and future merger initial conditions.

**Additive halo/baryonic components accounted for for this discriminator?** The paper improves halo decomposition and mass calibration, but the result identified in this run does not report a dedicated overlap-region residual after subtracting the fitted main halo, subcluster halo, third halo, and baryonic contribution.

**Classification:** Methodologically relevant background, not a new discriminator-level update. It predates the 2026-09-07 baseline and does not justify a new notification now.

### Previously tracked closest observational case: Perseus
HyeongHan et al., *Direct Evidence of a Major Merger in the Perseus Cluster*, Nature Astronomy (2025); arXiv:2405.00115.

Source checked: https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.00115

Perseus remains the closest discriminator-like case already tracked: a >3σ mass bridge is reported between the main cluster and NGC 1264 subcluster, and prior detailed inspection found that integrated bridge significance survives partial subtraction of the two halo profiles. However, the bridge is also traced by cluster member galaxies, and idealized merger simulations reproduce a bridge through ordinary repeated core passages.

**Additive halo/baryonic components accounted for?** Only partially. It is not a full residual after fitted halo A + halo B + ordinary baryons.

**Classification:** Partial / suggestive correspondence with a strong conventional merger explanation, but not a new development today.
