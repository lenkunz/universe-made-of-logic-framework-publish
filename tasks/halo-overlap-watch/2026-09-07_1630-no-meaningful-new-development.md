### Roche et al., *Host Dependence and Line-of-Sight Effects on Galaxy-Galaxy Strong Lensing in Clusters*
arXiv:2608.30079, submitted 2026-08-30.

This recently indexed paper studies why observed galaxy-galaxy strong-lensing probabilities in clusters can exceed simulated expectations. It finds that correlated line-of-sight structure can enhance the lensing probability by a few percent for typical systems and up to roughly 15% for efficient lenses; baryonic physics and host-cluster Einstein radius also materially affect the result. Matching simulated and observed selection reduces some of the discrepancy, while the remaining tension depends strongly on field-of-view definition.

**Additive halo/baryonic components accounted for for this discriminator?** No. The paper is about galaxy-galaxy strong-lensing event probabilities and line-of-sight/selection effects, not an overlap-region residual between merger halos after subtracting fitted components.

**Classification:** Conventional/systematics context for interpreting cluster-lensing excesses, but not a material update to this specific overlap discriminator. It also predates the previous 2026-09-05 check and does not justify a new notification now.
