## Sources / results examined

### HyeongHan et al. — *Direct evidence of a major merger in the Perseus cluster* (Nature Astronomy 2025)
Still the closest known observational analogue found in this run: a >3σ mass bridge between the Perseus main halo and NGC 1264 subcluster, with idealized merger simulations able to generate the bridge through repeated core passages.

**Additive components accounted for?** Only partially for this discriminator. Prior analyses of the bridge include halo-profile comparison/subtraction, but the published result is not a complete `halo A + halo B + ordinary baryons` residual decomposition. The bridge is also traced by cluster member galaxies and has a conventional merger-history explanation.

**Classification:** Previously known partial/suggestive correspondence plus conventional explanation; not a new development.

### Hackett — *Long-Lived Merger Signatures in the Perseus Cluster and a Candidate Remnant Interpretation* (Galaxies 2026, published 18 May 2026)
Uses Perseus to motivate a population-level test of whether nominally relaxed clusters retain merger-history information in residual mass–gas offsets. It explicitly identifies heterogeneous archival measurements as a weakness and calls for a homogeneous weak-lensing/X-ray/SZ pipeline with controls for projected substructure and line-of-sight geometry.

**Additive components accounted for?** No. This is a phenomenological/pilot population argument, not the requested overlap residual subtraction.

**Classification:** Methodologically relevant background, already known before the current search window; not a new development.

### Chappuis et al. — *Multi-scale weak lensing detection of galaxy clusters with source redshift tomography* (A&A 710, A318; 29 June 2026)
Develops tomographic/multi-scale weak-lensing detection to improve cluster detections in simulated datasets.

**Additive components accounted for?** No merger-specific two-halo plus baryon residual decomposition.

**Classification:** General weak-lensing methodological background, but not sufficiently specific to materially change this discriminator.

### Scofield et al. — *An Active Galaxy Cluster Merger at Cosmic Noon Revealed by JWST Weak Lensing and Multiwavelength Probes* (ApJL 999 L1, March 2026)
JWST weak-lensing mass reconstruction of XLSSC 122 finds coherent merger-related elongation and multiwavelength offsets.

**Additive components accounted for?** No dedicated inter-halo residual after subtracting fitted merger halos and baryons.

**Classification:** Merger-lensing context, not a direct/partial test of the discriminator.
