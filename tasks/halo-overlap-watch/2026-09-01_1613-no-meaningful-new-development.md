## New literature examined

### Valdarnini (2026), “Hydrodynamical simulations of the merging cluster El Gordo in a two-state self-interacting dark matter scenario”

- **Date:** 2026-08-22
- **Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.21844
- **What was measured/modelled:** A large N-body/hydrodynamical simulation suite was compared against observed weak-lensing profiles of the two colliding El Gordo subclusters. The paper argues that a two-state SIDM model can reproduce several observed properties for a particular range of elastic and inelastic cross-sections, while stressing sensitivity to primary mass and projected DM-halo separation.
- **Were additive halo/baryonic components removed in the discriminator’s sense?** **No.** The analysis compares merger simulations and component weak-lensing profiles. It does not demonstrate an overlap-region positive residual after subtracting fitted halo A, fitted halo B, and ordinary baryonic mass.
- **Classification:** Relevant merger-lensing / dark-matter-model context, but **not a qualifying methodological advance for this discriminator** and not a Direct or Partial correspondence. It does not materially improve isolation of a non-additive overlap excess.
- **Strongest caveat for this watch:** The paper is about fitting the component profiles and merger configuration, not about residual mass in the overlap after additive components are removed.

## Previously relevant cases rechecked for updates

### Perseus Cluster — HyeongHan et al., “Direct Evidence of a Major Merger in the Perseus Cluster”

- **Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.00115
- Reports a >3σ mass bridge between the main cluster and a subcluster.
- The original analysis is unusually close to the discriminator because the two halo radial profiles were explicitly subtracted in a line-profile test; the midpoint signal falls while an integrated bridge signal remains significant.
- **Additive components accounted for?** Partially, not completely. The bridge is also traced by cluster member galaxies, and merger simulations reproduce a bridge through repeated passages.
- **Classification:** Existing **Partial / suggestive correspondence plus conventional merger explanation**, not a new development in this run.

### Bullet Cluster — current JWST-era lensing work

Recent Bullet Cluster work located in this run includes MOND/baryonic-budget analyses and the existing JWST lens reconstructions, but no newly surfaced paper was found that isolates a positive overlap-region residual after subtracting both fitted halos and ordinary baryons.

These address total/core missing-mass accounting under alternative gravity/baryonic assumptions, not the specific non-additive overlap residual targeted here.

## Assessment

No genuinely new result found in the present window materially changes the state of the overlap-residual test.

The new El Gordo simulation paper is worth tracking because it uses weak-lensing profiles to constrain merger dark-matter physics, but it does **not** ask whether an extra convergence/mass component remains specifically in the overlap after subtracting the two fitted halos and baryons. Counting it as support would move the goalposts.

Perseus remains the closest known observational case located by this watch, because a bridge signal persists in an analysis that explicitly subtracts two halo profiles. However, the subtraction is not the full `halo A + halo B + baryons` decomposition, and the bridge has a conventional merger-history interpretation in the authors’ simulations. Its status therefore remains suggestive rather than direct.
