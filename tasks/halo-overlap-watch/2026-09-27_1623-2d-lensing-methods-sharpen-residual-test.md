## Meaningful new work

### Murray, Kilbinger & Payerne — *Two-dimensional weak lensing shear for cluster mass, concentration, ellipticity and miscentering estimation*

- arXiv:2609.24948
- Submitted 2026-09-21; revised 2026-09-23
- https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.24948

**What was measured/tested:** A simulation-based-inference forward model fits the full two-dimensional reduced-shear field of a galaxy cluster rather than compressing it to a one-dimensional radial profile. The model includes cluster mass, concentration, miscentering and ellipticity, plus non-Gaussian correlated large-scale structure from N-body simulations and uncorrelated line-of-sight structure.

**Result:** The two-dimensional treatment improves cluster-mass constraints by up to 20%. The authors also find that omitting line-of-sight structure can underestimate the mass uncertainty by as much as a factor of two for the most massive clusters.

**Were additive halo and baryonic components removed or modeled?** **No.** This is a single-cluster methodological study, not a merger-overlap analysis. It does not fit halo A + halo B + ordinary baryons and inspect the remaining overlap convergence.

**Classification:** **Methodological advance.**

**Why it matters for the discriminator:** A credible subtraction test must not mistake halo ellipticity, miscentering, correlated structure, or line-of-sight structure for an inter-halo residual. Full-field inference provides a materially better foundation for fitting each halo while carrying these nuisance effects into the residual uncertainty. The reported uncertainty inflation is especially important: a nominal residual can look more significant if line-of-sight structure is omitted.

**Strongest caveat:** The method has not yet been extended or demonstrated on a binary merging cluster, and it does not include the required baryonic decomposition.

### Williams et al. — *Shape Degeneracies: the likely culprit for the differences between lens mass models of galaxy clusters*

- arXiv:2609.27147
- Submitted 2026-09-22
- https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.27147

**What was measured/tested:** An analytical study of why independently reconstructed strong-lensing cluster mass maps can disagree in their small-scale isodensity shapes.

**Result:** The paper attributes much of the disagreement between mass-map techniques on scales below about 30 kpc to generalized shape degeneracies not captured by the usual mass-sheet or source-position transformations. These degeneracies are substantially suppressed where multiple lensed images are tightly grouped.

**Were additive halo and baryonic components removed or modeled?** **No.** This paper does not analyze a merging overlap region or subtract two fitted halos plus baryons.

**Classification:** **Methodological advance** (primarily a systematic-control result).

**Why it matters for the discriminator:** It identifies a concrete way a small apparent positive residual in a strong-lensing convergence map could be reconstruction-dependent rather than physical. It also gives an observational/design safeguard: the strongest residual claims should come from overlap regions with dense, local multiple-image constraints and should survive materially different lens-model families.

**Strongest caveat:** The result concerns general cluster mass-map degeneracies and sub-30-kpc structure; it is not a direct sensitivity forecast for a broad inter-halo overlap excess.
