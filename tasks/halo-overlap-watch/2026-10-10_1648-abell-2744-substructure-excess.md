## Meaningful new result

### Patel et al. (2026), *The BUFFALO Survey: The Subhalo Mass Function for Abell 2744 with Strong+Weak Gravitational Lensing*

- arXiv:2610.08919, submitted 2026-10-06.
- MNRAS DOI: 10.1093/mnras/stag1849; accepted 2026-08-26 and online publication reported 2026-09-26, with the arXiv version newly surfaced this week.
- The authors combine strong-lensing constraints in the core with HST/JWST weak-lensing measurements to reconstruct projected mass from the core to the outskirts of the highly disturbed merging cluster Abell 2744.
- They identify substructures and construct the subhalo mass function, then apply the same detection procedure to projected mass maps of massive BAHAMAS simulated clusters.
- They report a systematic excess of detected substructures in Abell 2744 relative to BAHAMAS.
- The authors interpret the excess as likely reflecting Abell 2744's exceptionally disturbed, complex ongoing merger state rather than evidence of tension with ΛCDM or SIDM, while noting that a larger sample across dynamical states is needed.

**Classification:** Methodological advance, with a conventional-explanation caution relevant to residual searches.

### Additive components accounted for?

**No, not in the form required by the framework discriminator.** The analysis reconstructs the total projected lensing mass and studies detected substructures statistically. It does not fit and subtract a designated pair of overlapping halos plus an ordinary baryonic mass model and then measure the remaining overlap-region convergence.

Therefore the reported substructure excess is **not** a Direct Match and is **not** Partial / suggestive correspondence for the framework prediction itself.

### Why it matters to the discriminator

The result provides an important control lesson: a violently disturbed merger can contain an unusually rich projected substructure population under an ordinary cosmological interpretation. A future positive overlap residual should therefore be tested against merger-state-dependent substructure and projection, rather than compared only with relaxed-cluster expectations or a smooth two-halo baseline.

The strong+weak reconstruction spanning core to outskirts, combined with identical detection on hydrodynamical simulation projections, is a useful architecture for constructing such a null distribution. A framework-specific test would need to go one step further: define the relevant halo pair, model/subtract their ordinary profiles and baryons, measure the overlap residual in a pre-specified aperture/geometry, and compare that statistic against simulated mergers processed through the same pipeline.

### Strongest caveat

The measured quantity is subhalo abundance, not a pairwise overlap residual. The observed excess therefore cannot presently distinguish additional shared-overlap mass from ordinary merger complexity, projection, or transient substructure.
