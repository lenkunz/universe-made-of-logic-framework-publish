## Meaningful new result

### Lee et al. — *TNGC-SIDM I: Evolution of massive cluster mergers under the self-interacting dark matter paradigm*

- **arXiv:** 2610.18957
- **Submitted:** 2026-10-13; surfaced in the subsequent arXiv listing
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18957
- **Authors:** Wonki Lee et al.

The paper performs cosmological magnetohydrodynamical zoom-in simulations of two cluster mergers selected from TNG-Cluster under collisionless CDM and SIDM. The massive system is a Bullet-like ~1:3 merger with a first-pericenter separation of ~80 kpc and collision velocity of ~3000 km/s. The study follows halo, gas, ICL, radio-relic and BCG observables through the merger history.

Important results for designing merger tests include:

- halo separation evolves differently under CDM and SIDM and can differ by ~100–200 kpc at useful merger phases;
- double-radio-relic separation evolves much more similarly across the tested dark-matter models, making it a possible merger-phase reference;
- projection angle, merger phase, minor mergers, baryonic gas drag, formation history and numerical resolution can all alter apparent halo observables;
- the sign and magnitude of a dark-matter-induced orbital difference can change in minor mergers or after later passages;
- the authors therefore argue that sample selection and multi-wavelength constraints are essential when testing dark-matter physics with mergers.

### Classification

**4. Methodological advance.** It materially improves the control-model design for the halo-overlap discriminator by showing that ordinary merger history, baryonic gas dynamics, viewing angle, merger phase and substructure history must be conditioned on before interpreting a residual as anomalous.

It also carries a **conventional-explanation warning**: ordinary CDM merger dynamics plus baryonic physics can generate phase-dependent changes in halo positions and morphology that could bias an overlap-region subtraction if the two fitted halos are treated as static or overly simple components.

### Were additive halo/baryonic components accounted for?

**Not in the form required by the discriminator.** This is a forward cosmological MHD simulation study, not an observational residual analysis. It does not take an observed convergence map and explicitly compute `observed overlap − fitted halo A − fitted halo B − ordinary baryons`.

However, unlike dark-matter-only null models, the simulations include baryonic galaxy-formation physics and intracluster gas, allowing ordinary merger-driven effects to be propagated into observables. This makes the work relevant for constructing a physically richer null distribution for a future residual test.

### Why this changes the test design

A robust future overlap test should not compare the observed residual only with two static analytic halos. It should compare against merger-conditioned controls matched, where possible, on:

1. merger phase;
2. mass ratio and impact parameter;
3. viewing angle / line-of-sight velocity;
4. gas morphology and baryonic mass;
5. radio-relic or shock separation as a phase proxy;
6. recent minor-merger / accretion history;
7. the same lensing reconstruction and halo-subtraction pipeline.

The framework prediction is then best tested against the distribution of post-subtraction overlap residuals generated by ordinary CDM+baryonic merger histories, rather than against a single smooth-halo baseline.

### Strongest caveat

Only two cosmological merger systems are simulated, so the paper does not yet provide a population-level null distribution. It also studies SIDM observables rather than the framework's proposed shared-seating residual directly.
