## Sources / papers examined

### HyeongHan et al., “Direct evidence of a major merger in the Perseus cluster”
Nature Astronomy (2025); arXiv:2405.00115.

The search again surfaced the known >3σ weak-lensing mass bridge between the Perseus main halo and the NGC 1264 subcluster. The published analysis and simulations interpret the bridge as merger-related structure and galaxy-member distribution rather than as a demonstrated non-additive residual.

**Additive components accounted for?** Partially in the published analysis, but not as a complete `halo A + halo B + baryons` residual decomposition matching the discriminator.

**Classification:** Previously known Partial / suggestive correspondence with a conventional merger explanation. Not a new development in this run.

### Rihtaršič et al., “Mapping dark matter in the Bullet Cluster using JWST imaging and spectroscopy”
arXiv:2601.22245 (2026).

Updated strong-lensing reconstruction using JWST NIRCam + NIRSpec, with 135 secure multiple images from 27 spectroscopic background galaxies. The model includes several large-scale halos, cluster members, intracluster gas, and group-scale halos, and reports detailed mass-distribution differences relative to earlier lens models.

**Additive components accounted for?** The lens model explicitly includes multiple halo and baryonic components, which is methodologically relevant. However, the paper does not report a dedicated positive overlap residual remaining after subtracting / marginalizing the two principal cluster halos plus ordinary baryons.

**Classification:** Methodologically relevant background, but no new discriminator-level result surfaced in this run.

### Shi & Cui, “Nonlinear Weak Lensing reconstruction for Galaxy Clusters”
arXiv:2601.02816 (2026).

Develops a nonlinear weak-lensing reconstruction method aimed at improving mass mapping in high-convergence cluster cores. In simulations without shape noise, reported residuals can be below 0.02σ in unmasked regions.

**Additive components accounted for?** This is a reconstruction-method paper rather than a merger-overlap subtraction analysis.

**Classification:** General methodological advance for cluster mass mapping, but not newly surfaced in the current window and not specific enough to materially change this discriminator today.

### Umetsu et al., “CHEX-MATE: AMALGAM weak-lensing analysis of 41 Planck Sunyaev-Zel'dovich-selected galaxy clusters”
arXiv:2606.24142 (2026).

Large weak-lensing calibration analysis of 41 Planck clusters using Subaru and CFHT imaging. It models azimuthally averaged excess surface mass-density profiles with NFW halos and carefully treats calibration and modeling biases.

**Additive components accounted for?** The study is not an overlap-region residual analysis of merging halo pairs.

**Classification:** Useful weak-lensing methodology / calibration context, not a direct or partial test of the discriminator.

### Zhang et al., “Baryonic mass budgets in the central regions of the Bullet Cluster and their consistency with strong lensing in MOND”
arXiv:2606.19454 (2026).

Re-estimates baryonic masses in Bullet Cluster core regions under an IGIMF-based stellar-population treatment and argues that larger baryonic budgets can reduce the unseen-mass requirement in those cores.

**Additive components accounted for?** This changes assumptions about baryonic mass budgets but does not perform the required two-halo overlap residual test.

**Classification:** Conventional / alternative baryonic-model context relevant to mass decomposition, but not a new overlap-residual result and not a material change to this test today.
