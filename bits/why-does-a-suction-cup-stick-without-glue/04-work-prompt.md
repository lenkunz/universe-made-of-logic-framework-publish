## Episode-specific article focus

Start from the familiar bathroom/window suction-cup mystery. Establish ordinary pressure physics before framework vocabulary. Make the tiny-leak transition the centerpiece: a narrow new route can erase a large pressure-supported capability while most visible geometry remains unchanged.

Framework connection should be narrow and late: **Interiority is routing before geometry.** Present the suction cup only as a candidate structural correspondence, not as evidence that the framework derives pressure or fluid mechanics.

Keep this distinct from `bits/how-can-a-gecko-stick-without-glue/`: gecko adhesion owns hierarchical dry contact and peeling; this Bit owns pressure difference plus seal/leak routing.

## Google Flow / image concept

Create a 16:9 editorial landscape macro image of a transparent or translucent suction cup attached to a clean vertical glass pane, positioned center-right. Show the cup strongly seated over most of its circular rim, but one tiny section of the rim is just beginning to lift. Through the transparent cup, make the enclosed cavity subtly visible; at the lifted edge, show only a faint realistic intrusion of outside air or a tiny gap, not arrows or a diagram. The visual tension is **a large grip depending on one nearly invisible sealed route, with one small leak threatening the whole attachment**. Leave generous negative space on the left for title placement. Use realistic daylight, restrained scientific/editorial photography, crisp rubber and glass textures. Avoid glue, gecko feet, arrows, pressure labels, equations, glowing air, vacuum-as-a-black-hole imagery, sci-fi effects, galaxies, brains, and baked-in text.
