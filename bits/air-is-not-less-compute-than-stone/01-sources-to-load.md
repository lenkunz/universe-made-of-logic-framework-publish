## External sources

- [ ] OpenStax, **Fluids, Density, and Pressure**
  https://openstax.org/books/university-physics-volume-1/pages/14-1-fluids-density-and-pressure
  - Why NotebookLM needs it: clear operational definition of pressure and the distinction between force and force per area.
  - Established explanation supported: pressure is a scalar normal force per unit area; a fluid develops reaction force when constrained or compressed.

- [ ] OpenStax, **Molecular Model of an Ideal Gas**
  https://openstax.org/books/university-physics-volume-2/pages/2-1-molecular-model-of-an-ideal-gas
  - Why NotebookLM needs it: pressure-volume-temperature relation, Boyle's law, and the effect of a resisting boundary such as a tire wall.
  - Established explanation supported: for an approximately ideal gas with fixed molecule count and temperature, pressure and volume vary inversely; when a boundary resists expansion, adding or compressing gas changes pressure.

- [ ] OpenStax, **Kinetic Theory: Atomic and Molecular Explanation of Pressure and Temperature**
  https://openstax.org/books/college-physics-2e/pages/13-4-kinetic-theory-atomic-and-molecular-explanation-of-pressure-and-temperature
  - Why NotebookLM needs it: microscopic account of gas pressure as the average effect of molecular collisions with a boundary.
  - Established explanation supported: gas molecules transfer momentum to container walls; the collective result is measured as force per area.

- [ ] OpenStax, **Stress, Strain, and Elastic Modulus**
  https://openstax.org/books/university-physics-volume-1/pages/12-3-stress-strain-and-elastic-modulus
  - Why NotebookLM needs it: contrasts a solid's deformation response with a gas-plus-boundary system without reducing either to “more density.”
  - Established explanation supported: materials respond to stress with strain; elastic moduli quantify how strongly a material resists particular deformation modes.

- [ ] OpenStax, **Intermolecular Forces**
  https://openstax.org/books/chemistry-2e/pages/10-1-intermolecular-forces
  - Why NotebookLM needs it: ordinary molecular distinction among solid, liquid, and gas and the role of particle arrangement, motion, intermolecular attraction, temperature, and pressure.
  - Established explanation supported: phases differ in molecular organization and motion; phase depends on conditions and interactions, not on a single generic “amount of stuff per space.”

- [ ] Bureau International des Poids et Mesures, **SI Brochure**
  https://www.bipm.org/en/publications/si-brochure
  - Why NotebookLM needs it: authoritative measurement-layer reference for the pascal and the derived-unit structure of pressure.
  - Established explanation supported: the pascal is a derived SI unit, `Pa = N/m²`; calling a quantity derived does not make it unreal.

- [ ] NASA Technical Reports Server, **Inflatable Habitats**
  https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20110000798
  - Why NotebookLM needs it: engineering example in which internal pressure, a bladder, and a restraint layer work together as a load-bearing structure.
  - Established explanation supported: an inflatable system's supporting capacity belongs to the organized gas-boundary structure, not to unconstrained air alone.

## Source discipline

Keep these layers separate:

1. established pressure, gas, elasticity, phase, and structural engineering;
2. framework-native rules about relation, resolution, persistence, budget, and anchor closure;
3. the candidate mapping from material states to persistence regimes.

Do not use the ordinary fact that an air mattress supports a person as proof that My GUT Deduction has derived pressure or matter.

Do not equate framework `budget` with joules, pressure, temperature, density, molecular collision rate, elastic modulus, or stress-energy.
