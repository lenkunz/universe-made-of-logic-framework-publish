## Central question

Why can metal and wood at the same room temperature feel so different to the same hand?

## Central payoff

A thermometer reports the object's temperature; the skin initially reports a changing contact. Metal usually feels colder because it removes heat from the skin faster, while wood's surface warms locally and slows the exchange. The felt result belongs to the hand–material encounter, not to temperature alone.
