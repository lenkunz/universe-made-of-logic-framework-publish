# NotebookLM Audio Overview Prompt

Use the loaded sources, especially `02-notebooklm-source.md`, as the factual and semantic guide for this episode.

## Episode shape

Open with:

> **“If a metal spoon and a wooden spoon have been sitting in the same room all day, why does the metal still feel colder?”**

Build the ordinary physics first. Make the first distinction explicit:

- a thermometer can report the same object temperature;
- touch begins a heat-transfer process;
- the skin initially responds to its own changing thermal state.

Explain why metal usually draws heat from a warmer hand more quickly while the wood surface warms locally. Introduce thermal conductivity, then sharpen the explanation with **thermal effusivity** `√(kρc_p)` as the useful contact quantity. Treat the ideal interface-temperature equation as a model, not a complete finger.

Use the cold-wood experiment as the key twist: when cooled wood produced a signal similar to ambient metal, human participants misidentified it as metal in 93.8% of those trials. Then reverse the direction: above skin temperature, the same metal can feel hotter than wood.

Only afterward introduce My GUT Deduction's narrow lens:

> **Appearance belongs to the relation between structure and context.**

and:

> **Stage is where relation meets an anchoring domain.**

The interpretive payoff is that felt coldness can surface from the hand–object encounter rather than residing inside the object as a permanent property.

## Host behavior

- Keep established heat-transfer science separate from the framework interpretation.
- Explain why conductivity alone is incomplete without burying the listener in symbols.
- Mention contact area, pressure, roughness, moisture, duration, finite thickness, and skin temperature as caveats.
- Challenge the interpretation once: **“Is the framework predicting thermal effusivity or proving temperature is unreal?”** Answer no.
- Make clear that temperature remains a real, measurable quantity; the sensation answers a different relational question.
- Do not turn the episode into a framework lecture.

## Episode-specific focus

Opening hook: Same-temperature metal and wood feel different.

Main example: Metal spoon versus wooden spoon at room temperature.

Most important conceptual turn: The skin reports a changing contact, not a context-free label carried by the object.

Useful skeptical / testing question: Can material identity be confused by reproducing the same thermal signal? Yes—the cold-wood experiment shows that it can.

Closing takeaway:

> **“When two objects have the same temperature but feel different, are you sensing what the object is—or what the encounter is doing to you?”**

## Things to avoid

- Do not say metal is actually colder in the opening example.
- Do not say touch directly measures absolute object temperature.
- Do not claim thermal conductivity is the only relevant property.
- Do not equate framework budget with heat, joules, temperature, conductivity, effusivity, heat capacity, or neural firing.
- Do not imply the framework derives the heat equation, thermoreceptors, or a contact-temperature curve.
- Do not call ordinary thermal physics proof of My GUT Deduction.
- Do not recommend touching unknown hot surfaces.
