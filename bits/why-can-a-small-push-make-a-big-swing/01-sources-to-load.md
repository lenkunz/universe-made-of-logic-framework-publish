## External sources

- [ ] OpenStax, University Physics Volume 1, §15.6 “Forced Oscillations” — https://openstax.org/books/university-physics-volume-1/pages/15-6-forced-oscillations
  - Why NotebookLM needs it: accessible standard account of natural frequency, periodic driving, resonance, damping, resonance curves, and Q.
  - Established explanation: a periodic driver transfers energy to an oscillator; response becomes large near resonance, with damping limiting and broadening the response.

- [ ] MIT OpenCourseWare, Physics III, Chapter 2 “Forced Oscillation and Resonance” — https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/8-03sc-physics-iii-vibrations-and-waves-fall-2016/782069da3820fc514c10c26ae0c15b01_MIT8_03SCF16_Text_Ch2.pdf
  - Why NotebookLM needs it: equation-level distinction between driving frequency, natural frequency, damping, amplitude, and phase.
  - Established explanation: the external driving frequency is independent of the oscillator's natural frequency; the steady response depends on their relation and on damping.

- [ ] MIT OpenCourseWare, Introduction to Oscillations and Waves, Lecture 5 “Driven Oscillations” — https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/res-8-009-introduction-to-oscillations-and-waves-summer-2017/mitres_8_009su17_lec5.pdf
  - Why NotebookLM needs it: useful boundary for ideal pure resonance versus real dissipative/nonlinear systems.
  - Established explanation: ideal undamped resonance can produce growing amplitude, but real large-amplitude behavior requires damping and/or nonlinear effects.
