## Episode-specific article focus

Title: **Why Can a Small Push Make a Big Swing?**

Lead with the playground swing. Let the reader first discover why repeated modest pushes can outperform badly timed pushes. Explain natural frequency, driving, resonance, phase-sensitive energy transfer, and damping in ordinary language before introducing framework vocabulary.

The conceptual hinge is: **input size alone does not determine consequence; each input meets the state produced by the path before it.**

Only later introduce the framework connection: present topology is ground, and path is memory. Treat resonance as a candidate structural correspondence, not a framework derivation. State plainly that ordinary oscillator mechanics already predicts the phenomenon.

Avoid Tacoma Narrows as the main example because the collapse is often misleadingly reduced to simple resonance; aeroelastic flutter is the important caveat.

## Google Flow / image concept

Create a **16:9 editorial landscape** image of a real playground swing in motion at late afternoon. Show one adult hand giving a small, precisely timed push to the back of the swing seat as it passes through a useful point in its arc. Use a restrained sequence of two or three translucent motion echoes of the swing along the same arc to suggest accumulated motion from repeated timing, while keeping the hand and current swing physically crisp. The visual tension is **small repeated input, large accumulated motion because the pushes meet the motion at the right time**.

Place the swing and hand center-right with generous quiet sky or park negative space on the left for title placement. Ground the scene in editorial photography: believable chains, seat, body mechanics, gravity, and natural light.

Avoid arrows, sine-wave overlays, equations, clocks, glowing energy, sci-fi rings, cosmic imagery, infographic styling, baked-in text, giant impact effects, or anything implying free energy. Do not show a bridge collapse.
