Title: Why Do Onions Make You Cry?
Slug: why-do-onions-make-you-cry

Subtitle: *The onion does not simply contain a bottle of tear gas. Cutting it creates the encounter that makes the irritant.*

## Central mystery

An intact onion can sit on a counter without making anyone cry. Slice it and, seconds later, nearby eyes may sting and water. What changed?

The useful surprise is that chopping does more than release a pre-made irritant. It damages cellular compartments and lets previously separated chemistry interact. The tear-producing molecule is then generated through a reaction pathway.

## Ordinary external explanation / observations

Onions store sulfur-containing flavor precursors in their tissues. Tissue disruption brings these precursors into contact with enzymes that had been spatially separated from them. Alliinase acts on cysteine-sulfoxide precursors, producing reactive sulfenic-acid intermediates. Onion lachrymatory factor synthase (LFS) then catalyzes formation of the volatile lachrymatory factor, commonly described as (Z)- or syn-propanethial S-oxide. ACS and Nature sources establish this pathway and the specific role of LFS.

Because the lachrymatory factor is volatile, some escapes from the cut tissue and reaches the moist surface of the eyes. It irritates the eye and stimulates tearing, a protective response that helps dilute and remove the irritant.

The key correction is therefore:

> **The onion does not merely release a finished tear chemical that was waiting unchanged inside it. Damage changes which molecules and enzymes can meet, and the new contact launches chemistry that produces the volatile irritant.**

This also explains why “tearless onion” experiments are scientifically useful. LFS-suppressed onion lines produce far less lachrymatory factor after tissue disruption. The intervention changes the pathway, not merely the ability of a finished irritant to escape.

The pathway also branches. Suppressing LFS can redirect reactive sulfur chemistry toward other thiosulfinate products. The cut therefore opens a reaction network; it does not uncover one inevitable molecule hidden in advance.

## What remains conceptually interesting

Before the cut, the ingredients already existed, but the consequential relation did not. Cellular organization kept relevant reactants and enzymes apart. Cutting destroys part of that boundary. The chemistry that becomes possible afterward depends on a new arrangement of contact.

That makes the onion a tiny lesson in why an outcome cannot always be assigned to one participant in isolation.

The knife does not contain tears. The onion precursor does not by itself contain the completed tear response. The enzyme does not make eyes water while safely compartmentalized. The eye does not cry until an irritant reaches it.

A chain of newly available relations turns intact plant tissue into a human tear response.

The current framework keeps the Cut and relation tightly connected. At its deepest bookkeeping level, once A and B are referable, a consequential relation can be written:

`A + B → A + B + R_AB`

The onion provides a restrained physical analogy for a related idea:

> **A new relation can make a consequence available that was not a property of either side considered alone.**

The plant already contains precursors and enzymes, but compartmentalization constrains which relations can occur. Mechanical cutting changes those boundaries. New chemical relations become possible, and their products can participate in still further relations: volatile transport through air, contact with the eye, sensory irritation, and tearing.

This is especially useful because the framework does not need to pretend that relation means “mysterious extra substance.” In the onion, the ordinary mechanism is completely physical. The analogy concerns structure: what can happen changes when the available relations change.

A second narrow connection is that a boundary can be consequential by controlling routes. An intact cell is not merely a visible outline. Its compartmental organization helps determine which molecules can meet. Breaking that organization changes the reaction field.

## Examples and research notes

### LFS was a real correction to the older simple story

The 2002 Nature paper showed that the lachrymatory factor is not merely a spontaneous by-product following alliinase action. A specific enzyme, lachrymatory-factor synthase, produces it. That makes this episode stronger: the outcome depends on an organized pathway.

### Tearless onions are an intervention, not just an anecdote

A 2016 Scientific Reports study characterized non-transgenic LFS-reduced onion lines. Their lachrymatory-factor levels were dramatically reduced relative to normal onion. Earlier LFS-silencing work also redirected sulfur products toward thiosulfinates. Changing one pathway relation changes the downstream chemistry.

### Keep the eye explanation modest

For a general audience, it is enough to say the volatile lachrymatory factor reaches and irritates the eye, stimulating tearing. Do not over-specify receptor pathways unless a strong source is loaded for that exact claim.
