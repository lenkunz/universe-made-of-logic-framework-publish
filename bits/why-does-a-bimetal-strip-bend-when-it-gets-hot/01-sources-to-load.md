## External sources

- [ ] NIST/NBS, *Calibration of temperature measurement systems installed in buildings*, section 4.7 Bimetallic Thermometers: https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/Legacy/BSS/nbsbuildingscience153.pdf
  - Why NotebookLM needs it: direct description of two firmly bonded metals with different thermal-expansion coefficients deforming under temperature change and use in switches/thermometers.
  - Established observation / explanation this source supports: differential expansion of bonded layers produces deformation; bimetal devices can convert temperature change into switching or indication.

- [ ] NIST/NBS Circular 486, *Thermal Expansion of Solids*: https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/Legacy/circ/nbscircular486.pdf
  - Why NotebookLM needs it: technical treatment of thermostat metals, including curvature and dependence on expansion coefficients, elastic moduli, layer thicknesses, and temperature change.
  - Established observation / explanation this source supports: heating a straight bimetal strip bends it with the lower-expansion material on the inner side; curvature depends on the coupled properties of both layers.

- [ ] NIST, Chiang, Chiang & Wu (2002), *A Technique for Deducing In-Plane Modulus and Coefficient of Thermal Expansion of a Supported Thin Film*: https://www.nist.gov/publications/technique-deducing-plane-modulus-and-coefficient-thermal-expansion-supported-thin-film
  - Why NotebookLM needs it: modern example of curvature arising from coupled thermal/mechanical properties in bonded film-substrate systems.
  - Established observation / explanation this source supports: curvature can encode mismatch between thermal expansion and elastic response of joined materials.
