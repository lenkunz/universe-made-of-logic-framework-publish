# NotebookLM Audio Overview Prompt

Use the loaded sources, especially `02-notebooklm-source.md`, as the factual and semantic guide for this episode.

Open with: **“If heating one metal strip mostly makes it longer, why can heating two metals bonded together make the pair curve?”**

Build the ordinary science first. Contrast two loose strips with two firmly bonded layers. Explain different coefficients of thermal expansion, then the compatibility constraint: the high-expansion layer wants to become longer, the low-expansion layer wants less change, but the bond prevents independent lengths. The mismatch becomes internal stress and curvature, with the higher-expansion material toward the outside of the heated bend. Briefly connect this to bimetal thermometers and thermostatic switches.

Make the conceptual turn: **the curve is not a property either free strip displays on its own; it is a response of the joined composite to a difference the bond makes consequential.** Mention that curvature also depends on elastic moduli, thicknesses, geometry, temperature range, and real-device details.

Only afterward introduce My GUT Deduction's narrow lens: current Basics III treats a seated relation as consequential structure and says **“The whole can act as one while the relations making it one keep acting.”** Treat the bimetal strip only as a candidate structural correspondence for a new capability belonging to a constrained composite.

Ask skeptically: **“Is the framework deriving thermal expansion or calculating the strip's curvature?”** Answer no. Standard thermoelasticity explains the effect. The framework has not derived expansion coefficients, elastic modulus, stress, strain, curvature, hysteresis, or switching temperature.

Do not say heat inherently bends metal, all metals expand equally, the bond creates energy, or a metallurgical bond is literally framework `R_AB`. Do not equate framework budget with heat, temperature, stress, strain, curvature, expansion coefficient, or energy.

Close with: **“If neither free strip bends this way, where does the curve belong: to either material, or to the constraint that makes two different expansions share one shape?”**
