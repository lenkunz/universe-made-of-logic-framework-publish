## Central question

Special relativity says a ruler moving along its own length is measured shorter by the same factor that appears in relativistic clock-rate comparisons.

Is that a second independent rule, or can the framework read both effects as two surfaced measurements of the same bounded projection geometry?

## Central payoff

> **The clock and the ruler may be two readings of the same projection ratio.**

The inherited simple projection candidate is:

`q = √(1-v²/c²)`

and the ruler-side candidate is:

`L = qL_0`

matching the standard longitudinal length-contraction factor at the level of the measured ratio.

## Standard-science boundary

Established special relativity gives:

`L = L_0√(1-v²/c²)`

for the component parallel to relative motion, with endpoint positions assigned to the same observer-frame simultaneity slice.

A photograph is not automatically such a measurement because finite light-travel-time effects can alter visual appearance.

Do not claim that:

- a camera or retina receives literal universal frames;
