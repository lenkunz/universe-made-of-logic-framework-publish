# NotebookLM Audio Overview Prompt

Use the loaded sources, especially `02-notebooklm-source.md`, as the factual and semantic guide.

Open with: **“How can two people agree on exactly what happened and still disagree about what the evidence means?”**

Start with ordinary reasoning, preferably a medical-test example. Explain the difference between observation, hypothesis, likelihood, prior, and posterior without turning the episode into a math lecture. Emphasize the key comparative question: *would this observation also have been easy to get if the explanation were wrong?* Use the likelihood-ratio idea to show why “my theory can explain this” is weaker than “my theory expected this much more than its alternatives did.”

Then discuss chronology and post-hoc flexibility: a specific expectation recorded before an event constrains interpretation differently from a flexible story assembled afterward.

Only after that introduce My GUT Deduction’s methodology: **“Evidence is observation becoming constraint on the available interpretations.”** Explain `C/S/I/R/F` qualitatively as a record of chronology, specificity, independence, recurrence, and interpretive freedom. Make clear that it is not a scalar score and not Bayesian statistics.

Useful skeptical question: **“Is this framework profile just Bayes’ theorem with different labels?”** Answer no. Bayes supplies quantitative conditional-probability relations when models and probabilities are specified; the framework profile is qualitative ontology-level bookkeeping and does not calculate a posterior.

Preserve the guardrail that correspondence does not establish intention, a sender, a hidden mechanism, or a preferred causal story merely because the framework permits it.

Avoid treating priors as arbitrary excuses, confusing `P(E|H)` with `P(H|E)`, claiming all interpretations are equally valid, or presenting the framework as a replacement for statistics.

Close with: **“When two people see the same fact and reach different conclusions, are they really disagreeing about the fact, or about the world in which that fact would be surprising?”**
