## External sources

- [ ] American Chemical Society, *The Science of Getting Things Clean* (2014): https://www.acs.org/content/dam/acsorg/education/outreach/celebrating-chemistry/2014-cced-celebrating-chemistry-english.pdf
  - Why NotebookLM needs it: accessible chemistry of surfactants with hydrophilic and hydrophobic portions and their role in carrying oil into wash water.
  - Established observation / explanation: soaps and detergents are surfactants; amphiphilic structure enables interaction with water and nonpolar oils.

- [ ] Poce-Fatou, *A Superficial Overview of Detergency*, Journal of Chemical Education 83 (2006): https://doi.org/10.1021/ed083p1147
  - Why NotebookLM needs it: detergency is broader than the cartoon of a single spherical micelle; wetting, adhesion, emulsions, micelles and mechanical washing all matter.
  - Established observation / explanation: cleaning is an interfacial/surface-chemistry process involving surfactants and multiple mechanisms.

- [ ] Saha et al., *Surfactant Adsorption to Different Fluid Interfaces*, Langmuir 37 (2021): https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.langmuir.1c00668
  - Why NotebookLM needs it: modern experimental grounding for surfactant adsorption and reduction of oil-water interfacial tension.
  - Established observation / explanation: surfactant adsorption changes fluid interfaces; behavior depends on the hydrophobic phase and oil polarity.

- [ ] Chemistry LibreTexts, *Soap (Exemplar)*: https://chem.libretexts.org/Ancillary_Materials/Exemplars_and_Case_Studies/Exemplars/Sports_Physiology_and_Health/Soap_%28Exemplar%29
  - Why NotebookLM needs it: simple oil-water-soap explanation and hard-water caveat.
  - Established observation / explanation: nonpolar tails associate with oil while polar/ionic heads remain compatible with water; dispersed oily droplets can then be rinsed away.
