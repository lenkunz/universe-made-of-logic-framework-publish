## Episode-specific article focus

Title: **Why Can Soap Make Oil Follow Water?**

Subtitle: *One molecule can face two incompatible worlds at once.*

Begin with the greasy-pan puzzle, not framework language. Give enough surface chemistry to make the ordinary answer satisfying: oil/water immiscibility, amphiphilic surfactants, interface adsorption, interfacial tension, wetting, dispersion/emulsification and micelles. Avoid the common simplification that cleaning is nothing but spherical micelles swallowing grease.

The conceptual pivot should be: **soap does not erase the difference between oil and water; an added participant reorganizes their interface and changes what the pair can do together.**

Bring Basics III in only after that: a relation can become an additional consequential participant and can make further action/properties available. Mark this as a candidate structural correspondence. Standard chemistry remains the explanation of detergency.

Do not equate surfactants with framework relations or budget with energy, concentration, interfacial tension, surface free energy or chemical potential.

## Google Flow / image focus

Create a 16:9 editorial landscape macro image of a dark glossy ribbon of cooking oil on a ceramic plate meeting clear rinse water. At the boundary, a thin pearlescent soap film breaks the oil into a small procession of rounded droplets that are visibly being carried into the moving water. Keep the transformation physically grounded and photographic rather than diagrammatic. The focal idea is **a third participant changes the boundary so two reluctant materials can travel together without becoming the same material**.

Place the oil-water interaction on the right or center-right and leave generous clean negative space on the left for title placement. Use tactile kitchen realism, crisp surface reflections and subtle fluid motion.

Avoid molecule diagrams, head-and-tail icons, arrows, labels, equations, glowing chemistry, giant soap bubbles, foam-as-cleaning clichés, advertising aesthetics, sci-fi imagery, galaxies, brains, and baked-in title text.
