# NotebookLM Audio Overview Prompt

Use the loaded sources, especially `02-notebooklm-source.md`, as the factual and semantic guide.

Open with: **“Why does greasy water slide off a pan, but a little soap suddenly lets the rinse carry the grease away?”**

Start with ordinary chemistry. Explain why oil and water separate, then introduce surfactants as amphiphilic molecules with water-compatible heads and oil-compatible tails. Emphasize adsorption at the oil-water interface, reduced interfacial tension, wetting and dispersion. Explain micelles as one important part of the story, but do not reduce all cleaning to perfect spherical micelles swallowing grease.

Use the greasy pan as the main example and hard water as one short caveat. The key conceptual turn is: **soap does not make oil become water; a third participant changes what the oil-water pair can do together.**

Only then introduce My GUT Deduction’s narrow Basics III lens: a seated relation can become a consequential participant rather than mere commentary about two finished objects. Treat surfactant chemistry as an analogy/candidate correspondence, not evidence for the ontology.

Ask skeptically: **“Is the framework explaining why surfactants lower interfacial tension?”** Answer no. Standard chemistry explains the molecular mechanism; the framework has not derived surface free energy, hydrophobic interactions, critical micelle concentration, micelle geometry or detergency equations.

Do not equate computational budget with energy, concentration, interfacial tension or chemical potential. Do not say foam causes cleaning, every aggregate is spherical, or oil and water become the same substance.

Close with: **“If adding a relation can change what two things are able to do together without changing what either one is, how much of a system’s behavior belongs to its parts, and how much belongs to what has been built between them?”**
