## External sources

- [ ] Engineering Statics, “Trusses” — https://engineeringstatics.org/Chapter_06-trusses.html
  - Why NotebookLM needs it: accessible statics treatment of simple trusses, triangular construction, rigidity, unstable mechanisms, and tension/compression in members.
  - Established observation / explanation this source supports: ideal pin-jointed simple trusses use triangular geometry because triangles are rigid under fixed member lengths; insufficiently braced arrangements can remain mechanisms.

- [ ] Engineering Statics, “Frames and Machines” — https://engineeringstatics.org/frames-and-machines.html
  - Why NotebookLM needs it: clean distinction between independently rigid frames and mechanisms, including the practical use of triangles to identify rigidity.
  - Established observation / explanation this source supports: a structure can be made from individually rigid members yet still move internally if its connection pattern leaves a degree of freedom.

- [ ] MIT OpenCourseWare, “Lecture 11: Rigidity Theory” — https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-849-geometric-folding-algorithms-linkages-origami-polyhedra-fall-2012/resources/lecture-11-rigidity-theory/
  - Why NotebookLM needs it: places the simple square/triangle intuition in the broader mathematical subject of linkages, degrees of freedom, generic rigidity, and minimally rigid graphs.
  - Established observation / explanation this source supports: rigidity is a property of the constraint graph and geometry, not merely of the material pieces.

- [ ] Engineering Statics, “Interactions between members” — https://engineeringstatics.org/Chapter_06-interactions-between-members.html
  - Why NotebookLM needs it: explains how connected structural members transmit forces through joints.
  - Established observation / explanation this source supports: when the braced frame carries load, member interactions redistribute it through tension/compression rather than the diagonal acting as a magical stiffener in isolation.
