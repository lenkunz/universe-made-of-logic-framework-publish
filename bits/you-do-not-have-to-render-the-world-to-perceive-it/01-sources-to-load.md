## External sources

- [ ] Ronald A. Rensink, J. Kevin O'Regan, and James J. Clark, **To See or Not to See: The Need for Attention to Perceive Changes in Scenes** (Psychological Science, 1997): https://www2.psych.ubc.ca/~rensink/publications/download/PS97.pdf
  - Why NotebookLM needs it: original flicker/change-blindness experiments showing that substantial scene changes can be difficult to detect when the transient that would draw attention is interrupted.
  - Established observation / explanation this source supports: attention is important for detecting changes under the tested conditions. It does not prove that the brain stores no visual detail.
- [ ] Daniel J. Simons and Christopher F. Chabris, **Gorillas in Our Midst: Sustained Inattentional Blindness for Dynamic Events** (Perception, 1999): https://doi.org/10.1068/p281059
  - Why NotebookLM needs it: original dynamic inattentional-blindness study using a demanding counting task and an unexpected visible event.
  - Established observation / explanation this source supports: people can fail to report a conspicuous unexpected event while attention is engaged elsewhere. It does not show that the event was physically absent.
- [ ] Dana H. Ballard, Mary M. Hayhoe, Polly K. Pook, and Rajesh P. N. Rao, **Deictic Codes for the Embodiment of Cognition** (Behavioral and Brain Sciences, 1997): https://doi.org/10.1017/S0140525X97001611
  - Why NotebookLM needs it: primary theoretical/research account of eye and hand movements, task structure, and temporary deictic bindings connecting external sensory data to cognitive programs and motor action.
  - Established observation / explanation this source supports: natural-task cognition can use body orientation and the environment as part of the working arrangement rather than requiring one exhaustive internal scene description.
- [ ] J. Kevin O'Regan and Alva Noë, **A Sensorimotor Account of Vision and Visual Consciousness** (Behavioral and Brain Sciences, 2001): https://doi.org/10.1017/S0140525X01000115
  - Why NotebookLM needs it: the most direct theoretical comparison to “the world as outside memory” and seeing as skilled sensorimotor activity.
  - Status: influential theoretical proposal with extensive commentary, not settled proof that all visual representation is absent or that this framework is correct.

## Source discipline

Change blindness and inattentional blindness constrain claims about complete, uniformly accessible visual representation; they do not prove that the brain contains no detailed representations. Sensorimotor and deictic accounts are relevant comparisons, not empirical confirmation of the ontology. Do not equate GPU heat, watts, FLOPS, neural activity, or physical energy with framework budget.
