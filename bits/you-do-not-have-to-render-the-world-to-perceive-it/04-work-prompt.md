## Article direction

Use the title **You Do Not Have to Render the World to Perceive It** and the subtitle direction *Seeing is not the same job as drawing every pixel.* Aim for roughly 1,600–2,000 words.

Open with the GPU/display contrast and the tempting inner-screen metaphor. The GPU comparison is functional, not a benchmark. Do not quote wattage or imply comparable workloads unless new sources and careful boundaries are added.

Give the ordinary research first:

1. change blindness and the need for attention to detect changes under the tested flicker conditions;
2. inattentional blindness during a demanding pass-counting task;
3. deictic codes and the role of eye/hand/body orientation in natural tasks;
4. the sensorimotor “outside memory” account as a theory, not consensus.

State the provisional ordinary answer before the framework: visual experience does not by itself prove a uniformly detailed inner raster; perception can be selective, active, task-directed, and supported by repeated access to the world.

Then introduce only the necessary framework terms: perception as domain-relative Stage resolution, coarse as exact about consequential distinctions, and encounters that need not expose their whole supporting network.

Preserve the stronger exploratory proposal: brain/body activity may be the coupled Stage expression/interface of perceptual-domain resolution, not a separate ontological origin that first calculates a full internal world. Do not weaken it to “the brain skips pixels,” but do not present it as established neuroscience. Name the missing experimental discriminator.

Keep physical heat separate from framework budget. Do not call the brain, consciousness, Earth, or the environment a literal quantum computer.

## Image

Create a 16:9 editorial conceptual image split by one coherent scene rather than an infographic. On one side, a monitor visibly resolves a dense mosaic of pixels. On the other, a human observer actively turns their eyes and reaches toward one meaningful object in a rich room while peripheral detail remains naturally present but visually de-emphasized. The image should suggest selective relation and active sampling, not blindness or a low-resolution world.

Leave negative space for title placement. Avoid labels, arrows, equations, glowing brains, literal tiny screens inside heads, cyberpunk circuitry, quantum symbols, or misleading heat imagery.

Suggested visible caption:

> **Image Caption:** A display must emit the pixels it shows. Perception can return to the world and resolve the detail the present task makes consequential.
