## Central question

A graphics system may have to calculate a complete image for a display. Does perception require the brain or consciousness to maintain an equally complete inner picture of everything in view?

## Central payoff

> **Perception can use the world, the body, attention, and retained structure to resolve what the present task needs without proving that a pixel-complete inner screen exists.**

Change blindness, inattentional blindness, and research on embodied/deictic task control make the ordinary question serious. The framework then proposes a stronger reading: a domain resolves consequential distinctions on Stage rather than rendering a full duplicate world for a separate inner observer.

## Framework status

- External-science-led topic: attention strongly affects what changes and unexpected events people report; eye and hand movements can participate in natural task control; sensorimotor accounts argue against a complete inner reconstruction, but that theoretical interpretation is not the only account of vision.
- Framework-native ground: perception is domain-relative Stage resolution; coarse does not mean blurry; the current encounter need not expose or render its whole supporting network.
- Exploratory proposal: brain/body activity is the coupled physical expression or interface of perceptual-domain resolution rather than a separate ontological origin that first computes a full world model.
- Not established: consciousness without neural activity, a brain that performs no computation in ordinary scientific language, a pixel-free theory of every visual representation, or a quantum-computing explanation of perception.
