# NotebookLM Audio Overview Prompt

Create an English Bits episode titled **You Do Not Have to Render the World to Perceive It**. Use the detailed dossier as the factual and status guide. Keep ordinary perception research in the foreground and introduce the framework only after the visual puzzle is clear.

Open with a graphics processor producing a frame for a display. The display needs enough information to show the distinctions on its surface. Then ask whether visual experience requires the brain to draw a second, equally complete screen inside the head—and who would watch it.

Explain change blindness using Rensink, O'Regan, and Clark's flicker experiments: interruptions can make substantial scene changes difficult to detect when the normal local transient does not direct attention. Explain Simons and Chabris's inattentional-blindness task: many observers engaged in counting passes failed to report a conspicuous unexpected event. Do not turn either result into “the brain represents nothing.”

Then use Ballard and colleagues' deictic-code account. Eye and hand movements, body orientation, external objects, and task structure can help organize what is needed next. Introduce O'Regan and Noë's “world as outside memory”/sensorimotor account as a relevant theory, not settled consensus.

State the ordinary-language result before framework terms:

> **Rich visual experience does not by itself prove a pixel-complete inner screen. Perception is selective, active, and supported by body, attention, memory, task, and repeated access to the world.**

Only then introduce the framework's broad definition of perception as domain-relative Stage resolution. Ask which distinctions the current encounter makes consequential and at what grain they can resolve. Coarse does not mean blurry; a sharp task-level relation can leave other details unresolved.

Preserve Len's stronger proposal without weakening or promoting it: perceptual-domain relation projects, the supported continuation resolves on Stage, and neural/body activity may be the coupled physical expression or interface of that resolution rather than a separate ontological origin that first computes a full internal world. Explicitly say this is exploratory and currently lacks an experimental discriminator from conventional neural control accounts.

Treat the GPU comparison as a difference of tasks, not a watt/FLOPS contest. Do not equate heat, electrical power, neural metabolism, or computation with framework budget. Do not call the brain or Earth a literal quantum computer.

Ask one productive challenge: **What observation would differ if neural activity were the ontological origin of perception versus the coupled Stage expression of a wider relation?** The framework has not yet supplied that discriminator.

Close with: **The screen has to show pixels because pixels are its output. Seeing may be the work of staying in relation with a world that can be sampled again.**
