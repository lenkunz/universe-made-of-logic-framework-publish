## External sources

- [ ] APS Physics, “The Minimum Temperature for Levitating Droplets” (2021): https://physics.aps.org/articles/v14/s107
  - Why NotebookLM needs it: accessible overview of Leidenfrost levitation, onset/persistence hysteresis, and measured water vapor-layer stability.
  - Established observation / explanation: sufficiently hot surfaces can support a vapor layer beneath water; the layer can persist below its formation temperature.

- [ ] Burton et al., “Geometry of the Vapor Layer Under a Leidenfrost Drop,” Physical Review Letters 109, 074301 (2012): https://journals.aps.org/prl/abstract/10.1103/PhysRevLett.109.074301
  - Why NotebookLM needs it: direct measurements of the thin vapor cushion geometry beneath Leidenfrost drops.
  - Established observation / explanation: evaporating liquid continuously feeds a vapor pocket that levitates the drop.

- [ ] Shirota et al., “Dynamic Leidenfrost Effect: Relevant Time and Length Scales,” Physical Review Letters 116, 064501 (2016): https://journals.aps.org/prl/abstract/10.1103/PhysRevLett.116.064501
  - Why NotebookLM needs it: impact dynamics and the distinction between static and dynamic Leidenfrost conditions.
  - Established observation / explanation: impact speed matters to whether generated vapor prevents liquid-solid contact.

- [ ] Lee et al., “Transient dynamics in drop impact on a superheated surface,” Physical Review Fluids 3, 124308 (2018): https://journals.aps.org/prfluids/abstract/10.1103/PhysRevFluids.3.124308
  - Why NotebookLM needs it: ultrafast x-ray visualization of the transient vapor layer.
  - Established observation / explanation: vapor-layer geometry evolves during impact and affects vaporization.
