# NotebookLM Audio Overview Prompt

Use the loaded sources, especially `02-notebooklm-source.md`, as the factual and semantic guide for this episode.

Open with: **“Why can a water drop vanish furiously on a hot pan, then seem to survive longer and skate around when the pan gets even hotter?”**

Build the ordinary physics first. Explain the Leidenfrost effect: sufficiently intense evaporation can sustain a thin vapor cushion, reducing direct liquid-solid contact and changing the heat-transfer route. Make clear that the vapor layer is continuously fed by evaporation, is not perfect insulation, and does not mean the drop stops receiving heat.

Emphasize two research points: the vapor pocket has been directly measured, and onset is not the same as persistence. Use the water hysteresis experiment as an example, while making clear its temperatures are experiment-specific rather than universal kitchen thresholds. Briefly mention that impact speed and surface properties shift the conditions.

Then make the conceptual turn: **stronger forcing can create an intermediate layer that changes the route of the interaction itself.** Only here introduce My GUT Deduction's narrow lens: **interiority is routing before geometry; a boundary matters by constraining routes between sides.** Treat the Leidenfrost vapor layer as a candidate structural analogy, not a derivation.

Ask skeptically: **“Is the framework predicting when a droplet will levitate?”** Answer no. Standard thermodynamics and fluid mechanics explain Leidenfrost behavior. The framework has not derived boiling curves, Leidenfrost temperature, vapor-film thickness, heat flux, or droplet dynamics.

Do not equate computational budget with temperature, heat, energy, thermal conductivity, latent heat, or vapor pressure. Do not claim hotter always means slower evaporation, or that vapor completely blocks heat.

Close with: **“What if a boundary is not primarily a wall, but a change in which routes remain available between two sides?”**
