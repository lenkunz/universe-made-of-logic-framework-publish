## Episode-specific article focus

Title: **Why Do Water Drops Dance on a Hot Pan?**

Subtitle: *Sometimes making the surface hotter makes the water touch it less.*

Lead with the ordinary kitchen paradox, then explain film boiling and the Leidenfrost vapor cushion. Give the listener/reader enough conventional heat-transfer and droplet physics to understand why reduced direct contact can coexist with a hotter surface. Include onset-versus-persistence hysteresis as the second surprise, but keep experimental temperatures explicitly experiment-specific.

The conceptual pivot should be: **stronger forcing can create an intermediate layer that changes the route of interaction itself.** Only afterward bring in the framework's current statement that **interiority is routing before geometry**. The vapor layer is an analogy/candidate correspondence, not a derivation of the Leidenfrost effect.

Preserve these boundaries: the framework has not derived boiling curves, Leidenfrost temperature, vapor-film thickness, heat flux, capillary dynamics, or droplet motion. Budget is not heat, temperature, energy, latent heat, thermal conductivity, or vapor pressure.
