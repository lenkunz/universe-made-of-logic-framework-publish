## External sources

- [ ] Autumn et al. (2000), “Adhesive force of a single gecko foot-hair,” *Nature*.
  - URL: https://doi.org/10.1038/35015073
  - Why NotebookLM needs it: direct force measurements at the single-seta level and the classic attachment-angle result.
  - Established observation / explanation this source supports: gecko setae generate strong, controllable dry adhesion through their geometry and loading.

- [ ] Autumn et al. (2002), “Evidence for van der Waals adhesion in gecko setae,” *PNAS*.
  - URL: https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.192252799
  - Why NotebookLM needs it: direct experimental comparison of hydrophobic and hydrophilic polarizable surfaces and the geometry-based van der Waals account.
  - Established observation / explanation this source supports: intimate dry contact at spatular tips can generate substantial intermolecular adhesion without wet glue or suction.

- [ ] Tian et al. (2006), “Adhesion and friction in gecko toe attachment and detachment,” *PNAS*.
  - URL: https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.0608841103
  - Why NotebookLM needs it: directional attachment, inward gripping, toe rolling, and peeling mechanics.
  - Established observation / explanation this source supports: the same hierarchical contact can switch between high adhesion/friction and rapid release by changing pulling and peeling geometry.

- [ ] Izadi, Stewart & Penlidis (2014), “Role of contact electrification and electrostatic interactions in gecko adhesion,” *Journal of the Royal Society Interface*.
  - URL: https://doi.org/10.1098/rsif.2014.0371
  - Why NotebookLM needs it: a necessary caveat against presenting the microscopic mechanism as a settled single-force story for every material.
  - Established observation / explanation this source supports: contact electrification can materially contribute to gecko adhesion on tested surfaces.

## Source discipline

Keep external observations and established surface science separate from framework interpretation.

Do not turn “many small contacts act together” into proof of the framework. Do not claim one microscopic force explains every substrate or condition. Do not use the source list itself to promote a candidate correspondence into established science.
