# New framework-native deduction to preserve

Let:

- `R_p` = the fine persistence relation of the measured domain;
- `R_a` = the persistence relation supplied through / measured against the anchor relation;
- `R_ap` = the newly seated relation between those persistence structures.

Then:

`R_a + R_p`
→ `R_a + R_p + R_ap`

Once `R_ap` seats strongly enough, the composite can participate as one coarse persistence relation:

`R_all`

This final step is **coarsening / referability-as-one**, not arithmetic addition.

## Candidate projection rule

At the lowest measuring grain, treat `R_a` and `R_p` as independently consequential one-dimensional projections.

Because resolving one does not resolve the other, they require independent axes at that grain.

That gives the candidate composition:

`R_all² = R_a² + R_p²`

The square relation is therefore proposed as geometry generated by independent one-dimensional relations, not as a primitive relativity equation.

## Bounded-resolution reading

If each component tried to retain the full composite maximum independently, the vector demand would exceed the bounded `R_all`.

The seated relation therefore normalizes/contracts the available projections until they fit inside the same bounded composite relation.

For the physical mapping only, if the surfaced maximum is identified with measured `c`:

`R_all ↔ c`

and the anchor-relative projection is identified with measured `v`:

`R_a ↔ v`

then:

`R_p = √(R_all² - R_a²)`

and therefore:

`R_p / R_all = √(1 - v²/c²)`

This is the familiar inverse-Lorentz rate factor.

Do **not** say this already derives all of special relativity.

Do say:

> **The framework now has a native geometric reason for why a bounded total relation can force a familiar rate ratio once the physical mapping is made.**

## Reusable significance beyond relativity

The candidate rule may be useful whenever:

- one composite relation is bounded;
- several sub-relations are independently consequential at the same grain;
- each is represented through a one-dimensional projection;
- the composite must preserve all of them without resolving one as the other.

This may reduce future “formula debt” by allowing some ratios to be derived from projection geometry after the relational structure is established.

Do not claim every physical formula will reduce this way.
