# New framework-native deduction to preserve

Let:

- `R_p` = the fine persistence relation of the measured domain;
- `R_a` = the persistence relation supplied through / measured against the anchor relation;
- `R_ap` = the newly seated relation between those persistence structures.

Then:

`R_a + R_p`
→ `R_a + R_p + R_ap`

Once `R_ap` seats strongly enough, the composite can participate as one coarse persistence relation:

`R_all`

This final step is **coarsening / referability-as-one**, not arithmetic addition.

## Simplest low-bias projection case

Let:

- `R_p` = the fine persistence relation of the measured domain;
- `R_a` = the anchor-relative persistence relation;
- `R_ap` = the consequential relation between those persistence structures;
- `R_all` = the coarse composite persistence relation once the structure can participate as one.

Then:

`R_a + R_p`
→ `R_a + R_p + R_ap`
→ `[stable composite / coarsening]`
→ `R_all`

`R_all` is not arithmetic addition.

At the lowest measuring grain, suppose `R_a` and `R_p` are independently consequential one-dimensional projections.

If `R_ap` adds no further consequential bias between them at that grain, resolving one does not resolve the other and neither projection leans into the other.

The simplest representation is orthogonal:

`R_all² = R_a² + R_p²`

Under the physical correspondence:

`R_all ↔ c`

`R_a ↔ v`

therefore:

`R_p/R_all = √(1-v²/c²)`

This is the familiar inverse-Lorentz rate factor.

The important result to preserve is:

> **The framework approaches the same simple measured formula from its own persistence/projection ontology.**

## Contraction and shared persistence in the simple case

For positive independent components:

`max(R_a, R_p) < R_all < R_a + R_p`

The composite is larger than either persistence contribution alone but smaller than treating both as completely independent linear persistence costs.

This supports a useful **simple-case analogy**: once the relation seats, some persistence work is shared rather than paid twice, leaving reusable relational capacity for other unresolved freedom.

Do **not** define `R_ap` universally as the linear-sum gap.

`R_ap` is the relation itself.

## Biased case

When `R_ap` carries consequential bias:

- the angle between the projections becomes part of the state;
- orthogonality is no longer guaranteed;
- the same component magnitudes can yield a different composite because their relation differs;
- the clean right-triangle relation is no longer the whole description.

Do not force a general advanced formula into this Bit. The conceptual point is enough:

> **bias changes the geometry.**

## More relations and the compact-law boundary

With several independently consequential projections, the composite can carry more relational structure than the two-projection case.

If they remain simple and mutually independent, a compact higher-dimensional geometry may still be possible.

As pairwise biases multiply, the geometry becomes state-dependent. This is where the framework expects compact law-like description to begin giving way to numerical, statistical, or forecast-like description.

Connect this to `source/internal/short-notes-queue/part-05.md` and the candidate **When a Law Becomes a Forecast**.

The user's recurring “around four consequential variables or fewer” intuition is a working heuristic, not an established theorem.
