## Central question

When two clocks or physical processes accumulate different readings after different histories, what has actually been measured, and can the framework produce the familiar rate relation without making time primitive?

## Central payoff

> **The reading can be right while the ontology remains open to challenge.**

The Bit accepts relativistic measurements as measurements while separating the measured reading from the ontology used to interpret it.

## Framework status

Current framework ground used by the package:

- time is not primitive;
- a clock is a selected repeating persistence loop;
- seated relations can become coarse participants at the next grain;
- independently consequential distinctions can remain distinct while belonging to one relation;
- reality pays for consequential distinction rather than unnecessary fine detail.

The package also develops a **deduction candidate** for the simplest low-bias projection case:

`R_all² = R_a² + R_p²`

Under the candidate physical correspondence `R_all ↔ c` and `R_a ↔ v`, this gives the familiar inverse-Lorentz rate factor:

`R_p / R_all = √(1 - v²/c²)`

This same-formula result does **not** establish that full relativity has been derived. The physical mapping and extension to the rest of relativistic structure remain open.

`R_ap` is treated as the consequential relation determining how the projections participate together. The orthogonal form is only the simplest low-bias case, not a universal rule for all resolution.
