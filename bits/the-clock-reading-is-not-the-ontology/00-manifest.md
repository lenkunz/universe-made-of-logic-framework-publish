## Central question

When two clocks or physical processes accumulate different readings after different motion or gravitational histories, what has actually been measured, and how much of the usual language is measurement versus interpretation?

## Central payoff

> **The reading can be right while the ontology remains open to challenge.**

The Bit accepts the empirical success of relativistic clock, decay, and transformation measurements. It does not argue that the instruments are wrong.

The live framework question is narrower:

> Could the same measured structure eventually be derived from deeper non-temporal primitives such as resolution, projection, bounded propagation, and domain-relative process history, leaving spacetime as an effective representation rather than the primitive substrate?

## Framework status

- `Time is not primitive` — current framework ground.
- `A clock is a selected repeating persistence loop` — current framework ground.
- `Coordinate non-privilege` — structural correspondence, not a derivation of general relativity.
- `Gravitational clock differences as differently supported persistence loops` — Candidate Correspondence / physical mapping pending.
- `Lorentz structure from resolution/projection` — speculative interpretation / audit candidate, not yet derived.
- `Spacetime is not fundamental` — not established by the framework until the observed relativistic structure is reproduced from deeper primitives.

## Measurement / interpretation guardrail

The Bit must preserve this distinction:

`relativistic measurement`
≠
`framework refutation of relativity`

and:

`clock/process histories differ`
≠
`the experiment uniquely proves one primitive ontology`

The framework must reproduce the successful measured structure before it can claim a physically competitive reinterpretation.
