Open with: **“Why can ketchup sit in an upside-down bottle as if it were almost solid, then suddenly pour after you tap, shake, or squeeze it?”**

Build ordinary rheology first: contrast a simple Newtonian-fluid picture with ketchup's measured non-Newtonian shear thinning; explain that ketchup is a structured suspension/weak gel; distinguish yield-stress-like behavior from shear thinning, and mention that formulation, temperature and shear history matter. Use the bottle as the main example and paint or ceramic-printing paste as one brief extension.

Then make the conceptual turn: asking “how thick is ketchup?” is incomplete unless the interaction/flow conditions are specified. Only here introduce My GUT Deduction's narrow lens that a seated relation can become a consequential participant in what resolves next. Phrase the connection as an analogy about **state plus interaction**, not a derivation of rheology.

Useful skeptical question: **“Is the framework predicting ketchup's flow curve?”** Answer no. Standard rheology explains it; the framework has not derived viscosity, yield stress, shear thinning, or constitutive equations.

Do not equate computational budget with stress, energy or viscosity. Do not merge shear thinning, yield stress and thixotropy into one mechanism. Do not imply every ketchup formulation behaves identically.

Close with: **“When a property changes with the way we interact with a thing, where does that property really live: in the thing alone, or in the relation between its structure and what is happening to it?”**
