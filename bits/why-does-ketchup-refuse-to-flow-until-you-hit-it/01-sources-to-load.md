## External sources

- [ ] NIST, “The Science Behind Ketchup Pouring Could Lead to Ceramic 3D Printing” — https://www.nist.gov/blogs/taking-measure/science-behind-ketchup-pouring-could-lead-ceramic-3d-printing
  - Why NotebookLM needs it: accessible authoritative introduction to the upside-down-bottle puzzle and force-dependent flow.
  - Supports: ketchup is non-Newtonian and becomes easier to flow under forcing/shear.
- [ ] Kumbár, Ondrušíková & Nedomová (2019), “Rheological properties of tomato ketchup,” DOI 10.5219/1161 — https://potravinarstvo.com/journal1/index.php/potravinarstvo/article/view/1161
  - Why NotebookLM needs it: measured commercial/homemade ketchup flow curves.
  - Supports: pseudoplastic/shear-thinning behavior and power-law description.
- [ ] Juszczak et al., “Effect of Modified Starches on Rheological Properties of Ketchup,” Food and Bioprocess Technology — https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11947-012-0813-x
  - Why NotebookLM needs it: microstructure and formulation-sensitive rheology.
  - Supports: shear thinning, tendency to yield stress, weak-gel/viscoelastic behavior, particle and starch contributions.
- [ ] Koocheki et al. (2009), “The rheological properties of ketchup as a function of different hydrocolloids and temperature,” DOI 10.1111/j.1365-2621.2008.01868.x — https://ifst.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/j.1365-2621.2008.01868.x
  - Why NotebookLM needs it: formulation and temperature dependence.
  - Supports: non-Newtonian pseudoplastic behavior across tested formulations; hydrocolloids alter apparent viscosity/yield point.
