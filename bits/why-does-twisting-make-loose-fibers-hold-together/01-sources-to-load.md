## External sources

- [ ] Seguin & Crassous, “Twist-Controlled Force Amplification and Spinning Tension Transition in Yarn,” Physical Review Letters 128, 078002 (2022). https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.128.078002
  - Why NotebookLM needs it: primary peer-reviewed study combining experiments, simulations, and theory for the transition from loose fibers to load-bearing yarn.
  - Established observation / explanation this source supports: twist strongly amplifies frictional binding; strength rises sharply with a dimensionless combination of twist, friction, and geometry; above a critical regime, fibers break before the yarn slips apart.

- [ ] APS Physics, “How Cotton Fibers Become Yarn” (2022). https://physics.aps.org/articles/v15/27
  - Why NotebookLM needs it: accessible explanation of the PRL experiment and the historical puzzle of making long yarn from short fibers.
  - Established observation / explanation this source supports: overlapping short fibers can transmit load through twist-enhanced friction, with an optimum rather than unlimited benefit from additional twist.

- [ ] Nature Reviews Physics, “Spinning a strong yarn” (2022). https://doi.org/10.1038/s42254-022-00437-3
  - Why NotebookLM needs it: independent research highlight summarizing the force-transmission problem and the role of friction, twist, fiber length, and radius.
  - Established observation / explanation this source supports: yarn strength is an emergent mechanical property of the twisted assembly and can be captured by a dimensionless twist/friction/geometric parameter.

- [ ] “A Review of Friction in Low-Stress Mechanics of Fibrous Flexible Materials” (2024). https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11313532/
  - Why NotebookLM needs it: broader textile-mechanics context and the important caveat that staple yarn has an optimal twist rather than “more twist is always stronger.”
  - Established observation / explanation this source supports: twist, fiber migration, and inter-fiber friction create self-locking and tension transmission; excessive twist can reduce tensile performance.
