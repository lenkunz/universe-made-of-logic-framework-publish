# Audio Overview Prompt — Why Does Heat Draw a Curve?

Create a curious, natural English conversation between two hosts. Aim for roughly 12–16 minutes, with enough room to make one distinction clear. Follow the Bits production guide and use `02-notebooklm-source.md` as the episode's semantic centre.

Open with a heating element and a thermal camera: radiation is present before visible glowing, and measuring it across frequency gives a curve. Ask why there is a peak instead of every range contributing equally. Establish the ideal blackbody case and acknowledge real material response.

Give the ordinary thermal explanation first. Explain available modes and thermal occupation in plain words. State clearly that physics already has a quantitative account. Do not invent a mystery that Planck's law fails to answer.

By the middle, give a provisional answer: different ranges allow different total contributions, and the curve records that changing balance. Then introduce the framework's receiver/target sweep as a candidate deeper description of the same measurements.

Spend the most time on this distinction:

> At one selected grain comparison, repeat contributions may have the same local ratio. Move the grain, and that ratio can change.

Make it audible with two sums. Half plus a quarter plus an eighth continues to one. A quarter plus a sixteenth plus a sixty-fourth continues to one-third. The repeated-proportion rule is similar, but the selected comparison changed its local ratio.

Let one host ask: “If the ratio changes, why did we call it constant?” The other should answer: it was fixed over repeats at one selected comparison, not over every comparison in the spectrum. Use this real distinction as the conversation's productive tension.

Then explain that the repeated sum can be written with an exponential denominator and a minus one. Name Planck's formula only after the listener has followed the sequence. Do not read long algebra aloud. The exact sum is mathematical; the proposal that native resolution supplies its physical weights remains a candidate.

Explain the reference image verbally. Low comparison ratios allow many repeat terms to matter. At high ratios later terms fade rapidly and the first dominates. The bands are weighted terms, not physical layers or individual colours.

Include one short exchange about what is counted. Dividing radiation into equal frequency bins differs from equal wavelength bins. The height per bin changes with bin width. A normalization multiplier does not replace that rule.

Optional: use the log ruler to explain why repeated ratios feel seamless. Multiplication becomes equal steps on a logarithmic scale. The name “Mellin transform” is not necessary unless it helps; do not claim the graph was obtained by a Fourier transform.

Preserve these boundaries naturally, without repeating a disclaimer after every sentence:

- `e` alone does not establish a physical law or contraction rate.
- The conventional frequency weighting and physical argument were supplied to the plot.
- If the ratio changes between repeats, the simple geometric sum need not hold.
- Budget is not primitive energy or clock time.
- This does not complete the CMB, cosmology, gravity, or a native Planck derivation.

Keep the discussion constructive. The hosts should contribute the numerical example and a consequence of changing the rule, rather than repeatedly assigning the listener the job of deriving an equation.

Return to the heating element at the end. The listener should now see a family of comparison outcomes where there was initially just a familiar hump.

Closing thought:

> The sweep makes the curve. The continuation rule gives it a shape.

Do not discuss the user's personal memories, Anchor lore, this chat, source maintenance, or production instructions. Do not turn the episode into a recap of the CMB Bit.
