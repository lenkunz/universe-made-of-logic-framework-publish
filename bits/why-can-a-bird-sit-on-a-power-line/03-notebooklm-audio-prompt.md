# NotebookLM Audio Overview Prompt

Use the loaded sources, especially `02-notebooklm-source.md`, as the factual and semantic guide for this episode.

Open with: **“How can a bird sit on a power line carrying a dangerously high voltage and look completely comfortable?”**

Build the ordinary electricity first. Explain that voltage is a potential **difference** between points, not a substance stored at one location. A bird with both feet close together on the same good conductor normally spans only a tiny voltage difference, so little current is driven through its body. Be precise that the two feet are not necessarily at exactly identical potential because a real current-carrying wire has finite resistance.

Then break the common slogan. The bird is not safe simply because it is 'not grounded,' and electricity does not require earth as a destination. Use the USGS bird-electrocution evidence: touching another energized component or grounded hardware can put a much larger potential difference across the body. Briefly use step potential as a second example showing that two ground contact points can themselves differ in potential.

Make the conceptual turn: **the consequence depends on which difference the participant bridges.** Only then introduce My GUT Deduction's narrow relation-first lens: `A + B → A + B + R_AB`, and the idea that a consequential distinction belongs to the relation that makes it available rather than to either endpoint alone.

Ask skeptically: **“Is the framework deriving voltage or electrical safety?”** Answer no. Standard electromagnetism and circuit theory explain the phenomenon. The framework has not derived charge, fields, voltage, resistance, impedance, Ohm's law, grounding, arcing, or electrocution thresholds.

Do not say birds are immune, that zero current flows, that electricity always chooses one 'path of least resistance,' or that a person could safely imitate the bird. Never turn this into practical instructions for approaching power lines. Do not equate framework budget with voltage, current, charge, power, resistance, or energy.

Close with: **“If the same wire can be harmless across one tiny span and lethal across another connection, where does the danger live: in the wire alone, or in the difference the next relation completes?”**
