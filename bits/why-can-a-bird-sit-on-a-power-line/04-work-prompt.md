## Episode-specific article focus

Title: **Why Can a Bird Sit on a Power Line?**

Subtitle: *The wire can be thousands of volts above ground. What matters to the bird is the difference across its body.*

Open with the familiar bird-on-a-wire mystery. Establish ordinary circuit physics before framework language. The article's main correction is that the bird is not protected by a magical absence of 'ground'; its two feet normally bridge only a tiny potential difference on one conductor. Then use the dangerous counterexample of bridging another energized component or grounded hardware. A brief step-potential example can show that even two points on the ground can differ enough to matter.

The framework should enter late and lightly. Use Basics III's relation-first semantics only as a candidate structural lens: **the consequence depends on the specific difference/relationship established, not a context-free label attached to one endpoint.** Do not imply the framework derives electromagnetism.

## Google Flow / hero-image concept

Create a **16:9 editorial landscape photograph** of a small bird perched naturally on a single high-voltage power conductor, positioned center-right. Its two feet should be close together on the same wire. In the deeper background, show a utility structure with another conductor and grounded hardware visibly separated from the bird, making the missing second contact the visual idea without turning the scene into a diagram.

The central tension is **a dramatic electrical environment, but almost no potential difference across the bird's two nearby contact points**. Use realistic daylight, believable utility hardware and insulators, crisp feather detail, and generous sky negative space on the left for title placement.

Avoid lightning bolts, glowing electricity, sparks around the safely perched bird, arrows, voltage labels, equations, circuit diagrams, superhero imagery, sci-fi effects, galaxies, brains, generic AI aesthetics, or baked-in text. Do not show a person touching the line. Do not depict a second simultaneous contact that would imply the bird is being electrocuted.
