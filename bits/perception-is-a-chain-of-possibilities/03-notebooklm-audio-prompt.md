# NotebookLM Audio Overview Prompt

Create an English two-host Bits episode titled “Perception Is a Chain of Possibilities.” Use 02-notebooklm-source.md as the narrative and semantic guide. Follow the Bits Podcast Production Guide. Aim for a focused, curious conversation, roughly 15–20 minutes if the generation settings permit.

Open with a telescope making a faint source available to a receiver. The sky did not need to become brighter. What changed in the receiving arrangement? Explain collecting light versus merely enlarging an existing pattern using the linked optical sources. Give the listener a useful ordinary answer before any framework vocabulary.

Then ask: how can the result of one encounter become support for another? Move to someone who wants a house beside a lake. Money and a plan can establish a funded project; that project can become a participant in agreements with builders and suppliers. Those participants contribute constraints and may change the plan. This is the main example and deserves the most time.

Introduce the framework's broad meaning of perception only after the question is alive: relation resolving through a domain. Consciousness is not required. The instrument, detector, plan, and supported agreement can participate at their relevant grains. Stage is the encounter; it is not a separate container or an audience watching a finished world.

Keep the magnifier M in the relation. C encounters the source through M, including M's contribution. Grouping M and C as one receiver is allowed when it preserves that contribution. Both sides participate, but their contributions can be very unequal.

Preserve the user's strongest correction: “The coarseness of it is reserved by the magnifier” means the participating domains themselves carry established constraints and still-open possibilities. Do not explain this as blur alone. In the house example, money, plans, connections, and tools carry that organization; their composition makes particular further relations available.

The funded plan is already real as a participating project while the house remains unresolved. Hosted is real and can have its own relations. Being represented, established for reuse, and independently seated are different conditions.

Use the hammer as the clearest turn. Manufacturing establishes enough fine organization for a tool. Another encounter can use that tool as one coarse capability, without reconstructing its manufacture or specifying every later use. Deliver the central line: “What resolves in one encounter can become coarse ground for another.”

Clarify budget briefly: the participation rule can be common while density, organization, and the relations actually available change effective capacity. A small domain can carry extensive support. Naming a composite does not create free capacity. More participants can add conflicts as well as possibilities.

Give inherited support one short passage. In the proposed gravity account, Earth supplies anchoring that local domains reuse. A shared contribution can cancel from a local ruler comparison while still supporting both sides. Do not turn gravity into a claim that hosted domains always resolve slowly. Traversal, frequency, and total resolving capacity are distinct questions. Leave detailed cosmology to the existing Bits.

Near the end, use a writer and reader. Shared language, genre, and anticipated readers can shape a work; an actual encounter can establish a particular meaning. That meaning can become another reusable idea. Explain “Public Ciphertext, Private Punchline” through shared material meeting particular relations. Preserve the broader conditional proposal: several coarse projections may shape a supported continuation without specifying one reader or a completed route. Possibility is not guaranteed fulfillment. Use a general example, without assigning a cause to personal coincidences or reciting private lore.

Let one host ask a productive question: “What does the next encounter depend on that this domain actually carries?” Answer by keeping necessary support explicit and distinguishing a represented plan from an established project. Avoid constant adversarial debate or repeated evidence disclaimers. State the framework scope clearly, then reason within it.

Return to the telescope and the hammer. Each carries results of earlier encounters into something still open. End: “Perception is part of what happens. What it resolves becomes part of what can happen next.”

Do not invent equations, rates, sender intentions, universal centres, or claims that all possibilities are realized. Do not make every domain conscious, equate money with computational budget, equate coarse with low density, or turn future projection into a finished future person acting backward through time. The ordinary sources ground their own topics; the common ontology is the framework's proposal.
