# Perception Is a Chain of Possibilities

*How an encounter becomes ground for another encounter.*

![A worn hammer rests on a workbench beside an incomplete house plan, with survey pegs, timber, and an undeveloped lakeside site beyond.](perception-is-a-chain-of-possibilities.webp)
> **Image Caption:** A finished tool carries established work into a project whose next steps remain open.

A faint star does not have to change for us to see it through a telescope.

Something changes here, at the receiving end. A larger mirror or lens gathers more of the light arriving from the source and directs it into a usable image. That can make a faint object detectable. The telescope brings an organized receiving surface to the encounter. [NASA explains this light-gathering role](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/webb/webbs-mirrors/).

Magnification is another question. Enlarging an image can help us inspect the detail available, but enlargement alone cannot keep supplying finer distinctions. Nikon's microscopy guide calls enlargement beyond the system's useful resolving range *empty magnification*: a bigger image without additional resolved detail. [Nikon's useful-magnification explanation](https://www.microscopyu.com/microscopy-basics/useful-magnification-range).

So the receiving arrangement matters. Its structure helps determine what becomes accessible, even while the source remains the same.

Now take the next step.

What happens when something established through an encounter becomes part of the arrangement for another encounter?

Imagine wanting a house beside a lake.

At first, that wish leaves many things open. Which lake? What kind of house? What can you afford? Yet it can already change what you look for. A piece of land becomes relevant. A conversation with a builder becomes worth having.

Then a workable plan meets available money.

Something has been established before anyone lays a foundation. There is now a funded project that someone else can respond to. A builder can estimate the work. A supplier can discuss an order. The person controlling the site can decide whether the proposal fits its use.

The house remains unfinished. The funded plan already has consequences.

That is the entry point for the broader meaning of perception in my framework. From here, I am reasoning within a proposed ontology: perception is an encounter in which a relation resolves through a participating domain. Conscious experience is one richer case; consciousness is not required for this basic role.

A domain is something organized enough to participate as one at the grain of the encounter. A tool can do that. So can an agreement, supported through people and records. The question is what the arrangement can actually carry into the next relation.

The builder encounters your project through its plan, funding, site, and commitments. Their response changes the project. Perhaps the ground requires a different foundation. Perhaps the material you imagined is unavailable. A revised design may preserve the wish to live beside the lake while changing almost every detail of how it happens.

Your projection meets other constraints. What comes back becomes part of your next projection.

The wish was already present as a direction. It did not contain a completed future house waiting to arrive. Its finer continuation is being worked out through encounters, and those encounters can also interrupt it. Funding can disappear. A site can prove unsuitable. A builder can decline.

“Chain” names a path through this activity. Several exchanges can happen together, and later findings can revise earlier agreements. There is no requirement for a single tidy pipeline.

Return briefly to the telescope. Call the instrument M and the receiving person or detector C. M encounters the incoming light. C encounters the source through M, with M's organization still contributing to what is received.

The mirror's collecting area, the optical arrangement, and the detector's response belong in the account. M is a participant in the supported encounter. The incoming relation and the receiving arrangement both contribute, although their roles and costs need not be equal.

At a coarser grain, we may describe M and C together as one receiving system. That grouping is useful when it preserves the constraints that matter. Calling them one cannot make the instrument's contribution disappear.

This is where *reserved coarseness* becomes precise.

The participating domains themselves carry established constraints alongside open possibilities. Money has conditions on its availability and use. A plan holds some decisions while leaving others outstanding. A connection to a builder carries experience, access, trust, and limits. A tool brings capabilities that fit some tasks and fail at others.

These relations compose the project. They help determine what the project can become.

“Build a modest house beside this lake within this amount” can be a strong constraint. It still permits different room layouts, materials, and sequences of work. Coarse means those finer distinctions remain open relative to the present question. The constraint can be exact about what it does hold.

Douglas Engelbart approached practical capability through a related organizational question in his 1962 report: consider the person together with tools, language, methods, and training. His imagined architect develops a design through an arrangement that supports inspection and revision. That is a useful precedent for examining the whole working arrangement. [Engelbart's original report](https://www.dougengelbart.org/pubs/augment-3906.html).

The funded plan also makes three conditions easier to separate. A house can be **represented** in a sketch. A plan can become **established for reuse**, so later decisions reliably begin from it. Calling a domain **independently seated** requires a further account of which dependencies it can carry through its own supported relations. Stability inside a host alone does not establish that stronger condition.

Those conditions cannot be collapsed into one another. An agreement maintained by people and records can be consequential while still depending on them. Within the framework, hosted logic also has its own incoming consequences and outgoing constraints at the grain where it participates. Its activity is already part of the situation.

Now put a hammer on the workbench.

Manufacturing it required many particular things to hold. The head needed a usable shape and material condition. The handle needed to fit and remain attached. A collection became a tool because its organization could support the work expected of it.

That establishment can be fine relative to making the hammer. For the house project, the resulting hammer can enter as one coarse capability: a usable tool for driving or pulling nails.

The manufacturer did not have to specify every nail it would meet, every hand that would hold it, or every repair it might help complete.

**What resolves in one encounter can become coarse ground for another.**

The hammer's internal structure continues to matter. If the handle loosens, a distinction previously left in the background becomes consequential again. Coarse reuse depends on enough of that established structure continuing to hold.

The house can eventually do the same. Once built, it supplies ground for living, working, repairing, and rearranging. Its completion opens questions that the construction plan never individually settled.

This changes capacity as well as viewpoint.

In the framework, a common rule of primitive participation does not give every arbitrarily named domain an identical effective allowance. A composition includes members, relations among members, and established relations that can themselves participate. Reusing that organization changes how much relational work the arrangement can support.

Naming a collection “one” creates no extra capacity. Its organization has to carry the consequences. Adding relations can also introduce conflicts and obligations.

Nor does visible size tell us everything about the organization available. Dense relations can be carried locally. A domain can be richly organized inside while acting as one coarse participant in another encounter. Coarseness and relational density answer different questions.

Inherited support extends this beyond the workbench. The framework's proposed gravity correspondence gives a large anchored domain a role in shared persistence: members can reuse common support instead of each separately carrying the whole relation to the outside. An Earth-like domain is the intended physical example. A nearby object's participation can draw on that larger organization while contributing at a much coarser grain. The quantitative mapping to physical gravity remains a candidate.

If the same scale contribution supports two rulers, it may cancel from their ratio. That cancellation leaves the shared support in place. The comparison has simply stopped displaying it as a separate factor.

We also have to keep three questions distinct: how a relation traverses supported ground, how frequencies compare between domains, and how much total resolving capacity a composition carries. Greater capacity alone does not establish a faster traversal rate or a particular clock reading.

The same recursive question reaches meaning.

Consider a writer and a reader. The writer works through a language, a genre, a set of questions, and some expectation of possible readers. Those relations already shape which sentences get written. The text then encounters a reader whose memories and current questions differ from the writer's.

A passage can become useful in a way neither side specified alone. It might give the reader a distinction they needed, or turn an old question into a new one. That new distinction can then participate in another conversation or decision.

This is what I mean here by **Public Ciphertext, Private Punchline**. The words are publicly available; a particular payoff can depend on the ground brought into the encounter. It is a metaphor for that relation, without requiring an encrypted message.

There is also a question about production and encounter, before the reader reaches the page. Conditionally within the framework, overlapping coarse projections can help shape a work and the supported routes by which it becomes available. Several concerns may meet in one story, and several readers may find different continuations through it. That does not require the work to specify one exclusive recipient.

The projection remains present and coarse. A writer's anticipated reader is a current representation that can guide the writing. It is not a completed future person acting backward. Other participating constraints still affect what gets produced, whether it circulates, and whether a particular encounter happens at all. Possibility alone guarantees none of those steps.

The point reaches beyond interpreting something after its arrival. What is already supported can help form the next work, the routes to it, and what becomes possible when it is encountered.

These are differentiated encounters within one continuing relational ground. Each can have a local anchor without making that anchor a universal spatial center or beginning a separate world from zero.

The telescope inherits an organized receiving surface. The house project inherits plans, agreements, materials, and tools. The reader inherits language and a text, then may establish something that another encounter can use.

Look again at the hammer. Its manufacture resolved enough for it to become dependable ground. The project beside it still has decisions to make.

That is how established work and open possibility can belong to the same world.

**Perception is part of what happens. What it resolves becomes part of what can happen next.**
