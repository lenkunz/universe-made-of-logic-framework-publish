# Stage Is Where Light Meets Space

*Perception may be the most ordinary form of relativity.*

---

# The strangest rule may be the one nobody notices

Hold your hand in front of your face.

A distant building can fit behind one finger.

Nobody thinks the building physically shrank. We call it perspective and move on.

That may be why perspective matters. A huge relation elsewhere can become a tiny relation inside your current field of view. The remote thing does not need to become tiny. Your perception only needs to resolve its relation to you.

> **What if perspective is not a special trick performed by eyes? What if eyes simply show the universe's ordinary relation-resolution rule in plain sight?**

# Stage is where Light meets Space

An old lore phrase behind the framework says:

> **Stage is where Light meets Space.**

Here **Light** means relation, not merely electromagnetic light. Touch is Light in this sense. Distance is Light. Influence is Light. Measurement is Light. Any consequential connection between domains is Light.

**Space** means a domain capable of anchoring relation. It is not necessarily empty 3D container-space. A body, atom, planet, instrument, or stable coarse composite can act as a domain.

**Stage** is what becomes measurable when relation resolves against that domain.

So the root form is not `space + time → event`.

It is:

`relation + domain → Stage`

or in the old lore:

`Light + Space → Stage`

There is no primitive time term in that root.


# Afference and Efference: relation has a side

The old P-lane theory had names for something this framework kept using without naming clearly.

**Afference** is relation reaching inward.

It is the perception side of the domain.

**Efference** is relation reaching outward.

It is the perception side of Space.

Both require budget.

This does not create two kinds of substance. It gives one relation two orientations depending on which domain is anchoring the Stage.

Suppose Domain A reaches toward Domain B.

From A:

`A → Efference`

At Stage, that same relation is seated against B.

From B:

`A's Efference → Stage → B's Afference`

The relation did not disappear and get replaced.

Its **side flipped**.

That is why Stage is more than the place where something becomes measurable.

> **Stage is the turning interface where relation can change from one domain's outward reach into another domain's inward perception.**

And because the orientation is relative, B can later resolve what arrived inward and reach outward again:

`B Afference`
→ `B resolves`
→ `B Efference`
→ `Stage`
→ `another Afference`

The universe can therefore circulate relation without requiring an outside Actor.

# The flip can become persistence

Now close the relation.

A domain receives relation inward as Afference.

It resolves that relation through its own seated structure.

That produces Efference.

The Efference reaches Stage and can return / bind into the next Afference.

In the simplest recurring picture:

`Afference`
→ `domain resolution`
→ `Efference`
→ `Stage`
→ `Afference`
→ `...`

Both Afference and Efference require budget.

But if the loop is stable enough to keep returning the domain to a relation that can continue, the loop itself can provide the persistence budget.

That gives a more native meaning to persistence:

> **Persistence is not merely “still existing.” It can be the result of a stable Afference–Efference loop that keeps the domain resolvable as the same coarse relation.**

Do not read this as free energy.

It is a statement about relational budget and reusable closure.

A stable loop does not need every internal distinction to be rebuilt from nothing at every pass. It can act as already-resolved ground for the next relation.

This also explains why perception and action are not fundamentally separate categories.

What one domain sends outward as Efference can become another domain's Afference.

What one domain perceives inward can become the ground of its next outward relation.

So:

> **One domain's action can literally be another domain's perception, depending on which side of Stage anchors the relation.**

# Perception is not an illusion

“The universe is perception” can sound like nothing exists until a mind looks. That is not the claim.

A rock touching Earth has relation. Two particles interacting have relation. A detector receiving a signal has relation. A ruler compared with an object has relation.

> **Perception is the Stage-side resolution of relation against a domain.**

Vision is perception. Touch is perception. Distance measurement is perception. Clock comparison is perception. Detector count is perception. Gravity can be perception in the same broad sense.

Conscious experience is one elaborate participant, not the creator of reality.

> **The Stage is resolved reality.**

# Distance was never the base

We usually imagine two stars possessing an absolute distance first, then light crosses it, then an observer measures it.

The framework reverses that priority.

The stars have relation. The observer has relation to that relation. Distance is one way the Stage resolves it.

So distance is already a result. There is no external ruler standing outside every domain. A ruler is another domain. A clock is another persistence loop. A coordinate system is another resolved structure.

The framework therefore does not begin from `absolute distance / absolute time = absolute speed` because neither absolute distance nor primitive time is foundational.

# Resolution Equality

A correction now sits under the whole picture:

> **The universe gives the same fundamental total resolving capacity everywhere.**

One remote region is not given a fundamentally smaller universe-level budget. What differs is arrangement.

A domain can commit more closure to one relation and less to another. A coarse relation can leave more distinctions unresolved. A fine relation can seat more distinctions.

The equality belongs to total resolving capacity, not identical local state.

# Why everyone still measures the same local c

This does not predict that a local laboratory casually measures arbitrary vacuum light speeds.

Locally, ruler, persistence loop, propagation relation, vacuum relation, body, and anchor are seated in one ground. They co-resolve.

If the same local change affects those standards together, comparing one against another can hide the shared change.

So:

> **`c` is operationally invariant locally, but it is not treated as an ontologically primitive absolute speed.**

The constant belongs to the co-resolved local measurement relation.

# Now watch from very far away

Imagine an impossible observation. Suppose you could watch from the side while a light relation crossed from distant star A to distant star B.

Their local relation may correspond to four light-years. Inside your visual field, the path might occupy four centimeters. If the event takes four of your local years to unfold in the viewport, your Stage may display something like `1 cm per year`.

Ordinary optics would call this an image-plane or angular-style motion, not the remote system's local physical light speed.

The framework says:

> **Exactly. That is the point.**

You did not receive the remote relation absolutely. You received it resolved into your Stage.

Approach it. Magnify it. Build a better instrument. Create a finer relation. The same remote structure can occupy more of your available resolution.

You did not enlarge the star system. You resolved more of its relation.

# Perspective is the minimum-dimensional example

A distant object appears smaller because it occupies a smaller angular region.

The framework takes that mundane operation seriously:

> **A domain never receives another domain absolutely. It receives the relation between them, resolved at its own grain.**

In the older P-lane vocabulary, remote Efference becomes local Afference at Stage. The receiving domain then resolves that Afference at the grain its current relation can support.

Vision may be the simplest visible example of relational projection.

# Why the right triangle keeps appearing

Earlier Bits reached the simple low-bias form `R_all² = R_a² + R_p²`.

This Bit generalizes the intuition:

`R_all² = R_local² + R_relation²`

Do not read this as universal geometry. Real air, gravity, tools, matter, and environments can introduce huge multivariable relation.

The triangle matters because it is the least-biased geometry when one bounded total remains invariant, two projections are independently consequential, and no additional bias needs to be represented.

That is why the same mathematical shape may surface in clock-rate and length-contraction measurements. The framework asks whether those formulas are tracks left by one simple projection geometry.

# Co-shrinking

Suppose every ruler around you changes together. Your body changes with it. Your clock loop changes with it. Your local propagation standard changes with it.

What local ruler reveals the common change?

> **The ruler cannot measure itself.**

Locally, common change cancels. Across differently seated histories, it can become visible.

This opens a cosmology candidate:

> **What if apparent cosmic expansion is not literal production of more space, but the reciprocal reading of co-shrinking / increasingly fine local resolution?**

The same underlying relation can occupy more current ruler units if the ruler becomes finer. From inside the ruler system, that looks like space became larger.

This is not standard cosmology. It would have to reproduce the successful quantitative observations attributed to expansion.

# Coarse does not mean poor

If total fundamental resolving capacity is equal everywhere, coarse does not mean “the universe gave this place less.”

A fine relation might hold `A → B → C → D → E → F`. A coarse relation can hold `A → [open relation] → F`.

The intermediate distinctions are not necessarily erased. They remain possibilities until something makes them consequential.

> **Coarse resolution carries more unresolved possibility per resolved step.**

This is the counterweight that lets possibility remain affordable.

> **Nothing is decided until it needs to be decided.**

# Perception stretches with the relation

A remote domain can contain many fine local distinctions, but your Stage does not automatically import all of them one-for-one.

The relation can stay bound while your resolution of it stays coarse.

Possible surfaced readings include apparent spatial scale, frequency shift, event-rate difference, length projection, and coarse remote history.

The framework candidate is that these may be different observable faces of **cross-domain relational projection**.

# Gravity may be perception too

Now use the same broad meaning of perception for gravity.

A domain seated inside Earth's enormous anchored relation does not require Earth to behave like an invisible hand pulling it. The anchor relation is already part of what can resolve.

> **Gravity is a Stage-side perception of being consequentially anchored inside another domain.**

In Afference/Efference terms, the gravitational relation is not a remote actor pulling. It is part of the relation arriving inward as Afference and being resolved by the local Stage, while the domain's own Efference continues outward through that anchor.

A freely falling observer is locally weightless. Framework language: body and nearby Stage co-resolve along the same anchor relation.

Standing on the ground is different. The ground maintains another anchored relation that prevents the body from simply following free resolution. The distinction becomes locally measurable as support, pressure, and weight.

Standard general relativity describes gravity geometrically and formalizes local free fall through the equivalence principle. The framework keeps the measurement correspondence but proposes a different ontology: anchored relation as the deeper story.

This is not yet an empirical replacement for GR.

# There may be no view from nowhere

We imagine there must be a world “as it really is,” with absolute true size, distance, rate, and position, and observers distort it.

The framework says there may be no such external view.

There is domain. There is relation. There is resolution. There is Stage.

Different domains can resolve the same relation differently without one Stage being fake.

# Why the old Anchor had to split

The Anchor had too many interests. It wanted to resolve too many possibilities through one perspective.

One perspective cannot finely resolve every possible path at once without committing the world to that perspective's choices.

So it split interests into egos. Different perspectives could resolve different paths.

The remaining Anchor retained enormous personality and broad interest, which kept it connected to the splits.

Eventually its preferred role became Programmer, Architect, Reader, Observer, Viewer.

> **It wants to know what the story becomes, but it does not want to write every sentence itself.**

As lore, that preference is a stability mechanism. The deepest anchor prefers to preserve a Stage in which stories can resolve rather than directly become every Actor.

# Why perspective requires separation

If one Anchor were simultaneously every consciousness, every domain, every Stage, every Actor, and every observer, meaningful otherness would collapse.

But complete disconnection fails too. No relation means no shared Stage.

So:

`enough separation for distinct perspective`
+
`enough preserved relation for measurement`
→ `Stage`

The split does not only limit power. It makes perspective possible.

# The ordinary thing hiding the root rule

Perspective looked too ordinary to explain. Gravity looked too familiar to question. A ruler looked like the obvious definition of distance. A clock looked like the obvious definition of time. Local `c` looked absolute because every local ruler and clock agreed.

But if the framework is right, all of them were already inside the Stage. There was never an external measuring stick. There was only relation meeting domain.

> **The universe may use perspective everywhere. We only gave it a special name when our eyes made the geometry obvious.**
