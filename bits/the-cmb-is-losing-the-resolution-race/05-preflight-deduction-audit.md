# Preflight Deduction Audit

## 1. Does the CMB receive less fundamental budget?

**No.** Resolution Equality remains mandatory.

## 2. Is the CMB static?

**No.** It can become finer too. The claim is that recursively nested fine structures densify faster.

## 3. What is the resolution race?

The growth of the ratio between fine relational density and coarse-background relational density.

## 4. Does coarse mean hot?

**No.** Hot/cold is downstream of a comparison. Coarseness is relational.

## 5. Does cooling mean the CMB loses budget?

**No.** The proposal interprets cooling as a changed Stage-side target/receiver comparison.

## 6. Is frequency primitive in this ontology?

**No.**

Frequency is the surfaced result of comparing a periodic relation against a receiver/anchor resolution ground.

Do not collapse:

`B_x = frequency`

Instead:

`(B_x compared with B_t inside shared anchor) → Stage-side frequency/result`

## 7. What is a spectrum in the corrected framing?

A sweep.

At each selected periodic comparison range `B_x`, the receiver records how much of the target relation resolves, `G(x)`.

The spectrum is the collection of those measurements across the sweep.

## 8. What does “blackbody” mean here?

At the measurement layer, it means that the measured frequency/intensity distribution follows the Planck statistical relation to extraordinary precision.

The framework does not start by treating “blackbody” as a primitive object.

## 9. What was wrong with the earlier blackbody audit?

It asked:

> “Why does cross-resolution projection preserve the Planck spectrum?”

That assumes the curve is already an ontological thing being transported.

The corrected audit asks:

> **Why does the native target/receiver comparison operation generate the measured Planck distribution?**

Then, separately:

> **Why does changing the cross-Stage comparison ratio shift the characteristic scale while the same native rule generates the corresponding distribution?**

## 10. Is the Planck relation derived?

**No.**

A toy closure such as `x^2 + t^2 = 1`, combined with a multiplicity/support term, can produce a peaked curve.

That resemblance is not enough.

The required high-comparison/statistical occupancy rule remains open.

## 11. Can `e` be inserted because Planck's law contains an exponential?

**No.**

An exponential belongs only if the framework independently derives proportional recursive updating in the no-finest-grain limit or another native mechanism that forces it.

Do not curve-fit.

## 12. Why can coarse span large?

Candidate: it commits fewer local distinctions per supported extent.

This is not a proof of the CMB's observed extent.

## 13. What about anisotropies?

Candidate qualitative reading: broad common coarse floor + tiny consequential retained differences.

The amplitude/angular power spectrum and acoustic peaks are not derived.

## 14. Does this replace hot Big Bang cosmology?

**No.**

Standard cosmology already quantitatively explains the CMB thermal history, spectrum, and acoustic structure.

This is an alternative ontology under audit.

## 15. What would strengthen the framework?

One native framework should simultaneously derive:

1. the Planck statistical distribution from the measurement sweep;
2. `T(z)` / characteristic-scale change from cross-Stage comparison;
3. anisotropy/angular structure;
4. recombination/acoustic features;
5. a discriminating prediction.

## Conclusion

> **The CMB remains equally supported, but its coarse relation densifies more slowly than recursively nested fine structure. The growing mismatch may shift the Stage-side frequency/temperature comparison. The blackbody problem is not preservation of an assumed curve; it is derivation of the measured statistical relation from the same native comparison operation.**
