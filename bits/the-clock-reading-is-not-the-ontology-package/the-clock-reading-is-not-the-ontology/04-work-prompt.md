# Central editorial insight

Make this distinction unmistakable:

> **The measurement is not being rejected. The interpretation is what is being challenged.**

A clock-rate difference is an observation.

A muon lifetime comparison is an observation.

The Lorentz transformation is a highly successful mathematical law.

The speculative framework question is whether those relations uniquely establish **time as a primitive dimension**, or whether the same observable structure could eventually emerge from deeper non-temporal primitives.

Do not turn this into “Einstein was wrong.”

# Twin-paradox handling

Be precise.

The article may say:

- clocks and physical processes following different histories accumulate different readings;
- moving muon experiments are close physical analogues to round-trip comparisons;
- standard physics expects the entire physical organism to follow the same local relativistic rules.

Do not say:

- “consciousness definitely does not slow”;
- “the twin paradox has never been tested, therefore it is unsupported”;
- “there is no evidence for relativistic aging.”

The narrower philosophical boundary is:

> experiments do not isolate **subjective consciousness** as a separate primitive variable from the physical processes that support it.

Keep this brief.
