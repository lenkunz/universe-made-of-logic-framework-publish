## External sources

- [ ] NIST, `Optical scattering from surfaces`
  - URL: https://www.nist.gov/programs-projects/optical-scattering-surfaces
  - Why NotebookLM needs it: authoritative grounding that material properties, surface topography, contamination and roughness affect the angular distribution of scattered light.
  - Established observation / explanation this source supports: rough surfaces redistribute incident light rather than transmitting/reflecting it in one orderly direction.

- [ ] Germer & Marx, NIST / Applied Optics, `Ray Model of Light Scattering by Flake Pigments or Rough Surfaces With Smooth Transparent Coatings` (2004)
  - URL: https://www.nist.gov/publications/ray-model-light-scattering-flake-pigments-or-rough-surfaces-smooth-transparent-coatings
  - Why NotebookLM needs it: supports the role of rough-interface local slopes and transparent coatings in changing directional scattering.
  - Established observation / explanation this source supports: a smooth transparent coating over rough structure changes the optical interface and its bidirectional reflectance/scattering behavior.

- [ ] Ramella-Roman et al., NIST, `Hemispherical Imaging of Skin With Polarized Light` (2007)
  - URL: https://www.nist.gov/publications/hemispherical-imaging-skin-polarized-light
  - Why NotebookLM needs it: concrete scientific use of a glass slide and index-matching fluid to minimize the optical effect of an air-skin boundary.
  - Established observation / explanation this source supports: index matching can suppress boundary effects and surface-roughness contributions to scattering.

- [ ] Walter, Marschner, Li & Torrance, Cornell / EGSR, `Microfacet Models for Refraction through Rough Surfaces` (2007)
  - URL: https://www.cs.cornell.edu/~srm/publications/EGSR07-btdf.html
  - Why NotebookLM needs it: explicit treatment of transmission through rough surfaces including etched glass.
  - Established observation / explanation this source supports: rough transparent interfaces scatter transmitted light because many differently oriented microfacets refract it into different directions.
