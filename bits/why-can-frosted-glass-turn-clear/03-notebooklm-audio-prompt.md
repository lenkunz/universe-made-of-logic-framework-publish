## Episode shape

Open with: **“How can the same piece of rough glass hide a scene, then become much clearer when you add another transparent material to its surface?”**

Build ordinary optics first. Explain that frosted glass can transmit plenty of light while rough microfacets scatter its directions, destroying a sharp mapping of the scene. Then explain refractive-index mismatch and why filling/coating a rough interface with a better index-matched transparent medium can reduce the optical consequence of those tiny facets. Emphasize that the roughness can physically remain while becoming less consequential to transmitted image structure.

Keep the caveat that this works best when interface scattering is important; absorption or bulk scattering cannot be erased by simply wetting/coating a surface.

Only afterward introduce My GUT Deduction's narrow lens: **interiority is routing before geometry**. Use the optics as a candidate structural correspondence for the idea that a boundary's consequence can depend on what routes it permits, not merely its visible outline.

Ask skeptically: **“Is the framework predicting refractive index or scattering?”** Answer no. Standard optics already explains and calculates these effects. The framework has not derived Snell's law, Fresnel coefficients, refractive index, BRDF/BTDF, or rough-surface scattering.

Do not equate computational budget with photon number, optical power, wavelength, refractive index, or information-theoretic channel capacity. Do not imply any liquid makes any opaque object transparent.

Close with: **“If the roughness can stay while its optical consequence almost disappears, how much of a boundary belongs to its shape, and how much belongs to the relation crossing it?”**
