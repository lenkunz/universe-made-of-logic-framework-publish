Use the loaded sources, especially `02-notebooklm-source.md`, as the factual and semantic guide for this episode.

## Episode shape

Open with the ordinary mystery rather than framework vocabulary.

Follow this rhythm:

`ordinary mystery`
→ `standard explanation`
→ `one remaining conceptual question`
→ `framework lens`
→ `explicit boundary`
→ `memorable closing thought`

## Host behavior

- Explain the ordinary science clearly first.
- Let the framework enter only after the listener understands the ordinary question.
- Keep established external information distinct from the framework interpretation.
- Do not strengthen a candidate correspondence into established science.
- Do not invent numerical predictions or equations that are not in the sources.
- Prefer the kernel itself as the main example rather than a catalogue of pressure vessels.

## Episode-specific focus

Opening hook: **“Why does a popcorn kernel explode into a white foam instead of simply drying out as it gets hot?”**

Main example: follow one kernel from heating, through pressure buildup and starch softening, to pericarp rupture and rapid expansion. Emphasize that damaged/leaky hulls often fail to pop because the pressure cannot build sufficiently.

Most important conceptual turn: **the hull delays one route long enough for a pressure difference and softened interior to develop; opening a tiny route can then collapse the old supported state and enable rapid expansion.**

Only afterward introduce My GUT Deduction's narrow boundary/routing lens. A boundary can matter because of which routes it keeps unavailable, not merely because of its visible outline. Treat this as a candidate structural correspondence.

Useful skeptical / testing question: **“Is the framework predicting popping pressure, temperature, or starch behavior?”** Answer no. Standard food science and thermodynamics explain the phenomenon. The framework has not derived vapor pressure, pericarp fracture, starch rheology, expansion ratio, or optimal moisture.

Closing takeaway: **“If the kernel pops only because escape was prevented first, can a boundary create a future possibility simply by keeping one route closed long enough?”**

## Things to avoid

Do not say all water becomes steam before rupture, the hull creates energy, the kernel is perfectly sealed, or every kernel pops at one universal temperature/pressure. Do not identify the physical crack literally with framework Cut. Do not equate framework budget with heat, pressure, moisture, stress, energy, or volume.
