## External sources

- [ ] American Chemical Society, “The Secret Science of Popcorn” — https://www.acs.org/education/whatischemistry/adventures-in-chemistry/secret-science-stuff/popcorn.html
  - Why NotebookLM needs it: accessible established account of water, pressure, hull rupture, starch expansion, and the large volume change during popping.
  - Established observation / explanation this source supports: heating converts internal water to vapor; pressure rises until the kernel ruptures and starch expands.

- [ ] Barham et al., “Molecular Gastronomy: A New Emerging Scientific Discipline,” Chemical Reviews (2010) — https://pubs.acs.org/doi/10.1021/cr900105w
  - Why NotebookLM needs it: technical food-science account connecting hull softening, steam-pressure buildup, starch state, and explosive expansion.
  - Established observation / explanation this source supports: successful popping depends on the coupled timing of pressure buildup and material softening/failure.

- [ ] Chemical & Engineering News, “Another Popcorn Breakthrough” (2005) — https://cen.acs.org/articles/83/i19/Newscripts.html
  - Why NotebookLM needs it: evidence that leaky/damaged pericarps prevent sufficient pressure buildup and increase unpopped kernels.
  - Established observation / explanation this source supports: the boundary must retain pressure long enough; hull integrity matters.
