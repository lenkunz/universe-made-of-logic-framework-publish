## External sources

- [ ] NOAA/NESDIS, “How Do Snowflakes Form?” (2025): https://www.nesdis.noaa.gov/about/k-12-education/ice-snow/how-do-snowflakes-form
  - Why NotebookLM needs it: accessible authoritative overview of sixfold symmetry, facets, branching, and changing temperature/moisture along a crystal's path.
  - Established observation / explanation this source supports: ice crystal structure constrains sixfold form; atmospheric growth history shapes fine morphology.

- [ ] Kenneth Libbrecht, Caltech Engineering & Science, snow-crystal morphology overview: https://calteches.library.caltech.edu/681/
  - Why NotebookLM needs it: temperature/supersaturation morphology diagram, plate-column transitions, dendritic instability, and the role of facet growth plus diffusion.
  - Established observation / explanation this source supports: snow-crystal morphology changes strongly with growth conditions; crystals sharing broad symmetry can acquire different detailed forms.

- [ ] Barrett, Garcke & Nürnberg, Physical Review E 86, 011604 (2012): https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevE.86.011604
  - Why NotebookLM needs it: technical grounding for faceted snow-crystal growth as diffusion-limited growth with anisotropic surface energy and attachment kinetics.
  - Established observation / explanation this source supports: complex sixfold forms can emerge from constrained anisotropic growth rather than from a complete pre-specified blueprint.

- [ ] Murray et al., “Trigonal Ice Crystals in Earth's Atmosphere,” NOAA repository: https://repository.library.noaa.gov/view/noaa/22275
  - Why NotebookLM needs it: caveat against oversimplifying “all atmospheric ice is perfectly sixfold”; stacking-disordered ice can produce threefold forms.
  - Established observation / explanation this source supports: ordinary hexagonal symmetry is strong but atmospheric ice has important structural exceptions.
