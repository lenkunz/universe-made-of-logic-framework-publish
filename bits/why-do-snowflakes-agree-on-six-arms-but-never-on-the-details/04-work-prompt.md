## Episode-specific article focus

Lead with the apparent contradiction: a snowflake can show strong sixfold agreement while its detailed branches remain unique. Explain the ordinary crystal-growth story first: hexagonal structure, temperature/supersaturation-dependent morphology, diffusion, facet attachment kinetics, branching instability, and accumulated atmospheric history. Preserve the caveat about stacking-disordered/trigonal atmospheric ice.

Only later introduce the framework lens: a real coarse constraint can be exact at its own grain without pre-rendering every finer distinction. Keep this as an analogy / interpretive lens, not a derivation of snow-crystal physics.

The central payoff should remain:

> **A rule can be exact about the structure it constrains without specifying every detail that later grows inside that structure.**

## Google Flow / image brief

Create a landscape 16:9 editorial macro image centered on one snow crystal whose six main arms share unmistakable hexagonal order, but whose fine side-branch details become progressively individual and history-marked. The image should feel physically grounded and photographic/editorial rather than fantasy: crisp ice structure, subtle atmospheric depth, tiny variations and interruptions in branch growth, one strong focal crystal occupying the right or center-right, generous clean negative space on the left for title placement. The visual metaphor is **shared constraint, divergent detail**. Avoid generic galaxies, glowing circuitry, floating equations, diagrams, labels, text baked into the image, perfectly mirrored CGI ornament, or sci-fi aesthetics. The crystal should look grown rather than designed.
