## Image generation

Work should generate the image directly.

Use this episode-specific visual concept as the starting brief:

Create a cinematic editorial landscape image in 16:9 for **“Why Does a Crack Turn?”** Show a close macro view of a single crack advancing through a translucent or ceramic-like material. Ahead of the crack tip, suggest several physically available continuations through subtle internal stress textures, grain boundaries, or directional striations, but only one path has actually opened into a dark fracture. The image should communicate **many constrained continuations narrowing into one realized route**. Keep it grounded in real material fracture, not science fiction. Place the crack and tip mainly in the right or lower-right half, leaving generous calm negative space in the upper-left / left third for title placement. Avoid arrows, equations, framework diagrams, probability trees, galaxies, glowing brains, neon circuitry, shattered-screen clichés, human faces, text baked into the image, and perfectly symmetric branching.

Choose one strong conceptual relationship from the finished article and refine the image brief if needed, while preserving the same grounded editorial idea.

Avoid:

- embedded title text;
- labels or equations;
- infographic arrows;
- generic cosmic-AI decoration;
- visuals that silently turn candidate correspondence into established ontology.

The **Image Caption blockquote is part of the final public article**. Keep it visible in the Markdown and in Substack unless there is a specific editorial reason to omit that caption entirely.
