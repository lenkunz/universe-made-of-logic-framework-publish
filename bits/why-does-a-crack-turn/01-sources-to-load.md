## External sources

- [ ] NASA, *Linear Elastic Fracture Mechanics Primer* (NASA-TM-103591)
  - Source: https://ntrs.nasa.gov/archive/nasa/casi.ntrs.nasa.gov/19920021173.pdf
  - Why NotebookLM needs it: Background on Griffith fracture, near-tip elastic fields, stress-intensity factors, fracture toughness, and crack growth.
  - Established observation / explanation this source supports: Crack growth depends on near-tip mechanical driving relative to material resistance.

- [ ] NIST GCR 22-917-51, fracture assessment guidance
  - Source: https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/gcr/2022/NIST.GCR.22-917-51.pdf
  - Why NotebookLM needs it: Engineering framing connecting stress intensity, energy release / J-integral, and fracture toughness.
  - Established observation / explanation this source supports: Fracture occurs when crack-driving demand becomes sufficient relative to toughness.

- [ ] Sun, Edwards, Chen & Li (2021), *A state-of-the-art review of crack branching*, Engineering Fracture Mechanics 257, 108036
  - Source: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.engfracmech.2021.108036
  - Why NotebookLM needs it: Shows why branching/path selection remains harder than simple single-crack propagation and surveys candidate mechanisms.
  - Established observation / explanation this source supports: Crack branching can involve velocity, microcracking, stress reorientation, and dynamic instability rather than one universal path rule.

- [ ] Sharon & Fineberg (1996), *Microbranching instability and the dynamic fracture of brittle materials*, Physical Review B 54, 7128
  - Source: https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevB.54.7128
  - Why NotebookLM needs it: Experimental basis for dynamic microbranching.
  - Established observation / explanation this source supports: Fast cracks can become unstable and generate repeated side branches that alter propagation.

- [ ] Lubomirsky et al. (2024), *Quenched disorder and instability control dynamic fracture in three dimensions*, Nature Communications
  - Source: https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-024-51573-6
  - Why NotebookLM needs it: Modern 3D phase-field example where complex crack trajectories and topology changes emerge without prescribing a future crack line.
  - Established observation / explanation this source supports: Disorder and branching instability can jointly control dynamic fracture trajectories.

- [ ] Henry (2025), *Phase-field study of the effective fracture energy increase during dynamic crack propagation in disordered heterogeneous materials*, Physical Review E 111, 055502
  - Source: https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevE.111.055502
  - Why NotebookLM needs it: Demonstrates that spatial arrangement of heterogeneity can strongly affect propagation even when effective elastic properties change comparatively little.
  - Established observation / explanation this source supports: Bulk averages do not uniquely determine the local route available to an advancing crack.
