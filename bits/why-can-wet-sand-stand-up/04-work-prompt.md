## Episode-specific article focus

Title: **Why Can Wet Sand Stand Up?**

Subtitle: *A little water does not fill the gaps. It builds bridges across them.*

Preserve the important non-monotonic caveat: more water is not simply more strength because liquid morphology changes. Use the reported ~1% optimum only as a result of the cited experimental system, not a universal recipe.

## Google Flow / image concept

Create a **16:9 editorial landscape macro image** of a freshly cut wet-sand wall or small sandcastle turret on a beach, positioned center-right. The main surface should look physically ordinary and tactile. Along one clean broken edge, reveal a subtle macro-to-micro transition: individual sand grains remain clearly separate, but tiny curved water menisci visibly bridge neighboring grains at their contact points. The water should appear as realistic transparent films and concave bridges, not blue glue.

The central visual tension is **loose grains becoming a load-bearing wall because tiny connections span the contacts**. Include a small patch of dry loose sand slumping at the base for contrast, but keep one strong focal concept. Leave generous quiet negative space on the left for title placement. Use natural beach light, realistic quartz grain variation, and editorial scientific photography.
