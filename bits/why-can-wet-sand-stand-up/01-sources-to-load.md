## External sources

- [ ] Pakpour et al., “How to construct the perfect sandcastle,” *Scientific Reports* 2, 549 (2012). https://www.nature.com/articles/srep00549
  - Why NotebookLM needs it: central sandcastle experiment and quantitative stability discussion.
  - Established observation / explanation: capillary bridges give wet sand stiffness; the reported optimum strength occurred at a very low liquid volume fraction of about 1%; column failure can be treated as elastic buckling under self-weight.

- [ ] APS Physics, “Sliding Sand” (2014), synopsis of Fall et al., PRL 112, 175502. https://physics.aps.org/articles/v7/s51
  - Why NotebookLM needs it: accessible explanation of the counterintuitive role of small versus larger water contents.
  - Established observation / explanation: liquid bridges stiffen wet sand; adding some water can reduce sled friction by suppressing the heap in front, while higher water content changes the response.

- [ ] Mani et al., “Role of contact-angle hysteresis for fluid transport in wet granular matter,” *Physical Review E* 91, 042204 (2015). https://journals.aps.org/pre/abstract/10.1103/PhysRevE.91.042204
  - Why NotebookLM needs it: keeps the liquid-bridge picture from becoming an oversimplified static cartoon.
  - Established observation / explanation: contact-angle hysteresis affects equilibrium bridge-volume distributions in wet granular matter.

- [ ] Awdi et al., “Morphology-resolved stress contributions in sheared wet granular materials,” *Physical Review E* 113, 055408 (2026). https://journals.aps.org/pre/abstract/10.1103/jp22-sptz
  - Why NotebookLM needs it: recent micro-macro evidence on how liquid morphology contributes to stress.
  - Established observation / explanation: 3D x-ray microtomography resolves bridges and larger liquid clusters; in the reported conditions, simple two-grain bridges dominated isotropic capillary pressure even while larger morphologies localized under shear.
