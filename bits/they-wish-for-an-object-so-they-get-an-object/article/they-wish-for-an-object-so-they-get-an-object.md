# They Wish for an Object, So They Get an Object

*The wish becomes a tool. The tool becomes a constraint. The answer arrives at that grain.*

![A carefully assembled receiving instrument has an aperture, a mounted lens, and a recording surface with a few faint marks, all supported on a laboratory bench.](they-wish-for-an-object-so-they-get-an-object.webp)
> **Image Caption:** The instrument carries an established question into the encounter that can answer it. Editorial illustration of a generic instrument; the marks are illustrative, not experimental data.

A spot appears on the detector. Then another, somewhere else. Keep the experiment running, and the separate arrivals begin to form a larger pattern.

In Hitachi's single-electron demonstration, individual detections accumulate into interference fringes. The apparatus uses an electron biprism, which brings coherent alternatives from opposite sides of a fine filament into overlap. It is a double-slit-type experiment without a literal plate containing two cut slits. The fringes are built from the individual records. [Hitachi's experiment and footage](https://www.hitachi.com/rd/research/materials/quantum/doubleslit/index.html).

So the localized arrivals and the interference pattern belong to the same experiment.

There is a distinction to hold from the beginning: recording where something arrives and recording which path it took are different measurements. A spot on the receiving surface does not, by itself, supply that path record. The standard quantum account preserves this distinction. [Feynman's account of quantum behavior](https://www.feynmanlectures.caltech.edu/III_01.html).

Now look at the instrument that made the spot available as a result.

What did it have to be built to distinguish?

Someone wanted a measurement in which an individual detection could leave a separate record. That request became a design problem. A material had to respond. The response had to be carried into something that could be recorded. The arrangement had to be assembled and calibrated so the intended difference would count.

That is the continuity behind my sentence:

**They wish for an object, so they get an object.**

From here, I am developing a deduction within my proposed ontology. The wish, its supported development, the apparatus, and the measurement result belong to one relational account. The quantum experiment is the physical comparison that account has to meet.

The wish already holds a direction: make an individual detection distinguishable.

It leaves many decisions open. Which detector? Which geometry? What kind of record? How much separation between responses is enough for this task? Those questions can remain unresolved while the direction already makes some work relevant and other work unnecessary.

This is what I mean by an Aim being coarse ground. It is already a constraint at its own grain. Here, *grain* means the distinctions the relation actually requires.

Construction gives that direction further consequential structure. A design becomes an arrangement of materials. Testing exposes a mismatch. Calibration changes how the instrument's response is interpreted and used. Each encounter can establish something the next one relies on.

The wish remains in the account throughout this development. Its reality begins with its supported participation as a direction; building the apparatus seats finer relations through which it can operate. There is no extra ontological crossing where an unreal wish suddenly becomes real because hardware has appeared.

This continues the question from the perception Bit: what an encounter establishes can become ground for another encounter. A built instrument carries that accumulated work into the measurement.

When I say that the apparatus builds a fine-grain path, the path is this arrangement of consequential interaction. It includes what can respond, which differences can survive as records, and what the receiving system can distinguish.

It is not an already completed spatial trail assigned to the electron before the experiment.

Once a successful measurement requires two responses to count differently, its result has to preserve that difference. Leaving them equivalent would fail to answer the same question. The built relation has made a particular kind of distinction necessary.

That is the force of saying the encounter has to resolve along the arranged path.

Does this let the experimenter choose the exact spot on every trial?

The wish concerns the distinction being requested. Wanting an individual arrival recorded is different from wanting that arrival at one preferred position. The built apparatus carries the first request into the encounter. Which individual result is permitted still depends on the source, the arrangement, its coupling to the detector, and the other participating constraints.

The wish has become consequential without becoming the only consequence that matters. It can shape the form of a supported answer without supplying every value inside that form.

This brings us to uncertainty.

Imagine a constraint that says: the continuation must remain inside this boundary. That can be exact about what it excludes while leaving many finer arrangements inside the boundary open.

The coarse constraint is already doing work. Another relation can rely on it. Neither relation has to begin by selecting every internal distinction that might ever become relevant.

Later, an encounter may make one of those differences consequential. The finer resolution then has to satisfy the new demand along with the surviving boundary. The earlier constraint remains part of what the continuation must fit.

That is the sense in which uncertainty gives coarse projection room to resolve. Some things are firmly held, while the finer way they will be satisfied remains open.

The stronger claim in this ontology is that the openness can belong to the relation itself. It does not require an exhaustive microscopic answer already calculated somewhere and merely concealed from a limited observer. A coarse constraint can participate through what it presently holds.

Nor does a local encounter have to simulate its whole supporting world before it can resolve. Moving that exhaustive simulation into a larger invisible machine would retain the very assumption being questioned.

But missing detail has more than one meaning, and the distinction matters here.

| What is missing from this encounter? | What follows in the framework? |
|---|---|
| A finer distinction has not been established | The alternatives can remain genuinely open under the present constraint. |
| A distinction survives but is not exposed here | Retained or hosted support can still affect the encounter without being separately displayed. |
| A former distinction no longer leaves any surviving trace | Requesting more detail cannot recover that exact history merely by demanding it. |

“We do not see it” cannot decide which case we have.

Stored, accessible, and presently rendered are different conditions. A record may survive without being available to this receiving relation. It may become accessible without every detail being reconstructed now. And if the distinguishing traces are gone, a story compatible with what remains does not establish one unique past.

So open possibility is not permission to invent a history after the result. The surviving constraints continue to matter.

Now bring in Stage.

“Stage is where Light meets Space” names the encounter. In this vocabulary, Light means Relation, and Space means the participating Domain. Each brings established constraints and possibilities still open relative to them.

The possibility side belongs to both participants. The incoming relation brings what it can support, and the receiving domain brings the possibilities and limits of its own organization. Their meeting resolves a consequence at the grain of that encounter.

**Stage is that resolution in packaged form.**

The package is real. It can leave a record, constrain a later measurement, or become a participant in another encounter. Calling it packaged does not put its reality somewhere behind a display.

An observer can encounter the detector and its output as one organized result. That encounter need not separately expose every relation inside the detector, every step of its manufacture, or every piece of support still operating through it.

Those inner relations retain their own activity. Hosted relations can receive consequences and project constraints at their own grains, even when the outer encounter treats the organization as one.

The notation `R_all` names locally capped resolving participation for the relation being considered. An inner relation, its supported compartment, and a later encounter involving that compartment keep distinct participation. The later cap neither contains all their work nor grows simply because we rename the collection. This is not one universal pool owned by an observer.

The useful consequence here is straightforward: an encounter can resolve a supported package without exhaustively rendering everything supporting it.

Return to the detector spots.

In this reading, arrival detection makes a local record consequential. The record is fine relative to that question. A finer record at the receiving surface does not automatically establish which path was taken through the apparatus.

An arrangement that records path information changes which distinction the encounter must satisfy. Its path-sensitive interaction now belongs to the ground of the experiment. Simply making the screen's position record sharper is a different change.

Quantum mechanics already handles the physical distinction. For coherent alternatives, it combines probability amplitudes and obtains probabilities from the squared magnitude of their sum. When the alternatives become fully distinguishable through path recording, the corresponding interference is lost. This account does not require an electron to switch between the substances of a classical water wave and a tiny bullet. [Feynman's explanation](https://www.feynmanlectures.caltech.edu/III_01.html).

Intermediate cases also matter. Englert's analysis gives a quantitative bound relating fringe visibility and available which-way information in a two-way interferometer. Its derivation does not use a Heisenberg uncertainty relation. The broad word *uncertainty* in my ontology cannot substitute for that result. [Englert's original paper](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.77.2154).

My proposed account uses Domain and Relation as its starting kinds. It does not add wave and object as two further primitives. The observed forms belong to what supported encounters resolve: a localized record here, a pattern across many records, and different consequences when the participating distinctions change.

The object-like result is real at its grain. Taking that reality to require a completely specified prior object history is an additional ontological step. The result alone has not supplied every distinction that step assumes.

The physical completion task is to map the framework's relational quantities to an actual experiment and recover its probability distribution as phase, geometry, detector coupling, and path distinguishability change, including intermediate cases. The local cap notation does not derive those probabilities or the Born rule. A distinct physical theory would also have to identify whether, and where, its predictions differ measurably from quantum mechanics.

Look again at the apparatus. The wish became supported work. That work became a tool. The tool carried a question into an encounter, and a successful detection answered at the grain the arrangement required.

**They wish for an object, so they get an object.**

We have obtained a real answer. Have we also established every relation that supported it, or quietly mistaken an object-grain answer for a complete account of what came before?
