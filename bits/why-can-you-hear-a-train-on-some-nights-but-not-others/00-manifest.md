## Central question

How can the same distant sound source become much more audible at the same receiver on some nights than on others, even when the source and listener have not meaningfully moved?

## Central payoff

The atmosphere is not a passive gap between a sound source and a listener. Vertical temperature gradients and wind shear change the effective sound-speed profile and bend sound paths. A daytime temperature lapse can bend sound upward away from the ground; a nighttime inversion can bend it back down. Background quiet and physical propagation are separate contributors to audibility.
