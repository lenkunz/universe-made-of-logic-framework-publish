## External sources

- [x] [Daniel A. Russell, Penn State, “Refraction of Sound Waves”](https://www.acs.psu.edu/drussell/demos/refract/refract.html)
  - Why NotebookLM needs it: clear acoustic explanation, approximate sound-speed/temperature relation, daytime lapse, nighttime inversion, and an accessible across-lake illustration.
  - Established observation / explanation this source supports: a vertical temperature profile can refract sound upward or downward; the cabin story is an illustrative personal observation, not controlled evidence.
- [x] [Federal Highway Administration, “Noise Barrier Design Handbook,” §3.3.3](https://www.fhwa.dot.gov/ENVIRonment/noise/noise_barriers/design_construction/design/design03.cfm)
  - Why NotebookLM needs it: authoritative applied explanation of atmospheric absorption, refraction, turbulence, temperature gradients, and wind effects.
  - Established observation / explanation this source supports: upwind/downwind wind shear and daytime/nighttime temperature profiles can change sound levels at receivers; other propagation mechanisms remain active.
- [x] [Federal Highway Administration, “Noise Measurement Handbook,” §9.1.1.2](https://www.fhwa.dot.gov/Environment/noise/measurement/handbook.cfm)
  - Why NotebookLM needs it: demonstrates that meteorology must be controlled or recorded when comparing real-world sound measurements.
  - Established observation / explanation this source supports: measured highway-noise levels can be higher on clear inversion nights and lower on bright sunny days for otherwise similar traffic conditions.
- [x] [National Academies Press, NCHRP Research Report 882, “How Weather Affects the Noise You Hear from Highways” (2018)](https://www.nationalacademies.org/publications/25226)
  - Why NotebookLM needs it: research-program context and confirmation that meteorological variation is a practical factor in highway-noise prediction and measurement.
  - Established observation / explanation this source supports: highway noise varies with weather as well as traffic; the report develops guidance and tools for including meteorological effects.
