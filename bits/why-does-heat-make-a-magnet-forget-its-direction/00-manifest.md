## Central question

Why can sufficiently strong heating make a permanent magnet lose its macroscopic magnetization even though the material can still contain magnetic moments and can become ferromagnetic again after cooling?

## Central payoff

A permanent magnet is not magnetic merely because microscopic magnetic moments exist. It depends on collective order and on a domain structure that produces a net direction. Near and above the material's Curie temperature, thermal fluctuations defeat the long-range order supporting spontaneous magnetization. Cooling can restore ferromagnetic order without automatically restoring the same macroscopic direction.
