## External sources

- [ ] [NIST, *Basic Magnetic Quantities and the Measurement of the Magnetic Properties of Materials*](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/Legacy/MONO/nbsmonograph47.pdf)
  - Why NotebookLM needs it: Authoritative definitions of ferromagnetic versus paramagnetic behavior, magnetic domains, spontaneous magnetization, and Curie temperature.
  - Established observation / explanation this source supports: A ferromagnetic material transforms to a paramagnetic condition at its Curie temperature; domain structure and net specimen magnetization are not the same thing.

- [ ] [NIST Center for Neutron Research, “Magnetic Phase Transition and Spin Wave Excitations”](https://www.nist.gov/document/ncnrbt7handout)
  - Why NotebookLM needs it: Experimental framing of ordered magnetic moments below a ferromagnetic transition and the temperature dependence of magnetic order.
  - Established observation / explanation this source supports: Bulk magnetization is an order parameter that decreases as thermal excitation disrupts magnetic order.

- [ ] [NIST Center for Neutron Research, PBR experiment handout](https://www.nist.gov/ncnr/2018-summer-school-fundamentals-neutron-scattering/course-materials/experiment-handouts/pbr)
  - Why NotebookLM needs it: Concrete discussion of ordered spins below a Curie temperature and how field cooling can bias a preferred magnetization direction.
  - Established observation / explanation this source supports: Cooling through an ordering transition can establish order, but symmetry-breaking conditions and history influence its direction.

- [ ] [Kakehashi et al., “Ferromagnetism of transition metals and screened exchange interactions,” Physical Review B 83, 144409](https://journals.aps.org/prb/abstract/10.1103/PhysRevB.83.144409)
  - Why NotebookLM needs it: A primary research example showing that finite-temperature ferromagnetism and Curie temperatures depend on material-specific electronic and exchange physics.
  - Established observation / explanation this source supports: Curie temperature is not a universal constant and quantitative prediction requires a real material model.
