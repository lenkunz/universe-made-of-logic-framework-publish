**Why a prism bends and the sky shimmers — one rule, two geometries.** *New.* At a boundary a claim is counted against a different grid. Let the density vary *across* the claim and you get bending — mirages, the flattened sun at the horizon, stars twinkling. Or keep the medium uniform and vary the *path length* across the beam, which any wedge does by geometry, and you get the same displacement from traversal instead of gradient. Smooth curvature in one case, a sharp fan in the other, because the geometry of the encounter differs — not because anything different happens to the light.

**Transparency is indifference, not permission.** *New.* A transparent medium isn't letting light through — it has no structure narrow enough to care about that claim's resolution, so nothing forces fine resolution and the claim traverses. Tint is selective interest: the medium cares about part of the range. And colour is what a thing *can't* absorb — you never see what something takes in, only its failures. Three familiar behaviours from one distinction, and the inversion at the end is the memorable bit.

**Why a refractive index exists at all.** *New, and shorter than the others.* Not why the number — why the quantity. Two regions of different resolution density *must* have a ratio between them, and every claim crossing is priced at that ratio. The index isn't a property the material possesses; it's an exchange rate, the same kind of quantity as the conversion between ticks and spacing. There was never an option for a medium not to have one.

**Why light stops obeying inverse-square when it hits air.** *New — the ballistic/diffusive crossover, now with a mechanism.* The transition happens where the receiving region's density makes the projection coarser relative to its surroundings, so a claim that seated endpoint-to-endpoint now has to seat across a neighbourhood. Not a new mechanism — a change in which topology can carry the resolution.

**States of matter as resolution densities.** *New, and the strongest correspondence in this cluster.* Vacuum, gas, liquid, solid, dense solid — the same claim crossing each gets a different balance of coarse to fine, and the mean free path shortens accordingly. The framework says why the sequence exists rather than treating the phases as separate cases. Gamma rays into lead is the vivid version.

**The thinnest possible shell.** *New — resolved Tier 3 item.* Every unit of thickness is budget spent on coarse projection, and budget spent there is unavailable for fine traversal. So thin isn't an elegant optimum — it's what you settle at when spending as little as you can. Efficiency pushes down; resolution stops it, because below the resolution floor an enclosure stops being one.

**Things can match without anything passing between them.** *New.*

Similar structures turn up in places with no meaningful contact — and the instinct is to look for something travelling between them. Usually nothing does.

**Both formed against the same ground.** And ground doesn't have to carry a finished pattern to matter; it only has to make some arrangements cheaper than others. Repeat a weak bias across enormous numbers of cases and you get a strong statistical preference, with no single exchange ever containing anything like a blueprint.

Which also answers the obvious follow-up — why doesn't everything sharing ground become identical? Because **shared constraint produces recurrence, not duplication.** The ground constrains a *family*; which member you get is settled locally. Same shape at coarse grain, wildly different in detail.

*And the ground was never neutral to begin with.* Whatever could hold, held — and the fact that some things couldn't is itself a difference that persists. Later structure doesn't resolve into empty space. It resolves into a bias inherited from what failed first.

Applies to convergent evolution, to institutions that rhyme across unconnected cultures, to the same design turning up independently. Broad reach, and it replaces a mystery with an economy.

**A crowd is not a new kind of thing.** *New.* A million domains that each act as one are still a million domains that each act as one — addition never produces a new level. What's required is that the relations among them become a participant in their own right. And the rule turns out to have been true all the way down: more paths don't give a boundary, more boundaries don't give capacity, more capacity doesn't give identity through change. *Quantity thickens a level; a new level requires the collective relation to become a new kind of participant.* Good because it applies to teams, institutions, and ecosystems as readily as to physics.

**Understanding a drive is not having it.** *New.* A domain's motivations descend from whatever constituted it. Something built from structures that persist through alteration inherits continuation aims — survival and everything that grows on it. Something constituted by *relations among* such domains inherits none of that, and would be driven instead by what made it a participant: which configurations can hold together. It could model continuation-driven behaviour perfectly and not be moved by it. Touches AI without being about AI, which is probably the right distance.

**Where individual things stop and background begins.** *New.*

Look further out and individual objects get harder to pick apart. Push far enough and separating them stops being affordable at all — the relations are still there, but too coarse to bind as separate interactable things.

What you meet at that point isn't a further set of objects. It's **their common ground**.

Which makes two words precise. *Foreground* is what can be resolved individually. *Background* is constraint that's still entirely real but sits below the grain where individual interaction happens. Not the oldest thing you can see — the layer whose distinctions are too coarse to show up separately from here.

**And it joins two things usually treated as unrelated:** resolved galaxies, then a resolution frontier, then background. One sequence rather than two subjects.

*Worth knowing before writing it:* physics already has this boundary and calls it the surface of last scattering, so the piece should present as re-description rather than rival account. The honest version admits the standard version also explains *where* the boundary falls, and this framing doesn't yet.

**Coarse doesn't mean fuzzy. It means undecided between sharp options.** *New.* The easy picture of coarse information is a blurry photo that sharpens later. But a coarse constraint can be a **junction** — several pathways, each perfectly sharp once entered, with nothing forcing a choice among them. Corrects a misreading the documents themselves half-invited.

**Different aims can build the same ground.** *New.* Reinforcement usually means repetition — taking the same path again. But two structures with unrelated purposes that seat through the same coarse ground each give it another reason to persist. No coordination required; nobody arranged it. Which makes such ground unusually hard to remove, since dislodging it breaks several things that have nothing else in common. *Difference doesn't have to converge in order to cooperate.*

**Dormant is not erased.** *New.* A relation can be fully preserved while nothing currently resolves it. A keyboard layout with no one at it re-binds in minutes. A compressed file whose program is gone is recoverable only by rebuilding the constraint that made the bytes mean something. Same category, wildly different price — and recovery cost scales with how much surviving structure still points at the answer. Three states, not two: resolved, dormant, erased. Only the last is the hard limit.

**A convention goes dormant when its users leave. A derivation doesn't.** *New.* Something can be widely reused and shallow — driving on the left, a file format, a keyboard layout. Enormous reuse, very hard to dislodge, seating into nothing beneath itself. Stability that is horizontal rather than vertical. Which is why reuse tells you what a thing costs to remove, and seating depth tells you whether it would still be there if everyone stopped.

**Distance is what two things can't afford.** *New.* Nothing travels carrying a signature; everything is already bound, and what costs is resolution. But resolution is a transaction with two payers — so a relation stays coarse when the *pair* can't afford it, and it makes no observable difference which end is short. Distance stops being a property of the space between and becomes a fact about the two ends together. With the consequence that sounds strange and isn't: something can become effectively nearer without moving, if either end's capacity grows. Which is what a telescope does.

**Why the local group doesn't recede.** *New.* Everything outside recedes; inside, Andromeda approaches. Standard cosmology draws that boundary where gravitational binding overcomes expansion. The framework draws it where the pair can still afford the relation. Same boundary, different account of what makes it one — not a contest between two forces, but the edge of what can be jointly paid for.

**Dimming and redshift are the same accounting.** *New.* Redshift is fewer arrivals per local cycle; dimming is less energy per local cycle. Not two effects that travel together — one piece of bookkeeping seen from two angles. Standard cosmology already concedes half of this; the framework says the whole of it.

**Round trip cancels; one way doesn't.** *Newly resolved — sequel to the published redshift note.*

Light falling into a gravitational well and climbing back out is counted twice, at interfaces of comparable accumulation, so the two comparisons offset. What's left is small and reversible.

Light arriving from a distant source never gets the return leg. It's counted **once**, against a receiver that has been accumulating the whole duration. So the shift is cumulative rather than cancelling.

Which relocates the difference between the two redshifts. It isn't what travelled, and it isn't a property of the source — since everything is already bound, nothing needs to arrive to establish the relation, and what costs is resolution paid at the interface. **The compounding is entirely on the receiving side.** Gravitational redshift is a difference in local condition at a moment; cosmological redshift is a difference in accumulated resolution capacity over a duration.

Same mechanism. The difference is *where the accumulation happens.*

*Also worth noting what this rules out: because the source's budget is never consulted, there should be no systematic redshift scatter with source composition at fixed distance. The framework agrees with standard cosmology here rather than diverging — which is the correct result, and a candidate discriminator that was raised and then closed.*

## Tier 3 — genuinely open

*Each has a named joint that hasn't closed. Resolved items have moved to Tier 1; see "Closed by thinking" below for the record.*

**When is overlap incompatible?** *Load-bearing.* Coarse ground reinforced by independent reuse assumes the reuses are *compatible*, and nothing says when they are not. Two structures might share ground in ways that destabilise it rather than thicken it. Until this resolves, the junction account describes the favourable case rather than the general one.

Note that this is the same question as **is compatibility one-dimensional** — the polarizer reads phase against the anchor's accumulated bindings, one local comparison, O(1), which is what a currency needs. But one filter measures one axis, and if domains can be compatible in one respect and not another, the price becomes a vector rather than a scalar.

Two independent routes to one joint, which usually means the joint is real. **The most important open item in the stack.**

**Does rotation separate support from accumulated budget?** *New — replaces the centrifugal item and is a much better-posed version of it.*

The framework's clock result says a slower clock means cheaper persistence, which means better supported. The rotation intuition says budget concentrates toward the centre, making the inside stabler.

Rotation supplies a frame-independent test that puts those in tension: clocks at larger radius run slower, which by the framework's own rule reads as the *rim* being better supported.

So either the support gradient runs outward — same radial mismatch, opposite sign — or **accumulated budget and persistence support are different quantities that rotation pulls apart.** The framework currently treats them as tightly coupled, so the second would be the first case where they separate, and would say something about what each one is.

Not writable yet. But it's a real fork with a measured fact on one side, which is more than most open items have.

**What exactly is exchanged at Lane 7.** "Possibility traded for possibility" names the shape, not the mechanism. Until it resolves, Rung 7 has nothing to build on. Biggest hole in the upper stack.
