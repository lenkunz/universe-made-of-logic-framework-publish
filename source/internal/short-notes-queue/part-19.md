## Newly prepared

**Why Deep Time Is Slow, but We Aren't** — prepared on 2026-11-24 at `shorts/why-deep-time-is-slow-but-we-arent/`.

This promotes the older Part 3 Tier 2 candidate into a standalone public Short. It owns this question:

> Why can human knowledge and practice change within a lifetime while many biological changes spread through inherited differences over generations?

Central payoff:

> **When a useful relation is preserved in a reusable form, later people can build on it instead of rebuilding the original work.**

The Short uses the already-derived ratchet in a distinct external field: genetic inheritance versus cultural transmission. It does not propose a new biological or cultural mechanism.

### Status boundary

- **Derived:** a costly new relation can seat and become reusable ground; later traversal can reuse it at lower cost; growth can accelerate because more of the earlier work is already ground for later relations.
- **Established external science:** genetic evolution involves heritable variation and population change across generations; cultural traits can spread vertically, obliquely, and horizontally, including within a generation; gene-culture processes interact.
- **Illustrative:** the tool-making example and comparison across genes, nervous systems, language, writing, institutions, and networks.
- **Not claimed:** a framework derivation of mutation, selection, neural learning, social transmission, or their measured rates; that culture is always faster than biology; that hosted logic is automatically independently seated.
