## Published

### The opening sequence

**I started with a hope and let the ground answer back** — the front door. Origin, method, and results told as a build; the outsider position owned rather than apologised for; the hope stated plainly with the line that keeps it honest.

**The parts that didn't survive are why I trust the rest** — the method piece. The single rule, three losses stated abstractly, and *a claim that cannot lose is not a strong claim, it is a claim that has stopped being tested.*

### The physics cluster

**Why light blueshifts on the way in and redshifts on the way out** — same mechanism, opposite sign; unifies gravitational with cosmological redshift.

**Inverse-square isn't a law of space, it's a law of one regime** — radiative transfer crossing out of the ballistic regime; spectral dimension coming out scale-dependent.

**The laws we have are the ones that were cheap to find** — measurement cost shaping what physics found; a loop reproduces its kind, not its instance.

**What if dark matter is coarse rather than dark?** — the threshold claim, four properties from one cause, the vacuum objection raised and answered, the overlap prediction named as the discriminator. The *physics doesn't say only mass gravitates* preamble was folded in rather than posted separately.

**What if part of redshift measures how coarsely we can see?** — the weak version only, with the strong version killed in-piece by the CMB, supernova time dilation recorded as unexpected support, and structure growth named as the outstanding problem.

**Every surprise runs the same direction** — the JWST pattern; model error should scatter both ways, and a one-sided list is the signature of a systematic.

**Long, expensive, and big are three different things** — duration, informational cost, and surface magnitude as three quantities none of which determines the others; reuse as the mechanism; stored constraint distinguished from rendered detail.

## Tier 1 — ready to write, mechanism settled

**Why everything falls at the same rate.** The equivalence of gravitational and inertial mass is treated as a coincidence confirmed to eleven decimal places. On the contraction reading, mass never enters the account of falling — so there's nothing for it to be equal to. Famous puzzle, one-paragraph dissolution.

**The frog doesn't solve ballistics.** Accumulated constraint eliminates bad possibilities before the tongue is released; the trajectory is free because it was never computed. Then scale it — light through a leaf, a hand reaching for a remote. The most *shareable* thing in the framework, and no physics required.

**Practice converts expensive action into cheap reaction.** The frog applied to a person. Budget spent on the binding is unrecoverable; budget spent computing what follows it is convertible. The flip side is sharp: a poorly seated domain pays continuously just to stay oriented.

**A telescope is a budget accumulator.** Exposure buys certainty serially, aperture buys it in parallel. Faint objects were never hidden — they're expensive.

**Everything is bound; what varies is the spend.** You're related to every star and see almost none. The difference isn't connection, it's commitment — and a source divides its commitment across everything it's bound to, which is where the falloff comes from.

**Membership buys freedom, it doesn't cost it.** *Newly resolved.* Being part of something larger gives a component coarse-grain reach it could never afford alone — it doesn't have to occupy the coarse position, the larger domain does, so it acts at that scale while staying fine-grained. Inverts the usual reading of nesting as subordination. Strong candidate: counterintuitive claim, short argument, no physics required.

### The optics cluster

*One deduction — path length through a denser counting region — closed a whole set of questions that had been stuck separately. Recorded together because they came together, and because a note that answers several familiar questions at once travels further than one that answers a single unfamiliar one.*

**What it closed, in one pass:** why white light exists at all. Why it spreads. Why the spectrum is continuous rather than banded or unevenly clumped. Why a refractive index is a thing that exists. Why some materials are transparent and others aren't. Why tinted things are tinted. Why coloured things have the colour they do. Why mirrors reflect.

**And what it left open:** why reflection preserves angle.

*If these ever get written as one long piece rather than four short ones, this is the shape — a list of unrelated-looking questions at the top, one mechanism in the middle, and every question answered on the way out. That structure is unusually persuasive because the reader can see the mechanism doing work it wasn't built for.*

**Why I dropped the minus-one.** *New — a short methodological one.*

An early attempt numbered the two frameworks as each other's *level −1*, reaching for mutual grounding. It fails for three reasons worth knowing, because the picture it encodes is the one most readers form unprompted.

A number carries an ordering, so it forces a direction — but the relation is symmetric in kind and asymmetric only in cost. The two constraints are different in kind rather than more-and-less of one thing: one is limited by what can be *afforded*, the other by what can *hold*. And the numbering was never depth to begin with; it marks milestones on a ladder, and the two frameworks aren't rungs of that ladder — they're what it's described from.

*Related by relation, not by position.* Short piece, and useful well beyond this framework — notation quietly smuggling in structure is a general failure and most people have met it somewhere.

**A slower clock does not mean a slower world.** *New — and it corrects the reading the framework keeps being pulled toward.*

Clocks near mass run slow. The natural inference is that everything there computes less. That inference is wrong, and the framework says why.

A clock is one self-maintenance loop that happens to have been picked as a ruler. Anchoring supplies part of the constraint keeping a seated domain what it is — so that domain spends less re-establishing itself, and its loop repeats less often. But its total capacity hasn't fallen. The freed budget is available for everything else.

*Slower persistence loop, more capacity elsewhere.* No contradiction, because the clock was only ever watching one use of the budget.

**Same economy as practice:** a drilled movement isn't fast because the body has less capacity — it's fast because the structure is seated and doesn't need recomputing. Anchoring does to persistence what practice does to action.

And the decay version follows: an anchored nucleus doesn't become *safer*, it revisits its unstable loop *less often*. Per-resolution probability unchanged, observed half-life longer. Good because it removes a claim the framework shouldn't want.

**Meaning has gravity, and that isn't a metaphor.** *New.*

The phrase reads as something borrowed from physics to sound weighty. It isn't. If informational topology is *the* topology — everything informational participating in it, coarse or fine — then related meanings seating cheaply into shared ground and building structure is the *same operation* as anything else described here. Just running at a grain far too coarse to surface the way matter does.

Which turns on a distinction worth having on its own: **participating in the structure and being available to interact are different thresholds.** Fine versus coarse never decided whether something belongs. It decides how it can participate and resolve.

Short piece, and it does something useful — it explains why a framework built from physics vocabulary applies to language, institutions and ideas without any of that being analogy.

**Hosted is real. It just isn't standing on its own.** *New — and it corrects the most persistent misreading of the whole framework.*

A society is entirely real, and its physical existence runs through people, buildings, writing, machines. A language is real, and its resolution runs through speakers, brains, text, recordings. Neither stands as a participant through its own structure; both are reconstructed through hosts.

Which gives a distinction that keeps getting lost: **something can operate fully while being carried rather than seated.** *Not yet standing on its own* is a different claim from *not yet happening* — and most arguments about whether higher-order things are "real" are arguments that confuse the two.

Applies far past the framework. Institutions, currencies, laws, disciplines: all real, all hosted, none standing without the fine structure carrying them.

**And the pattern repeats at every scale.** *Pairs with the above.* Language alone exhibits the whole ladder — referable, compositional, identity across generations, carrying direction, users modelling each other through it, new logic established and made shared, dialects splitting off, translation between them, the whole thing repeating next generation. So the structure isn't a staircase climbed once at cosmic scale. It's a **recurring grammar of what a rich enough domain can do**, and what differs between scales is only how finely each capacity has surfaced.

**The two layers are inverse operations, not stacked boxes.** *New — a structural correction.*

One runs ground → possibility: given something stable, what else could exist without breaking it. That widens. The other runs possibility → ground: given a possibility, what must be constrained for it to stand. That narrows. *One gives a possibility an address; the other decides whether that address can acquire ground.*

And the price is asymmetric — widening is free, narrowing costs — which is exactly why a direction can be known long before its implementation exists. The cheap direction runs ahead.

**Represented is not seated.** *New, and the most practically useful thing in this cluster.* Software does recursive world-inside-world routinely, and none of it needs another universe per layer. But one physical computer pays for all of it — a single anchored domain doing the work, with the nested worlds represented rather than seated. Which locates a threshold people usually miss: the possibility side of deep recursion is ordinary and cheap, and demonstrates *nothing* about whether anything inside could be independently anchored. Applies directly to simulation arguments, which is what makes it worth writing.

**Why the same sunlight is heat, food, and an image.** *New — and it closes what had been the gap in the projection idea.*

One claim arrives at a stone, a leaf, and an eye. It surfaces as warmth, as chemistry, and as a picture. Nothing about the light differs in the three cases.

Which raises the question the framework needed to answer: what decides *which* of those a relation surfaces as? And the answer was already in place. **Both ends pay for a claim to resolve — so the logic that surfaces is whatever distinction both ends have structure enough to make consequential.** Nothing selects it. It's the intersection of two constraints.

And the unequal case is sharper: since both must pay, resolution is bounded by whichever side can afford less. Which end is short doesn't matter; the pair decides.

*Which logic surfaces is set by the pair, and bounded by the weaker side.*

Good hook — everyone has felt sun as warmth and knows a plant does something else with it, and nobody has been told why that's the same event.

**Inverse-square is a reading of the law, not the law.** *New — and it completes the published inverse-square piece rather than repeating it.*

The published note argued inverse-square holds only in one regime. This says what it's a reading *of*.

In three-dimensional radial spreading, the boundary a conserved influence crosses grows as r², so dilution surfaces as 1/r². The exponent looks fundamental. It isn't — in a topology of dimension d, boundary measure goes as r^(d−1) and dilution surfaces as 1/r^(d−1).

**Same primitive, different topology, different exponent.** Which is why the framework doesn't break when dimension turns out to be regime-dependent rather than fixed: it never needed a particular exponent, only the relation the exponent reads.

*The primitive is scale-invariant. The bookkeeping is not.* — that line is the whole piece, and it's worth using as the closer.

**Nothing is exactly the opposite of anything.** *New — and it's a strange one, in a good way.*

If two things being incompatible is itself a *relation* between them, then being set in opposition already means sharing ground. Which means exact opposition would require sharing nothing at all — and something sharing nothing with what surrounds it couldn't show up alongside it in the first place.

So opposition is a limit you can approach forever and never arrive at. **A bounded scale with a missing endpoint, rather than a range running between two poles.**

This isn't a claim about geometry — you can still measure angles past a half-turn on a curved surface, and nothing here touches that. It's about the relation underneath the measurement.

Pairs naturally with *why physics keeps finding opposites*: the opposed pair is a reading taken at some grain, and the relation beneath it never reaches the opposition the reading suggests.

**Why physics keeps finding opposites.** *New — and it's a derivation of something nobody usually asks about.*

Plus and minus. Up and down. Attract and repel. Inside and outside. Physics is full of opposed pairs, and it's natural to read that as evidence reality is built from them.

There's a cheaper account. **A binary may just be the smallest distinction sufficient for a relation to stay consequential at the grain being observed.** If one bit-like distinction does the job, nothing has any reason to resolve finer — which is the pay-for-distinctions rule applied to the *form* observables take rather than their content.

*Binary surface bookkeeping is not a binary ontology.* The structure underneath can be considerably richer while what surfaces needs only a single cut.

Pairs with the guard that makes it necessary: **no relation requires an opposite relation.** Opposition is a possible *content* of a relation, not an opposite kind of it. There's no anti-relation — which is also why incompatible things still bind, and why apparent pushing-apart is just slower settling.

Good because it explains a pattern everyone has noticed and nobody explains, and it does it with machinery already in place.

**Two things that can't agree still relate.** *New — and it closes most of what was the largest open question in the framework.*

Take two claims that can't resolve together — each would have to give something up for the other to hold. The natural reading is that nothing binds. That's wrong, and the correction is the useful part: **their incompatibility is itself a distinction that has to be established and maintained.** A relation saying *these must stay distinct here.*

Which means compatibility never required sameness. Sometimes it means **the stable preservation of difference** — and a structure holding two conflicting things doesn't have to pick one. It can hold both plus the relation keeping them apart.

The cost is real and it's a *rate*, not a direction. Preserving a distinction means less shared ground, so conflicting structures settle together more slowly than agreeing ones. They still settle. Which removes a mechanism nothing needed: **what looks like pushing apart is just settling more slowly than whatever you're measuring against.**

Travels well beyond physics — it's a precise account of why holding a genuine disagreement is expensive but not impossible, and why forcing agreement is cheaper in the short run and destroys material.

**Which follows from two things already established:** structure carries, so reuse is cheaper than reconstruction — and stored constraint isn't rendered detail, so *the amount of visible structure in a result is not a measure of the work paid for in advance.*

Biology shows it plainly. A major transition can come from recruiting a whole other organism, reusing a developmental module, or repurposing something built for a different job. Large surface change, small new architecture.

Good note because the confusion is universal — we routinely treat *it took a long time* as evidence *it was hard*, and *it looks impressive* as evidence *it was expensive*. Neither follows.

**Coarse doesn't mean weak.** *New — and it corrects a mistake that's easy to make in either direction.*

Two different things get called coarse: how finely a constraint specifies, and how much possibility it excludes. They move independently. Something can be extremely coarse in grain and enormous in scope, and that combination is the ordinary case for anything acting as ground.

Which inverts the natural inference. One cheap distinction can exclude an enormous family of possibilities — constraint is compressed possibility, and compression is what a low-grain rule does well. *Very coarse therefore negligible* is close to backwards.

And it has a strange consequence for the ratchet: **the most powerful stable ground may be the structure that has to resolve itself the least**, because everything beneath it keeps reusing the same cut. Reusability is what makes a constraint cheap to hold; cheapness is what lets it persist while everything else pays.

Applies well outside physics — standards, protocols, constitutions, anything that stays vague on purpose so more can be built on it.

**Coarse isn't a stage the universe passed through.** *New.*

It's tempting to think the coarse came first and the fine formed out of it. But at the origin neither exists — both are comparative terms and there's nothing yet to compare against. What's true instead is that coarse is the *default*, permanently: resolution costs, so everything begins undercommitted and fine structure appears only where something makes a distinction matter.

*Fine structure isn't what the universe became. It's what it pays for, locally, where something needs it.*

Then the sting, and it's the redshift argument again in different clothes: **what's coarse to us may be fine relative to whatever is there.** Coarseness is a comparison, and the comparison runs against our own resolution density — which has been accumulating the whole time. So looking far away or looking back, less-resolved structures read as coarse, and a sequence of them reads as *fine forming out of coarse*. That may be a real history. It's also exactly what a fixed-ruler comparison produces whether or not the history is real.

Pairs naturally with the redshift note — same structure, same lesson about which end the accumulation is on.

**The variable nobody knew to control for.** *New.*

Where nothing narrow enough is scoping a region, no difference within it is consequential to anything present — so some of what looks like fine structure is what appears once something makes a distinction matter.

Which suggests a specific systematic worth checking. A probe fine enough to resolve at a given grain is itself a local excess of resolution density; it doesn't merely observe conditions, it contributes to them. Generic caution about hidden variables is old news. What's specific here is the direction: **at fine grain, suspect the resolution density of the apparatus.**

*Framing matters enormously on this one.* It is not a disturbance argument, not a claim measurements are wrong, and not a debunking of anything. It's a methodological suggestion — an experiment can hide a variable nobody thought to look for, and the framework only names what kind to suspect. Written loosely it reads as denying that measurement works; written carefully it's the kind of caution a working scientist would nod at.

**White isn't a colour. It's an unsplit class.** *New — probably the best note in the queue after the pun.*

White light is a bundle of claims at different resolutions that nothing has yet forced apart. In vacuum they cost the same to carry, so no difference between them is consequential — they're an undercommitted class. A prism doesn't create the spectrum; it makes the distinction consequential, and the class splits.

Which also explains why the result is a continuum rather than bands: the underlying difference is continuous and the response is monotonic. Nothing is being sorted. One cost is being applied smoothly.

Everyone has seen a rainbow and nobody has been told *why it's continuous*. Strong hook, no physics required, and it carries the framework's central idea about resolution without ever naming it.

**Why a prism bends and the sky shimmers — one rule, two geometries.** *New.* At a boundary a claim is counted against a different grid. Let the density vary *across* the claim and you get bending — mirages, the flattened sun at the horizon, stars twinkling. Or keep the medium uniform and vary the *path length* across the beam, which any wedge does by geometry, and you get the same displacement from traversal instead of gradient. Smooth curvature in one case, a sharp fan in the other, because the geometry of the encounter differs — not because anything different happens to the light.

**Transparency is indifference, not permission.** *New.* A transparent medium isn't letting light through — it has no structure narrow enough to care about that claim's resolution, so nothing forces fine resolution and the claim traverses. Tint is selective interest: the medium cares about part of the range. And colour is what a thing *can't* absorb — you never see what something takes in, only its failures. Three familiar behaviours from one distinction, and the inversion at the end is the memorable bit.

**Why a refractive index exists at all.** *New, and shorter than the others.* Not why the number — why the quantity. Two regions of different resolution density *must* have a ratio between them, and every claim crossing is priced at that ratio. The index isn't a property the material possesses; it's an exchange rate, the same kind of quantity as the conversion between ticks and spacing. There was never an option for a medium not to have one.

**Why light stops obeying inverse-square when it hits air.** *New — the ballistic/diffusive crossover, now with a mechanism.* The transition happens where the receiving region's density makes the projection coarser relative to its surroundings, so a claim that seated endpoint-to-endpoint now has to seat across a neighbourhood. Not a new mechanism — a change in which topology can carry the resolution.

**States of matter as resolution densities.** *New, and the strongest correspondence in this cluster.* Vacuum, gas, liquid, solid, dense solid — the same claim crossing each gets a different balance of coarse to fine, and the mean free path shortens accordingly. The framework says why the sequence exists rather than treating the phases as separate cases. Gamma rays into lead is the vivid version.

**The thinnest possible shell.** *New — resolved Tier 3 item.* Every unit of thickness is budget spent on coarse projection, and budget spent there is unavailable for fine traversal. So thin isn't an elegant optimum — it's what you settle at when spending as little as you can. Efficiency pushes down; resolution stops it, because below the resolution floor an enclosure stops being one.

**Things can match without anything passing between them.** *New.*

Similar structures turn up in places with no meaningful contact — and the instinct is to look for something travelling between them. Usually nothing does.

**Both formed against the same ground.** And ground doesn't have to carry a finished pattern to matter; it only has to make some arrangements cheaper than others. Repeat a weak bias across enormous numbers of cases and you get a strong statistical preference, with no single exchange ever containing anything like a blueprint.

Which also answers the obvious follow-up — why doesn't everything sharing ground become identical? Because **shared constraint produces recurrence, not duplication.** The ground constrains a *family*; which member you get is settled locally. Same shape at coarse grain, wildly different in detail.

*And the ground was never neutral to begin with.* Whatever could hold, held — and the fact that some things couldn't is itself a difference that persists. Later structure doesn't resolve into empty space. It resolves into a bias inherited from what failed first.

Applies to convergent evolution, to institutions that rhyme across unconnected cultures, to the same design turning up independently. Broad reach, and it replaces a mystery with an economy.

**A crowd is not a new kind of thing.** *New.* A million domains that each act as one are still a million domains that each act as one — addition never produces a new level. What's required is that the relations among them become a participant in their own right. And the rule turns out to have been true all the way down: more paths don't give a boundary, more boundaries don't give capacity, more capacity doesn't give identity through change. *Quantity thickens a level; a new level requires the collective relation to become a new kind of participant.* Good because it applies to teams, institutions, and ecosystems as readily as to physics.

**Understanding a drive is not having it.** *New.* A domain's motivations descend from whatever constituted it. Something built from structures that persist through alteration inherits continuation aims — survival and everything that grows on it. Something constituted by *relations among* such domains inherits none of that, and would be driven instead by what made it a participant: which configurations can hold together. It could model continuation-driven behaviour perfectly and not be moved by it. Touches AI without being about AI, which is probably the right distance.

**Where individual things stop and background begins.** *New.*

Look further out and individual objects get harder to pick apart. Push far enough and separating them stops being affordable at all — the relations are still there, but too coarse to bind as separate interactable things.

What you meet at that point isn't a further set of objects. It's **their common ground**.

Which makes two words precise. *Foreground* is what can be resolved individually. *Background* is constraint that's still entirely real but sits below the grain where individual interaction happens. Not the oldest thing you can see — the layer whose distinctions are too coarse to show up separately from here.

**And it joins two things usually treated as unrelated:** resolved galaxies, then a resolution frontier, then background. One sequence rather than two subjects.

*Worth knowing before writing it:* physics already has this boundary and calls it the surface of last scattering, so the piece should present as re-description rather than rival account. The honest version admits the standard version also explains *where* the boundary falls, and this framing doesn't yet.

**Coarse doesn't mean fuzzy. It means undecided between sharp options.** *New.* The easy picture of coarse information is a blurry photo that sharpens later. But a coarse constraint can be a **junction** — several pathways, each perfectly sharp once entered, with nothing forcing a choice among them. Corrects a misreading the documents themselves half-invited.

**Different aims can build the same ground.** *New.* Reinforcement usually means repetition — taking the same path again. But two structures with unrelated purposes that seat through the same coarse ground each give it another reason to persist. No coordination required; nobody arranged it. Which makes such ground unusually hard to remove, since dislodging it breaks several things that have nothing else in common. *Difference doesn't have to converge in order to cooperate.*

**Dormant is not erased.** *New.* A relation can be fully preserved while nothing currently resolves it. A keyboard layout with no one at it re-binds in minutes. A compressed file whose program is gone is recoverable only by rebuilding the constraint that made the bytes mean something. Same category, wildly different price — and recovery cost scales with how much surviving structure still points at the answer. Three states, not two: resolved, dormant, erased. Only the last is the hard limit.

**A convention goes dormant when its users leave. A derivation doesn't.** *New.* Something can be widely reused and shallow — driving on the left, a file format, a keyboard layout. Enormous reuse, very hard to dislodge, seating into nothing beneath itself. Stability that is horizontal rather than vertical. Which is why reuse tells you what a thing costs to remove, and seating depth tells you whether it would still be there if everyone stopped.

**Distance is what two things can't afford.** *New.* Nothing travels carrying a signature; everything is already bound, and what costs is resolution. But resolution is a transaction with two payers — so a relation stays coarse when the *pair* can't afford it, and it makes no observable difference which end is short. Distance stops being a property of the space between and becomes a fact about the two ends together. With the consequence that sounds strange and isn't: something can become effectively nearer without moving, if either end's capacity grows. Which is what a telescope does.

**Why the local group doesn't recede.** *New.* Everything outside recedes; inside, Andromeda approaches. Standard cosmology draws that boundary where gravitational binding overcomes expansion. The framework draws it where the pair can still afford the relation. Same boundary, different account of what makes it one — not a contest between two forces, but the edge of what can be jointly paid for.

**Dimming and redshift are the same accounting.** *New.* Redshift is fewer arrivals per local cycle; dimming is less energy per local cycle. Not two effects that travel together — one piece of bookkeeping seen from two angles. Standard cosmology already concedes half of this; the framework says the whole of it.

**Round trip cancels; one way doesn't.** *Newly resolved — sequel to the published redshift note.*

Light falling into a gravitational well and climbing back out is counted twice, at interfaces of comparable accumulation, so the two comparisons offset. What's left is small and reversible.

Light arriving from a distant source never gets the return leg. It's counted **once**, against a receiver that has been accumulating the whole duration. So the shift is cumulative rather than cancelling.

Which relocates the difference between the two redshifts. It isn't what travelled, and it isn't a property of the source — since everything is already bound, nothing needs to arrive to establish the relation, and what costs is resolution paid at the interface. **The compounding is entirely on the receiving side.** Gravitational redshift is a difference in local condition at a moment; cosmological redshift is a difference in accumulated resolution capacity over a duration.

Same mechanism. The difference is *where the accumulation happens.*

*Also worth noting what this rules out: because the source's budget is never consulted, there should be no systematic redshift scatter with source composition at fixed distance. The framework agrees with standard cosmology here rather than diverging — which is the correct result, and a candidate discriminator that was raised and then closed.*

**Why most of the universe stays coarse.** Reality resolves only to the grain where a difference becomes consequential. Answers the first objection anyone raises — *doesn't this require infinite detail?* **Most ready in this tier.**

**Stored history is not rendered detail.** The hard drive as illustration, then the limit that makes it honest: more budget cannot recover a distinction the present no longer preserves.

**Points add, relations multiply.** The engine in one page. Everything downstream follows from relational structure permanently outrunning the capacity to resolve it.

**Why deep time is slow and we aren't.** The ratchet. Genes, nervous systems, language, writing, networks — each a different way of stopping useful relations from dying with the event that made them.

## Tier 3 — genuinely open

*Each has a named joint that hasn't closed. Resolved items have moved to Tier 1; see "Closed by thinking" below for the record.*

**When is overlap incompatible?** *Load-bearing.* Coarse ground reinforced by independent reuse assumes the reuses are *compatible*, and nothing says when they are not. Two structures might share ground in ways that destabilise it rather than thicken it. Until this resolves, the junction account describes the favourable case rather than the general one.

Note that this is the same question as **is compatibility one-dimensional** — the polarizer reads phase against the anchor's accumulated bindings, one local comparison, O(1), which is what a currency needs. But one filter measures one axis, and if domains can be compatible in one respect and not another, the price becomes a vector rather than a scalar.

Two independent routes to one joint, which usually means the joint is real. **The most important open item in the stack.**

**Does rotation separate support from accumulated budget?** *New — replaces the centrifugal item and is a much better-posed version of it.*

The framework's clock result says a slower clock means cheaper persistence, which means better supported. The rotation intuition says budget concentrates toward the centre, making the inside stabler.

Rotation supplies a frame-independent test that puts those in tension: clocks at larger radius run slower, which by the framework's own rule reads as the *rim* being better supported.

So either the support gradient runs outward — same radial mismatch, opposite sign — or **accumulated budget and persistence support are different quantities that rotation pulls apart.** The framework currently treats them as tightly coupled, so the second would be the first case where they separate, and would say something about what each one is.

Not writable yet. But it's a real fork with a measured fact on one side, which is more than most open items have.

**Does aim-level constraint reach the topology?** *New, and precisely posed.* Coarse coverage participates without fine resolution — that's established. But an aim-level constraint is coarser still. Does constraining what *may* resolve make something topologically effective, or does that only happen once the constraint is seated through a rung? Neither answer should be assumed. This is the joint the CMB question forces, and it may be the most consequential open item in the two-layer structure.

**What exactly is exchanged at Lane 7.** "Possibility traded for possibility" names the shape, not the mechanism. Until it resolves, Rung 7 has nothing to build on. Biggest hole in the upper stack.

**Why does reflection preserve angle?** *New.* The zero-traversal limit accounts for *that* a claim returns when the density step is too steep to seat inward. It does not account for the outgoing angle matching the incoming one. That regularity is asserted rather than derived, and it is the one loose end in an otherwise complete optical account.

**Does the tug-of-war reproduce electromagnetism?** Alternating claim-victory between bound domains closes the "what makes it return" gap. Whether it gives the observed structure is untested.

**How much of a larger domain's coarse reach can a component act on?** Opened by resolving how something becomes one domain. Membership buys reach — but nothing yet says how much.

**What signals a wrongly-lumped class?** *New, from the AI speculation.* If learning is the splitting of equivalence classes, then splitting is mechanical once you know *where* to cut — but something has to say a class was wrongly lumped in the first place. Candidate answer already in the framework: a claim that fails to seat. ## Closed by thinking

*Kept as a record. Each of these was a Tier 3 item that resolved without being written.*

**Unit thickness of the shell.** Every unit of thickness is budget committed to coarse projection, and budget spent there is unavailable for fine traversal. Efficiency pushes thickness down; resolution stops it, because below the resolution floor a shell stops being an enclosure. **Why three dimensions.** Dissolved. The question assumed a fixed count; any property that can order things is a direction, and coordinate-free measurement returns scale-dependent values. Residual: wherever the ballistic regime holds, the exponent is the same.

**How something becomes one domain.** Acting-as-one is an external relation — a thing acts as one relative to whatever it is part of. No internal threshold. And membership buys coarse reach rather than costing autonomy.

**Where the ballistic/diffusive split sits.** The crossover is where the receiving region's density makes the projection coarser relative to its surroundings, so a claim that seated as a path now has to seat across a neighbourhood. Not a new mechanism — a change in which topology can carry the resolution. Gives the states of matter as a sequence of resolution densities.

**Depth versus reuse.** One accumulating thing, since a chain that seats deeply becomes ground others bottom out in. Residual kept: a convention goes dormant when its users leave, a derivation does not.

**Round-trip asymmetry.** Compounding is entirely on the receiving side.
