That wording confuses **expressive scope** with **lack of constraint**.

The preferred public analogy is a general logic/programming language:

- many structures may be expressible;
- not every structure is affordable or compatible from every present ground;
- once the ground, budget, and seated logic are specified, the framework constrains what can seat next;
- the strongest prospective prediction is currently the Lane/Rung process by which new capacities can become supportable and independently seated.

The actual methodological danger is allowing primitive meanings to shift after seeing an outcome.

A safe public compression is:

> **The framework may permit many outcomes. It should not permit arbitrary reasoning about why an outcome occurred.**

The original hope should be kept distinct from the deduction:

- **motivation / hope:** the universe may eventually become capable of structures it cannot presently support;
- **derived constraint:** at every present stage, only relations affordable and compatible with the current ground can seat.


# 3. Cosmology side branches not promoted to active ground

The main CMB, redshift, dark-sector, and coarse-to-fine cosmology frontiers now live in **Framework Working State** and **Where the Framework Currently Stands**. Only side branches that are not currently allowed to carry later deductions remain here.

## 3.1 Half-resolved existence

**UNSETTLED / HISTORICAL**

One speculative extension of grain-relativity was:

> A structure may resolve sufficiently relative to its own relational context while remaining too coarse to appear as an independently fine object relative to ours.

The useful part is already active elsewhere:

> **Being individually distinguishable is itself a resolved condition.**

The stronger phrase “half-resolved existence” is dangerous because it can sound as though something is half-real. That is not intended.

If this branch returns, state it only as a relation between grains:

`real constraint at its own grain`
→ `not individually resolvable at another grain`

Do not use it as an explanation merely because an observation is difficult to resolve.

## 3.2 Hubble-tension permission

**RESEARCH LEAD / WAITING FOR DEDUCTION**

The framework can permit local fine-ground measurements and CMB-background measurements to relate different grain regimes and therefore not be trivially identical.

That permission is cheap.

It does not predict any observed values and therefore carries almost no evidential weight.

Only return to this if a single coarse-to-fine growth law can independently generate more than one of the relevant measurements.

> **Two unexplained numbers are not a prediction merely because the framework permits two numbers.**

## 3.3 One growth law as the real cosmology target

**WAITING FOR DEDUCTION**

A useful target survives several older cosmology branches:

> One coarse-to-fine growth/contraction law should eventually have to account for several currently separate surfaced relations without introducing a fresh mechanism for each one.

Possible targets include:

- local versus large-scale apparent expansion;
- redshift;
- CMB temperature relation;
- transition from individually resolved foreground to common background;
- compounding scale behaviour;
- coarse and fine contraction-rate differences.

The point is not to solve these here. The point is to prevent a future version from fitting each observation independently.

If every phenomenon needs its own adjustable rule, the unification failed.

## 3.4 How could different resolving grounds be compared if c is not primitive?

**UNSETTLED / RESEARCH LEAD**

This branch is conditional on the existing redshift candidate.

The framework now avoids the question:

> "Does the underlying speed of light change?"

because it does not begin with a primitive speed-of-light quantity.

Instead:

`local resolving ground`
→ `local budget`
→ `local clock / distance / propagation bookkeeping`

All of those can co-seat.

So an ordinary local measurement can continue recovering the same `c` even if another ground has a different resolving budget.

The useful question is:

> **Can two differently seated grounds be related in one measurement without allowing every part of the comparison to co-scale together?**

The redshift candidate already proposes one possible cross-ground surface: remote and local counting standards participate in one measured relation.

But this remains incomplete because the framework has no quantitative map from ground difference to the observed redshift.

Possible future discriminators could involve:

- a dimensionless relation built from processes anchored to genuinely different grounds;
- a boundary / support transition where two grounds participate simultaneously;
- another cross-ground observable derived from the same budget mismatch rather than fitted independently.

Do not call this a varying-speed-of-light theory.

Do not use local invariance of `c` as support for the redshift candidate. That invariance is expected under co-scaling and therefore is not an independent discriminator.

# 4. Coarse-to-fine formation: questions below the active frontier

The active deductions and current star/planet/chemistry mapping now live in **Framework Working State**. This section contains only questions that are not yet safe enough to promote.

## 4.1 Nuclear support-sharing mechanism: what still needs to be earned

**UNSETTLED / RESEARCH LEAD / HIGH VALUE**

The framework now has a candidate answer to the old question "why would fine nuclear configurations become stable at all?"

Candidate:

`separate nuclear domains`
→ `environmental support is insufficient / inefficient for maintaining each independently`
→ `compatible merged seating becomes cheaper`
→ `one tighter shared domain forms`
→ `persistence cost is distributed across the new domain`

This converts the previous completely open stability question into a sharper one:

> **Can "cheaper shared persistence under a given environmental support regime" actually generate the observed nuclear stability landscape?**

### Two ways incoming relation may increase complexity

Afference or efference can add consequential relation in at least two candidate forms.

**Broader reach / capacity:** the domain's relational claim expands and the resulting scale mismatch re-seats outward.

**Tighter internal quality:** separate structures merge into a more compact higher-complexity domain.

Possible surfaced correspondences are heat/electricity for the first and nuclear fusion for the second, but those mappings remain candidate.

### The iron wording needs care

Do not write:

> "Everything heavier than iron is unstable."

That is not the observed terrain.

The useful candidate is narrower:

> **There may be an environment-relative point beyond which additional nuclear complexity stops lowering the persistence cost of the merged domain.**

The familiar iron-group fusion boundary is a possible surfaced correspondence to that optimum, not yet a derivation.

Heavier nuclei can still exist and some are long-lived or stable. Heavy-element formation also occurs through processes other than ordinary energy-producing stellar fusion.

So the framework must eventually explain **the whole stability curve**, not just why stellar fusion stops being favourable around the iron-group region.

### Future support-regime shift

A speculative consequence remains worth preserving:

> If environmental computational support changes enough, the nuclear stability attractor itself may shift.

Here **stable** means strictly non-decaying. A nucleus with any non-zero decay tendency is unstable in the framework, even if its lifetime is extremely long.

The current idea is therefore not "iron is absolutely the most stable possible nucleus." It is that the iron-group region may be the **present** attractor under the current environmental support regime.

A future or differently supported domain could, in principle, make presently unstable higher-complexity configurations fully stable.

Do not promote this until the framework has a measurable support variable and can reproduce actual isotope stability.

`rapid fission`
