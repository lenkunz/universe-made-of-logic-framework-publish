→ `large reach with comparatively low seated complexity density`
→ `light nuclei experience a support regime similar in kind to stellar fusion conditions`
→ `merged nuclear seating becomes affordable`

Keep this at conceptual level only. It is a research lead about whether the same support variable can cover stellar and explosive fusion, not an operational model.

### Decay connection

The new mechanism naturally touches the already-seated anchoring result:

`better external support`
→ `less self-maintenance required`
→ `unstable loop revisited less often`
→ `longer observed lifetime`

The attractive possibility is a single support variable governing:

- when separate nuclei prefer to merge,
- which merged configurations are self-supporting,
- and how often unstable loops must be revisited.

That would be a real unification if it survives quantitative comparison.

### External terrain to preserve

Current conventional nuclear bookkeeping should remain as the test surface.

In particular, the framework must eventually confront:

- the binding-energy / stability structure around the iron and nickel region;
- stable and long-lived nuclei heavier than iron;
- neutron-capture pathways that produce many heavy nuclei;
- reaction thresholds and environment dependence;
- measured decay chains and half-lives.

Do not force those facts into the candidate mechanism. Let them decide whether the support-sharing rule survives.

## 4.2 Does coarse-to-fine development require a single-object lineage?

**WAITING FOR DEDUCTION**

The active candidate asks whether star-like and planet-like forms can be different resolution regimes of one recurring domain architecture.

A separate question is whether that requires:

`one observed star-domain → later the same observed domain as a planet-like object`

It may not.

Other possibilities still allowed by the current deduction include:

- one coarse domain differentiating into several finer domains;
- a large domain becoming ground from which smaller independently seated domains resolve;
- nested or overlapping domain identity where “same object” stops being the useful bookkeeping level;
- catastrophic restructuring producing several descendants rather than one transformed continuation.

Do not force a simple caterpillar-style lineage before identity-through-alteration has been applied carefully at astronomical grain.

## 4.4 Where should the strongest informational contraction occur?

**UNSETTLED / WAITING FOR DEDUCTION**

A new question raised by the coarse-to-fine picture is not simply “what object has the greatest matter density?” but:

> **Where has the greatest amount of mutually consequential relational structure become seated at the grain relevant to contraction?**

This must be separated from total hosted complexity.

A living planet may host enormous high-level structure without every hosted distinction contributing identically to fine physical contraction. A compact object may have extreme physical seating without hosting the same higher-order variety.

So the framework needs a rule for how informational structure at different grains contributes to a surfaced contraction profile.

Until that exists, phrases such as “Earth is more informationally dense than the Sun” are interesting intuitions, not settled claims.

A useful new distinction:

> **Density can change surfaced scale without changing relational class, but only if the relevant relational topology is preserved.**

This may eventually separate "more relation" from "the same relation packed differently," which are currently easy to blur together.

## 4.5 Can an embedded domain know the size of its larger anchor?

**UNSETTLED / CONSEQUENCE LEAD**

If every local ruler participates in the same large anchored domain, uniform support / co-contraction supplies no local comparison from which the total extent of that domain can be inferred.

Candidate statement:

> **An embedded observer detects a larger domain through differences in support, not through the domain's absolute extent.**

Possible observables would therefore appear only where:

- anchoring changes,
- a boundary is crossed,
- a support gradient exists,
- or comparison is made with something not seated in the same larger domain.

This may be useful for thinking about very large "aura"-like domains whose total extent greatly exceeds the observer's available comparison scale.

Open question:

> What framework-native criterion marks the point where a larger domain stops acting as one?

Do not turn this into "we can never know the domain's size." A boundary or differential relation could make the size inferable.

### Compact-object scaling caution

The same ruler logic now has a cleaner internal basis.

If objects are relational forms, then:

`same relational topology + different seating density`
→ `same kind of internal form at a different surfaced scale`

A lower-density version can appear more sparse to an external ruler while preserving its internal dimensionless relational pattern.

But two cautions are essential.

First, **equal relation count is not equal topology.** The same number of points or bindings can be wired differently, preserve different distinctions, and therefore be a different domain.

Second, even matching topology at one grain does not automatically make two surfaced objects physically equivalent.

A neutron-star / Earth comparison would still have to preserve or explain:

- relation pattern and routing,
- internal support profile,
- composition / nuclear state or its framework-native replacement,
- differential contraction,
- compatibility structure,
- and external interactions.

So the useful open question is not "could a neutron star literally be Earth?"

It is:

> **Can radically different surfaced scales instantiate the same relational class when the difference is primarily seating density rather than topology?**

That is allowed by the primitive. Whether nature supplies an example is a separate empirical question.

## 4.6 Memory retrieval, suppression, and the changing grain of the present

**UNSETTLED / PHENOMENOLOGY / RESEARCH LEAD**

A personal observation suggests a sharper memory question than "why do memories disappear?"

Some childhood memories can feel forgotten during ordinary use, yet deliberate search through timeline, place, people, and neighbouring events can recover surprisingly rich event structure.

Candidate framework interpretation:

`relation was seated`
→ `ordinary route becomes expensive / lacks direction`
→ `deliberate search or cue supplies a path`
→ `already-seated relations become traversable again`

This would make at least some subjective forgetting a **retrieval-distance problem**, not an erasure problem.

### Suppression

A second candidate is active host suppression.

If a host maintains a constraint against resolving through a particular route, remembering can become a conflict between:

`direction toward the memory`

and

`constraint preventing that route from resolving`

The relation can remain preserved while access becomes unusually expensive.

Reports of mental strain or headache when trying to recover suppressed material should remain phenomenology unless independently supported. Do not derive a symptom from the framework merely because the conflict picture is attractive.
