# Evidence and Interpretation

## Why this note exists

The framework keeps a strict distinction between **data**, **interpretation**, and **evidential weight**.

A datum does not become invalid merely because one ontology has no useful place for it. Measurements from established science remain data even when the framework disputes the ontology used to interpret them. The same rule applies to recorded experience: an observation may be kept as data without first requiring another ontology to approve its relevance.

At the same time, keeping an observation is not permission to infer anything from it. The framework's fixed-primitive rule still applies. A correspondence may constrain interpretation without establishing a particular cause, sender, intention, or mechanism.

> **Keep the observation. Reopen the interpretation.**

## Evidential weight is ontology-conditioned

Evidence strength is not treated as a context-free property carried by an observation on its own.

Write the evidence profile of observation `x` under ontology `O` as:

`E_O(x) = <C, S, I, R, F^-1>`

This is a **reference profile**, not a scalar score. It is not intended to manufacture a numerical certainty.

- **C — chronology constraint.** How clearly was the relevant claim, expectation, or structure recorded before the observation?
- **S — specificity.** How narrowly does the observation correspond to the prior structure rather than matching only at a very broad grain?
- **I — independence.** How little was the observation deliberately searched for, selected, constructed, or prompted by the prior claim?
- **R — recurrence.** Does the same class of correspondence recur across separately encountered events or domains?
- **F — interpretive freedom.** How many comparably easy reinterpretations could turn the observation into a match? Lower interpretive freedom contributes stronger constraint.

The profile does not say that high values prove the framework. It records why two observations should not automatically be treated as epistemically identical.

## Evidence as constraint on interpretation

Within the framework, evidence can be stated more natively:

> **Evidence is observation becoming constraint on the available interpretations.**

An observation is stronger when, under the ontology being evaluated, it leaves fewer compatible interpretations without requiring semantic drift or a new ad hoc primitive.

This makes evidential comparison relational rather than absolute. Different ontologies can assign different weights because they permit different relations, causal structures, independence assumptions, and admissible kinds of observation.

That difference must be made explicit rather than hidden behind the word *evidence* as though its weighting were ontology-neutral.

## Correspondence does not establish intention

A personally striking or unsolicited correspondence may therefore be recorded as evidence relevant to a relational ontology when its chronology, specificity, independence, recurrence, and interpretive freedom make it consequential under that ontology.

That does **not** by itself establish:

- that a System intentionally arranged the event,
- that there was a sender,
- that the event was a tutorial or message,
- that one particular hidden mechanism produced it,
- or that the correspondence is accepted as evidence under a different ontology.

Those are additional claims and require additional constraint.

> **A correspondence may contribute evidence to an ontology without proving a particular causal story inside that ontology.**

## Relation to mainstream measurements

This methodology does not reject established measurements merely because the framework proposes a different primitive interpretation.

A measurement can remain trusted while its ontological translation remains open. Likewise, a subjective or personally encountered datum can remain recorded while its cause remains unresolved.

The framework therefore refuses two opposite shortcuts:

1. **Discarding data because the current ontology regards it as irrelevant.**
2. **Promoting data directly into a preferred explanation because the framework permits that explanation.**

The observation comes first. The interpretation remains constrained by the framework's existing ground, status labels, and fixed primitives.

## Practical record format

When a correspondence or unusual observation is preserved, record at least:

- **Observation:** what occurred without the proposed explanation folded into the description.
- **Chronology:** what was recorded before what.
- **Selection:** whether it was searched for, prompted, constructed, or encountered without deliberate selection.
- **Correspondence:** exactly what matched and at what grain.
- **Alternative readings:** other interpretations that remain compatible.
- **Evidence profile:** qualitative `C / S / I / R / F` notes under the ontology being evaluated.
- **Status:** unresolved, candidate correspondence, testable consequence, or another existing framework status.

The purpose is not to turn every coincidence into a claim. It is to keep observations available without letting either dismissal or enthusiasm decide their meaning before enough relation exists.
