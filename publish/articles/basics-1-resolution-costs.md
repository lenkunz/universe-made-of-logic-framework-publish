# Basics I — Resolution costs

*First of three foundational pieces. This is the framework's starting economy: why particularity has a cost, why finite does not mean fixed, and why reality does not need to settle every distinction at once.*

![A finite relational field seats consequential distinctions while leaving other possibilities open](../assets/images/basics-1-resolution-costs/hero.webp)
> **Image Caption:** Finite resolving ground seats only the distinctions that have become consequential.

---

## The first question

This framework begins by refusing one assumption that is easy to make without noticing:

**that every possible detail must already be settled, whether or not anything presently depends on it.**

A complete hidden specification sounds natural. Every path fixed. Every microscopic distinction already chosen. Every future measurement merely uncovering a detail that was fully present all along.

But if making a distinction is real work, that picture is expensive.

So the framework asks a different question:

> **Why should reality pay for a distinction before that distinction becomes consequential?**

That question is where *resolution* enters.

---

## The primitive is finite resolving capacity

The framework does not begin with primitive time, primitive space, or a fixed stock of energy.

It begins with **budget**: finite resolving capacity available for consequential distinctions to participate in the present relational ground.

The newest clarification is that budget is **tick-like without being a universal time clock**.

The primitive rule is not:

`everything advances through one external second`

It is closer to:

`a seated point / relation / domain`
→ `receives one basic resolving participation at the iteration of that relation`

The rule of participation can be common while the amount of relational work carried by one participation can differ enormously.

Why?

Because relation itself becomes a participant.

Once:

`A + B`
→ `A + B + R_AB`

there are no longer only two isolated participants. `R_AB` is also seated ground. Its participation is not merely another detached tick. It addresses a composition that already includes A, B, and their relation.

So the useful CPU-like distinction is:

> **The primitive cadence does not have to accelerate. What grows is how much seated relation one iteration can carry.**

A shorthand such as:

`1 + 1 → 1 + 1 + 3`

can therefore be useful if read carefully. The `3` is **not three extra universal clock ticks**. It marks the effective relational span of the composed participant `[A, B, R_AB]`.

A more explicit notation is:

`1_A + 1_B`
→ `1_A + 1_B + 1_[A,B,R_AB]`

where the third participation can resolve over already-compressed shared structure.

This is why budget can appear larger in a densely related domain without requiring a faster primitive clock.

> **Budget composes through relation.**

![Two simple participants and one composed participant share a cadence while carrying different relational work](../assets/images/basics-1-resolution-costs/composed-budget.webp)
> **Image Caption:** Equal primitive participation does not imply equal effective budget; composed relation can carry more seated work.

Finite also does **not** mean that the universe was given one total allowance and is slowly spending it down.

The current topology is itself the current computational ground.

A useful schematic is:

`present topology`
→ `present relational participation / resolving budget`
→ `persistence + affordable new relations`
→ `new present topology`

Every relation that successfully seats becomes part of that next ground and can participate in later resolution.

> **Budget is finite per present relation, extensible through relational composition, and renewed through the structure that successfully seats.**

There is no separate framework-native reservoir outside the present storing unused reality, and there is no hidden RAM in which a more complete version waits.

The present structure is the working ground.

## Equal participation, closed comparison

**Resolution Equality does not imply Resolution Sameness.**

The common participation rule does not give every named object the same private allowance. What one participation can carry depends on the relations already seated and on the question being asked.

Before two budgets are compared, the comparison must include every anchor relation that still makes a difference. If only A and B are consequential, their pair can close itself; a third anchor is not required merely because a wider environment exists. A shared contribution may disappear from the explicit notation only when removing it loses no consequential distinction.

> **Before comparing budgets, close the anchors.**

A stable relation can then participate as one composite in a later comparison, but grouping is valid only when it preserves the relations the later question needs. Anchor closure is a rule for keeping the comparison honest, not yet a universal numerical operator for arbitrary budgets.

## Why scarcity survives growth

If relation composes resolving capacity, why is there any shortage?

Because possibility still grows faster.

Add informational participants and you add resolving routes.

Seat relations among them and those relations themselves can participate, sometimes carrying many already-compressed members as one.

But the number of relations those participants can potentially enter grows faster than the participant count itself. Even simple pairings already grow as:

`n(n - 1) / 2`

and once seated relations can themselves participate in further relation, the space of possible distinction expands again.

So both sides grow:

`more seated relational composition`
→ `more effective resolving capacity per iteration`

but also:

`more seated relational composition`
→ `even more possible relation`

Which gives the central pressure:

> **Every seated relation adds a participant and compresses usable ground. Possible relation still outruns the ground that becomes available.**

Scarcity is therefore not evidence of a universe running down.

It is a consequence of a universe whose relational possibilities grow faster than its currently seated capacity, even while each generation of relational ground can do more work per primitive participation.

## What resolution means

Resolution is not the rendering of every property.

It is the moment when a distinction that could previously remain equivalent becomes consequential enough that the present can no longer treat the alternatives as the same.

Suppose several possible configurations all satisfy what the current structure requires.

At the current grain, they can remain one class:

`[a b c d]`

Then a new relation makes one difference matter:

`[a b] ≠ [c d]`

A later relation may split one of those again.

So:

> **Resolution is the splitting of previously equivalent possibilities when a distinction becomes consequential.**

This also closes the apparent gap between the Cut and resolution.

If the Cut is relation, and resolution is the seating of a consequential distinction, then a seated Cut is already resolution at that grain:

`Cut / relation seats`
→ `a distinction becomes consequential`
→ `that distinction is resolved`

The complementary side is not another process. It is what the same relation did **not** need to distinguish. Those alternatives remain equivalent and open relative to the seated ground.

> **A seated relation tells us both what has been resolved and what can still remain unresolved.**

This is why the framework treats information as accumulated constraint rather than accumulated complete description.

A new informational point may say only:

`A differs from B`

or:

`this relation must remain inside this bound`

That can be enough.

It does not have to specify every finer possibility underneath it.

## Coarse is already a real result

If a distinction does not yet need to be paid for, the framework does not call the remaining state unfinished.

It calls it **coarse**.

A coarse constraint is already seated structure.

It can sharply rule out some possibilities while leaving many finer alternatives open.

So:

`coarse constraint`
→ `many fine implementations still permitted`

and:

`new consequential distinction`
→ `finer seated claim`

> **Reality pays for distinctions, not detail.**

This is the key reason coarse must not be read as a half-rendered fine state.

The framework is not imagining an invisible finished picture behind the coarse one.

It is saying the coarse constraint may be all that currently exists at that grain of consequence.

## To persist is also to pay

Resolution is not only about making something new.

A domain also has to continue maintaining the distinctions by which it remains distinguishable.

If a structure must still count as *this* rather than its alternatives, enough of that distinction has to remain seated.

> **To persist is to keep paying for the distinctions by which you continue to be distinguishable.**

That payment need not always be carried independently by every component.

Already-seated relational ground can maintain distinctions that another domain reuses as support or reference. That is a **relational anchor**.

The anchor need not be a larger parent object, spatial, or one-directional. A can anchor B under one relation while B anchors A under another, and their contributions do not have to be equal.

That becomes important later.

For this Basic, the point is simpler:

**persistence is not free merely because nothing visibly changed.**

Maintaining a consequential distinction is itself part of the present accounting.

Change has a relational cost too:

> **Re-seating cost depends on which existing relations a change makes consequential—not simply how many relations the object contains.**

An internally rich domain may reuse most of its organization while changing one outward relation. A small-looking change can also become expensive when it makes many supporting relations consequential. The environment belongs in the closure whenever it changes that support.

This is not a numerical renaming of pressure, drag, energy, or any other physical quantity. Those mappings still have to be earned separately.

---

## Stored does not mean rendered

This also changes what it means for history to survive.

A present structure does not need to contain a fully rendered copy of everything that happened to constrain what can happen next.

It only needs enough surviving relation to preserve the distinctions that remain consequential.

So keep three things separate:

`relation preserved`
≠
`relation presently accessible`
≠
`relation presently rendered in fine detail`

A stored constraint may be dormant.

It can continue limiting later resolution without being actively reconstructed at every moment.

> **Stored history is not rendered detail.**

This is not a claim that every lost detail remains recoverable.

Quite the opposite.

---

## The hard limit

Once two previously distinguishable histories no longer leave any surviving distinction in the present ground, finer resolution cannot manufacture that distinction back into existence.

A useful limit is:

> **Recoverable detail cannot exceed the distinctions preserved by present constraint.**

If the relation still exists but is not presently reached, it may be dormant.

If the relation has been destroyed strongly enough that the former alternatives now produce the same present constraint, that distinction is erased.

Those are different cases.

`preserved but dormant`
≠
`erased`

Resolution can refine what surviving structure still constrains.

It cannot create arbitrary content from nothing.

---

## Measurement does not get a special exemption

A measuring device can bring additional resolving structure into a relation.

That can make a distinction consequential that was not consequential before.

But the device does not get to choose the answer freely.

The result must still be compatible with the constraint already present.

So:

`stored constraint + consequential query`
→ `finer seated result`

not:

`query`
→ `arbitrary detail`

> **Resolution may add specificity. It cannot add unconstrained content.**

The observer supplies resolution, not permission to invent reality.

---

## The rule underneath the rest

The entire first Basic can be compressed into one sequence:

`present ground`
→ `finite available budget`
→ `not every possible distinction can seat`
→ `only consequential distinctions are paid for`
→ `the rest remains genuinely undercommitted`
→ `newly seated structure becomes part of the next ground`

This is why the framework can keep growing without ever becoming fully resolved.

It gains capacity.

It also opens still more possible relation.

There is always more that *could* be distinguished than the present needs or can afford to distinguish now.

> **Spend only enough constraint to preserve what has to remain consequential. Leave the rest open until another relation makes it matter.**

That is the starting economy.

The next Basic asks what such an undercommitted state actually means, because **coarse** is very easy to misread.
