# Basics II — Coarse is not blurry, weak, or absolute

*“Coarse” is one of the framework's most important words—and one of the easiest to turn into something the framework does not mean.*

![A sharp coarse constraint holds several precise finer possibilities without becoming blurry](../assets/images/basics-2-coarse-is-not/hero.webp)
> **Image Caption:** Coarse can be exact and strong at its own grain while leaving finer distinctions open.

---

## One word, three mistakes

Basics I ended with a simple result:

if reality does not need to pay for a distinction yet, that distinction can remain unresolved.

The resulting structure is **coarse**.

That word invites three familiar interpretations, and all three are wrong here.

> **Coarse does not mean blurry.**
>
> **Coarse does not mean weak.**
>
> **Coarse is not absolute.**

These are not three decorative cautions.

They keep three different quantities from collapsing into one:

- how finely a structure is constrained;
- how much scope that constraint reaches;
- and the relation or grain at which we are asking whether something counts as coarse.

---

## I. Coarse does not mean blurry

The easiest picture of low resolution is a blurry photograph.

Something definite exists underneath; we simply have not recovered it sharply enough.

That can happen.

But it is not what **coarse** means in this framework.

A coarse claim can instead be **undercommitted between several permitted fine outcomes**.

Imagine a present constraint that allows:

`A`
or
`B`
or
`C`

All three may be perfectly definite possibilities once one becomes consequential.

The current structure simply has no reason to pay for the distinction among them yet.

So:

> **Coarse can mean several precise futures remain permitted, not that one future exists in a fuzzy state.**

The uncertainty belongs to the cut that has not been required.

It does not require the allowed outcomes themselves to be vague.

---

## A familiar example

Language makes the shape easy to see without treating language as proof of the ontology.

Take a word with several established uses.

The token does not need to render every possible meaning every time it appears.

Context makes some distinctions consequential and leaves others inactive.

A sentence can therefore resolve one use sharply while other uses remain available without being selected.

The useful structural picture is:

`shared constraint`
→ `several permitted resolutions`
→ `context makes one distinction consequential`
→ `one reading seats`

Nothing had to be blurry first.

The coarse part was the earlier undercommitment.

This is also why:

> **Stored constraint is not the same thing as rendered detail.**

A structure can constrain several later outcomes without already containing every one as a fully displayed internal copy.

---

## II. Coarse does not mean weak

A second mistake is to treat low resolution as low importance.

That collapses **grain** and **scope**.

They are different quantities.

**Grain** asks:

> How finely does this structure distinguish?

**Scope** asks:

> Across how much later possibility can this structure remain consequential?

A constraint can be coarse in grain and enormous in scope.

One simple distinction can rule out a very large family of outcomes.

For example:

`must remain inside this boundary`

is a low-grain rule compared with a complete specification of every internal arrangement.

But it can constrain everything that later happens inside the domain.

So:

> **Grain and scope are independent.**

Or, in the framework's stronger wording:

> **Grain belongs to the structure. Appearance belongs to the relation between structure and context.**

Low grain therefore does not imply:

- low magnitude;
- small reach;
- little leverage;
- low importance;
- primitive status;
- or lesser reality.

And fine grain does not automatically imply the opposite.

---

## Coverage is not resolution

This gives another distinction that the framework needs repeatedly:

`coverage ≠ resolution`

A relation can remain consequential across a broad range while resolving very little fine structure across that range.

Likewise, something can be extremely fine in a small local interaction without having broad consequential reach.

So when the framework says that some relation is **coarse but extended**, there is no contradiction.

“Coarse” tells you about the grain of distinction.

“Extended” tells you about reach or coverage.

They belong on different axes.

---

## III. Coarse is not absolute

The final mistake is treating coarse as a property something possesses all by itself.

But grain is always the grain **of a relation being asked**.

A structure can participate as one point in one relation while exposing a huge amount of internal detail in another.

A person can be one point in a relation between countries.

The same person contains countless consequential distinctions in a medical relation.

A planet can be one point in an orbital calculation while containing an enormous internal topology.

So “one point,” “coarse,” and “fine” are all grain-relative descriptions.

> **A point is whatever participates as one at the grain of the current relation.**

That does not mean the point has no internal structure.

It means the current relation does not need to pay for those distinctions separately.

---

## Coarse relative to what?

A useful question whenever the word appears is:

> **Which distinction is consequential here?**

If a relation only needs to know whether a domain remains inside a boundary, everything below that distinction can remain coarse relative to that relation.

If another relation needs molecular arrangement, that same domain may have to resolve much more finely.

So the framework should never translate:

`coarse`

automatically into:

`old`
`early`
`sparse`
`weak`
`low-energy`
`simple`
`unfinished`

without another argument.

Those may sometimes correlate in a particular physical mapping.

They are not synonyms in the ontology.

---

## Coarse is not a historical stage

The framework also does not say:

`the universe was coarse`
→ `then it became fine`
→ `coarse disappeared`

Coarse possibility keeps appearing whenever new ground opens possibilities that have not yet been forced into finer distinctions.

At the same time, already-seated domains can become more internally differentiated.

So coarse and fine can coexist at every stage and every scale.

> **Growth happens outward in possibility and inward in resolution at the same time.**

Fine structure is not what reality eventually becomes everywhere.

Fine structure is what gets paid for where a distinction becomes consequential.

Coarse remains the standing condition everywhere else.

---

## What coarse does not license

Because coarse leaves several finer outcomes open, it can be tempting to treat it as permission for arbitrary later detail.

It is not.

The surviving coarse constraint still limits what finer resolution is allowed to become.

If a present state preserves:

`A or B`

then later resolution may choose between A and B if a new relation makes that distinction consequential.

It does not thereby permit C.

So:

`coarse`
does not mean
`anything goes`

It means:

`several outcomes remain equivalent under the present constraint`

until another relation splits them.

> **Broad possibility does not permit arbitrary explanation.**

The present ground still matters.

---

## Candidate physical readings are a separate step

This Basic is about the framework-native meaning of coarse.

Physical applications require another argument.

Ideas involving:

- white light and spectral splitting;
- transparency and refraction;
- dark-matter-like coarse coverage;
- cosmological resolution comparisons;
- apparatus resolution density;

may use the same coarse/fine machinery as candidate correspondences.

But those applications do not define the word **coarse**, and they should not be used to smuggle an unearned physical claim into the foundation.

The foundation is narrower:

`resolution costs`
→ `some distinctions remain unpaid`
→ `the surviving constraint is real`
→ `grain and scope remain separate`
→ `coarse/fine depends on the consequential grain of the relation`

That is enough.

---

## The clean definition

Put the three corrections together:

> **Coarse is a real constraint that leaves some finer distinctions unseated because the present relation does not require them.**

It may be:

- sharply constraining rather than blurry;
- enormous in scope rather than weak;
- coarse under one relation and fine under another.

The key is not how detailed it looks.

The key is **which distinctions the present structure has actually paid to preserve.**

Basics III now asks what happens when two such points or domains actually bind.

The answer is where the arithmetic starts becoming strange.
